# Authority Surface Mapping Guide

*v1 · 2026-10-21 · Published for independent review · Not adopted by anyone*

*Guidance document. Subordinate to the Core Requirements v1. It may explain how to meet them; it may never narrow, waive, or reinterpret them (6.1). Where it differs from the Core Requirements or the Operations Manual (SCBP-03), they govern.*

---

## What this is for

Rule **7.4** requires an authority to keep a documented **surface map**: a map of every place its authority is exercised, across the six dimensions of growth in 3.B.1.

The [Operations Manual](../../01-Framework/SCBP-03-Operations-Manual.md), **Stage 1**, already covers most of the work: describe the power by what it does (Step 1.1), set its boundaries in six dimensions (Step 1.3), assess harms (Step 1.4), design stop paths (Step 1.5), and plan the ending and long obligations (Step 1.6). Read that first.

This guide adds a way to bring those pieces together into one map per power, and a list of signs that the surface is growing. It adds no new requirement. Items marked **(practice)** are recommendations.

---

## 1. One map per power

The power, not the institution, is what is authorized (3.A.1). Make a separate map for each power. Describe each power by what it does, not by its label (1.3).

---

## 2. What the map covers

**The six dimensions (3.B.1).** For each, write the limit and what is excluded:

| Dimension | Ask |
|---|---|
| Scope | Which subjects, activities, and decisions? What criteria decide who is restricted or removed? (Changing those criteria is a change of scope.) |
| Duration | What is the maximum term (7.2)? Do any effects outlast it? |
| Reach | Which people, places, and systems? How precisely can it pick out individuals? |
| Speed | How fast can it act? How fast do its effects spread? |
| Intensity | What is the strongest action it can take? Force, sanctions, enforcement level, and resources (with numeric caps, 3.B.5). |
| Opacity | What is visible, to whom, and when (3.B.9)? |

**Four more layers (practice):**
- **Dependencies (3.B.6, 3.B.7):** funders, vendors, infrastructure, data sources, other institutions it relies on or that rely on it. How would each affect stopping it, renewing it fairly, or replacing it?
- **Stop paths (3.A.7, 3.C.1):** every path, who holds it, whether the holder is independent, and what it can stop (the whole power, one practice, or one case). At least one must be held by a body the authority neither funds, appoints, nor can remove.
- **Obligations beyond the term (3.A.8):** every debt, contract, easement, exclusivity, or data arrangement that would outlast the term.
- **Reversal (3.A.9, 3.D.1, 3.D.2):** for each dimension, how effects are rolled back, how the power ends, and whether a successor could take over within one term. Any effect that could not be reversed must be blocked before it happens.

For each dimension, note which harm categories of 3.A.5 it could cause, and which threshold watches it. A dimension with a foreseeable harm and no threshold is a gap in the harm assessment. A materially incomplete assessment invalidates the authorization (3.A.5).

---

## 3. Start, now, and drift (practice)

For each dimension, record:
- the state **before** the power began;
- the state **authorized**;
- the state **now**, at each review.

The map then shows growth at a glance. Growth in any dimension without new authorization is stopped (3.B.1). Renewal cannot approve it (3.E.2).

It also helps to think ahead, over at least two terms, about where pressure for growth is likely to come from: new funding, technology that becomes hard to replace, layers of procedure, emergency framing, or combining data and powers. This is not a plan to grow. It is a list of things to watch.

---

## 4. Signs the surface is growing

- scope widened through many small amendments (3.B.1);
- duration extended by calling something an emergency (1.6);
- targeting made more precise without a new authorization;
- more dependence on one vendor, funder, or data source each term (3.B.6);
- funding concentrated in ways that affect who renews the power;
- fewer independent stop holders, or holders that depend on the authority (3.A.7);
- powers, data, or jurisdictions combined across domains (3.B.3).

## 5. Four questions for each dimension

1. Does growth here make the power harder to stop?
2. Does it make leaving harder (2.C.3)?
3. Does it put interpretation in fewer hands?
4. Does it give the authority more say over its own renewal?

A "yes" to any is growth in the authority's advantage, which rule 3.A.6 requires you to track. Persistent growth without correction bars renewal.

---

## 6. Where the map goes

The surface map is part of the authorization record (SCBP-03 Step 1.10) and is kept current as a public record (7.4). It is compared with the original grant at every renewal (3.E.2).

When you record a power from the outside, the [Registration form](../../02-Forms/01-Registration.md), Part B, asks for the same information power by power. Write what actually exists; use **UNKNOWN** where the evidence does not establish the answer, and **NONE EXISTS** only where evidence establishes that nothing exists.
