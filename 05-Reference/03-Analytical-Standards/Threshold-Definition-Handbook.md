# Threshold Definition Handbook

*v1 · 2026-10-21 · Published for independent review · Not adopted by anyone*

*Guidance document. Subordinate to the Core Requirements v1. It may explain how to meet them; it may never narrow, waive, or reinterpret them (6.1). Where it differs from the Core Requirements or the Operations Manual (SCBP-03), they govern.*

---

## What this is for

Rule **3.A.5** requires measurable harm thresholds before a power operates. The [Operations Manual](../../01-Framework/SCBP-03-Operations-Manual.md), **Step 1.4**, sets out how to assess harms and set thresholds. Read that first.

This handbook adds help with writing good thresholds: what to avoid, how to add harm up, and how to handle slow harm and non-participants. It adds no new requirement. Items marked **(practice)** are recommendations.

---

## 1. The five required parts

Every threshold must state (3.A.5):

1. its **observable condition**;
2. its **data source**;
3. its **detection method**;
4. an **independent verification path**;
5. a **time window** over which harm is added up.

The manual adds a sixth part as practice: the **stop trigger**, meaning what happens when the threshold is crossed. Crossing a threshold stops the operation at once (3.C.2).

Two more conditions apply to every threshold (3.A.5):
- **It may not depend on anything the authority controls, produces, or can delay.** That includes its own findings, counts, or classifications.
- **Harm may not be split** into pieces that each stay under the line.

Thresholds must also include the size of an unauthorized exposure of personal information that is a stop condition. If no size is set, every exposure is a stop condition (3.C.2).

---

## 2. Writing the observable condition

Write a **fact someone can check**, not a feeling or an intention.

**Avoid:**
- thresholds based on intent ("deliberate misuse");
- vague words with no measure ("excessive", "unfair", "material" without a definition);
- standards only the authority can interpret.

**Use:**
- numbers: a rate, a count, a size, a duration;
- a clearly defined yes-or-no event with written criteria;
- a composite measure only if each part is published and a reader can see when it is crossed.

> **Good:** "Wrongful denials, as found by the independent appeals body, exceed 2% of decisions in any rolling 90 days."
> **Bad:** "When denials become unfair."
> **Bad:** "Complaints the agency upholds exceed 50 a month." (The agency controls the count.)

Where a harm cannot be measured directly, the threshold must still be an observable condition (3.A.5).

---

## 3. Adding harm up

The time window is required (3.A.5). When you choose it, also decide **(practice)**:
- **how harm is added up**: per person, per place, or across the whole power;
- **how harms combine**: whether harm in one category makes another worse, for example an economic loss that also cuts off access to care;
- **what "material" means**, as a number where possible, wherever a rule or record uses the word.

Keep these definitions stable. Loosening any of them is loosening the threshold, which needs re-entry (3.A.5).

---

## 4. Early warning (practice)

A threshold is a stop line. Crossing it stops the operation. It is wise to set a lower **early-warning level** as well, so people can act before the stop.

- **Early warning:** distance to the threshold is tracked and published. The manual suggests monthly tracking, with escalation to independent review if it rises for three months (Stage 3).
- **Threshold:** crossing it stops the operation at once (3.C.2). Restarting needs re-entry.

An early-warning level never replaces or delays the stop.

---

## 5. Slow harm and structural advantage

Rule **3.A.6** requires indicators of harm that builds up slowly, and of the authority's growing advantage over those who could stop, challenge, renew, replace, or exit the power. Persistent growth in them is a harm, even if no single event crosses a threshold.

Useful indicators **(practice)**:
- how many independent stop paths exist and who holds them, compared with the start;
- how independent the renewal process is, compared with the start;
- how concentrated funding, vendors, or data sources are (3.B.6);
- how much time, cost, or expertise it takes to challenge the power (2.C.2);
- how hard it would be for a successor to take over (3.A.8, 3.B.7).

Each renewal compares them with their authorized baseline (3.A.6, 3.E.2).

---

## 6. Non-participants

People who never chose to deal with a power get **stricter limits** (3.A.11). In practice that means lower tolerances, more sensitive triggers, and more frequent checks. Harm to non-participants beyond their tolerance is a stop condition. Consent by some never authorizes harm to others.

---

## 7. Changing a threshold

- Every change needs a **recorded justification and impact analysis** (3.A.5).
- **Tightening** is allowed at any time, by the authorizing body or the authority.
- **Loosening** happens only through **re-entry** (3.A.5).
- A threshold never adjusts itself to performance or perceived need.
- Every threshold, and every change to it, is published in the threshold registry (7.4).
- A reading that reduces the occasions for stopping waits for independent review (7.3).

Raising a threshold because it keeps being crossed is loosening. It needs re-entry.

---

## 8. Common failures

- **Easy metrics.** Choosing what is easy to meet rather than what detects harm.
- **Self-controlled data.** A threshold that depends on data, findings, counts, or classifications the authority controls, produces, or can delay. Independent review of that information does not, by itself, make the threshold compliant with 3.A.5.
- **Unreadable composites.** An index so complex no one can tell when it is crossed (1.7).
- **Loosening by stealth.** Changing a definition, a category, or a counting rule so fewer events count. This is loosening (3.A.5) and interpretation abuse (4.8).
- **Reclassification.** Calling an event something else so it falls outside the threshold (4.1, 4.8).
- **Targeting the number.** Improving the measure without reducing the harm. Thresholds limit power; they are not performance targets.

Undefined or unjustified thresholds, or a materially incomplete harm assessment, invalidate an authorization (3.A.5).

---

## 9. When recording from the outside

When you record a power you do not run, record the thresholds that **actually exist**, with sources. If the evidence establishes that none is published, write **NONE EXISTS**. If the evidence does not establish the answer, write **UNKNOWN**. A failed search is not enough for NONE EXISTS. Never write a threshold the authority does not have.
