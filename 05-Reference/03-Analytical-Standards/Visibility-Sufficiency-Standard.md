# Visibility Sufficiency Standard

*v1 · 2026-10-21 · Published for independent review · Not adopted by anyone*

*Guidance document. Subordinate to the Core Requirements v1. It may explain how to meet them; it may never narrow, waive, or reinterpret them (6.1). Where it differs from the Core Requirements or the Operations Manual (SCBP-03), they govern.*

---

## What this is for

Rule **3.B.9** says every power must be knowable to the people it affects. This standard helps you judge whether a power meets that rule. It adds examples and warning signs. It adds no new requirement.

**Where the procedure is:**
- Operating a power: [Operations Manual](../../01-Framework/SCBP-03-Operations-Manual.md), Step 2.1 (the five answers), Step 2.2 (explain without being asked, 2.C.1), and Stage 3 (the public records of 7.4–7.6).
- Recording a power from the outside: the Visibility row of the [Registration form](../../02-Forms/01-Registration.md), and [SCBP-09](../../01-Framework/SCBP-09-Record-Quality-Standards.md).

---

## 1. The test

Visibility is sufficient only if an ordinary affected person can learn, **from public records**, five things (3.B.9):

1. What may this power do?
2. When does it expire?
3. What stops it?
4. Who decides whether it is renewed?
5. Has it grown?

If any answer cannot be found, or can be found only with a specialist's help, visibility is not sufficient. Complexity that hides the answers counts as concealment (3.B.9). No authority may make understanding its scope depend on specialists (1.7).

**Judge visibility separately from compliance.** Visibility asks whether people can learn the facts, not whether those facts satisfy every other requirement. For example, a public record might plainly state that a power has no expiry date. That makes the missing date visible; it does not make the power compliant. The absence of a required expiration date remains a separate failure under 3.E.1 and 7.2.

## 2. Plain language: examples

**What the power may do**

> Instead of: "Program operates under multi-factor administrative mandate."
> Publish: "This program may collect air-quality sensor readings in the river district for two years. It may not collect anything that identifies a person. It ends on 30 June 2028 unless an independent body renews it."

**Interpretation changes** (7.3)

> Instead of: "Administrative reinterpretation applied to domain reclassification."
> Publish: "We asked the independent reviewer whether the power covers two more districts. The reviewer said no. The power still covers three districts."

A reading that would widen scope takes effect only after independent review (7.3). Publishing it after the fact does not make it valid.

**Periodic public summary** (7.4, 7.5)

> "This year one power expired. One was renewed for a shorter term with a narrower scope. People gave six stop signals. Each paused the operation concerned. The checking body confirmed two, which became stops; four were found unfounded and the pauses ended."

The stop-signal record gives counts, categories, and outcomes. It never identifies the people who gave signals (3.C.6, 7.5).

## 3. Warning signs

Visibility is likely insufficient when:

- renewal decisions are announced without the evidence and findings behind them (3.A.2 requires a public record of both);
- scope changes appear without explanation, or the surface map is out of date (7.4);
- dependencies change and nobody discloses it (3.B.6);
- stop signals fall while activity grows. That needs an independent review within 30 days (3.B.8), and is a renewal warning sign (7.7);
- understanding a document needs specialist knowledge (1.7, 3.B.9);
- people affected are not told, without asking, what is being done to them and how to challenge it (2.C.1).

## 4. What may stay confidential

Sensitive details may be protected. The **existence and boundaries** of a power may not (3.B.9). In short: **security may protect method, not boundary.**

Confidentiality may never hide:
- the power's scope and its limits;
- its expiry date;
- that stop paths exist, and who holds them;
- its renewal status;
- the kinds of dependency it has.

Independent reviewers must still be able to evaluate the power. Protected access, confidential examiners, or redacted data may change the *form* of disclosure. They may not remove the ability to check compliance (5.1).

---

## 5. Accessibility (practice)

Good public outputs:
- can be reached without registration, fees, or a request;
- use plain words, with technical terms explained;
- are short enough to read in one sitting, with detail linked, not buried;
- keep earlier versions available, so people can see whether the power has grown.

---

## 6. Why it matters

People can only stop, challenge, renew, or leave a power they can see. When the boundaries and renewal status of a power are clear, people can check them instead of guessing. Hidden boundaries invite both quiet growth and suspicion.
