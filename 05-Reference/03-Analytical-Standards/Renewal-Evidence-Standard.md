# Renewal Evidence Standard

*v1 · 2026-10-21 · Published for independent review · Not adopted by anyone*

*Guidance document. Subordinate to the Core Requirements v1. It may explain how to meet them; it may never narrow, waive, or reinterpret them (6.1). Where it differs from the Core Requirements or the Operations Manual (SCBP-03), they govern.*

---

## What this is for

Renewal is a **new authorization decision**, made on current evidence. It is never automatic and never presumed (Definitions: Renewal; 3.E.2).

The [Operations Manual](../../01-Framework/SCBP-03-Operations-Manual.md), **Stage 5**, lists the evidence a renewal needs (Step 5.1), the warning signs (Step 5.2), and how the decision is made (Step 5.3). Read that first.

This standard adds help with judging whether the evidence is good enough, and what failure looks like in each area. It adds no new requirement. Items marked **(practice)** are recommendations.

---

## 1. What the rules require

Rule **3.E.2** requires evidence of:
- **a comparison** of the power's current operation with its original grant, in every dimension of 3.B.1: scope, duration, reach, speed, intensity, and opacity;
- **tested proof** that every stop path still works;
- **the indicators of slow harm and structural advantage** required by 3.A.6, compared with their baseline;
- **a drift review** by testers independent of the authority.

Rule **3.E.3** adds **necessity**: independently verifiable evidence that the power is still needed **and** is reducing its scope, dependency, impact, or permanence over time.

Three more points:
- **Renewal cannot approve growth.** Growth beyond the original grant needs a new authorization (3.E.2).
- **Drift found and not corrected bars renewal** (3.E.2).
- **A renewal without recorded findings on each requirement of 3.E.2 and 3.E.3 is void.** The power is treated as expired (3.A.2, 3.E.1).

Timing: a renewal may not be decided more than 6 months before the term ends, and review must start early enough to finish in time (7.2). No body may decide more than two renewals in a row of the same power (3.A.2).

## 2. Five areas to check (practice)

Grouping the evidence into five areas helps the authorizing body make a finding on each. For each area: what good evidence looks like, and what failure looks like.

### Scope and growth (3.B.1, 3.E.2)

**Good evidence:** a side-by-side table of original grant and current operation, dimension by dimension; every interpretation in the log (7.3), with its independent review.

**Failure:** growth with no new authorization; growth through many small changes; a new function, population, territory, or system treated as an extension rather than a new power (3.B.1); a changed definition doing what a changed grant would have done (4.8).

### Harm and thresholds (3.A.5, 3.A.11)

**Good evidence:** threshold data for the whole term from independent sources; every crossing, stop, and response; the threshold registry and its change record (7.4); harm to non-participants measured separately.

**Failure:** thresholds missing or not monitored; thresholds fed only by the authority's own data; a threshold loosened without re-entry; reporting delayed or suppressed (3.B.8).

### Stop paths (3.A.7, 3.C.1, 3.C.6)

**Good evidence:** fresh test results for every stop path; confirmation that each holder is still independent; the public stop-signal record (7.5); how long it took from a signal to a pause, and from a pause to a check **(practice)**; evidence that nobody was penalized for giving a signal.

**Failure:** fewer stop paths, or paths moved into fewer hands; new cost, delay, or paperwork for people who signal (2.C.2); retaliation (3.C.6); stop signals falling while activity grows (3.B.8, 7.7).

### Dependency and advantage (3.A.6, 3.B.6, 3.B.7)

**Good evidence:** an updated list of funders, vendors, infrastructure, and data sources; the 3.A.6 indicators compared with their baseline. Useful comparisons, start of term against now **(practice)**:
- how stop power is spread;
- how independent the renewal process is;
- how concentrated funding is;
- how hard it is to challenge the power.

**Failure:** persistent growth in advantage with no correction (this bars renewal, 3.A.6); funding or vendor concentration that weakens stop holders' independence; procedures that wear challengers down (3.C.6).

### Reversibility and ending (3.A.8, 3.A.9, 3.B.7, 3.D.1)

**Good evidence:** a tested rollback; an updated ending plan; a check that a successor could take over within one term, and that data can be moved (3.A.8).

**Failure:** the power could not in practice be ended or replaced; long contracts that block exit or force renewal (3.A.8); growth used to make up for failure (3.D.4).

### Deciding (practice)

For each area, the authorizing body records **met** or **not met**, with the evidence. "Not met" in any area means no renewal; incomplete evidence means no renewal (SCBP-03 Step 5.3). Completing the steps of a process is not evidence that the power works.

There is no exception for claimed necessity (3.E.3).

## 3. Warning signs

Each of these requires an **expanded independent review** before renewal (7.7):
- stop signals falling while activity grows;
- approvals getting faster without more evidence;
- fewer external auditors taking part;
- interpretation concentrated in fewer hands;
- more people affected who never chose to be.

---

## 4. No renewal means expiration

A power whose renewal is not completed in time **expires**. Pending review does not extend it. Restarting needs re-entry (3.E.1). Where stopping would push people beneath a Floor, only the minimum essential function continues (3.C.5).

---

## 5. When recording from the outside

Most real authorities have no renewal process of this kind. When you record one, write what actually exists: whether the power has an expiry date, who (if anyone) reviews it, and on what evidence. If there is no renewal review, write **NONE EXISTS**. For an authority that has not adopted the Core Requirements, this is a public finding, not an obligation (Force and status). See the [Registration form](../../02-Forms/01-Registration.md).
