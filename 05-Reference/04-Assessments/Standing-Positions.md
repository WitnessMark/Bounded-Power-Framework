# Standing Positions: read before assessing

*For reviewers of v1 (Core Requirements v1, 2026-10-13). An assessment aid, not a rule. Where it seems to differ from the [Core Requirements](../../01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md), they govern.*

---

## Stage

v1 is published for independent review. Nothing in it has been adopted by any institution or government.

- There are no records of real institutions in v1. It holds only fictional [worked examples](../../03-Examples/) and [BPF's own record](../../04-Registry/SCBP-REG-0001-BPF.md).
- There is no place to submit records or witness reports, and no live reporting service.
- Checks commissioned by the maintainer are not independent reviews under the Core Requirements' definition of Independent. No independent review has been done yet.

So assess the design, not an operating record. Questions about how the framework works in practice are open by definition at this stage.

---

## Deliberate positions

The following are choices, not gaps that were missed. A reviewer may still argue that a choice is wrong.

### 1. Authority is defined by its reach, and its edges are contestable

The Authority definition is broad on purpose. Indirect influence is authority when it is operationalized into compulsion (limb a) or, at its scale, equivalent to directive control (limb b). Ordinary advice, reporting, and persuasion that people can ignore at no material cost are not authority. Where the line falls in a given case is a structural determination, contestable before an independent body, and never settled by the system's own assertion (Definitions). Findings that systems are one authority, or that an output is operationalized, need tier 5 or stronger on the Causation Ladder. Limb (b) needs no tier, and is the hardest to apply consistently. That difficulty is acknowledged, not hidden.

### 2. The framework is not self-executing

Rules are written as commands, but their force depends on adoption (Force and status). For an adopting authority, they bind as written. For everyone else, each rule defines a public finding, not an obligation. Any real consequence comes from others: courts, funders, the people affected, or the institution itself. BPF enforces nothing ([SCBP-06](../../01-Framework/SCBP-06-Expressing-Findings.md)).

### 3. At the Floors, the framework is not neutral

For the Floors (Part II), Part I, and Part III sections C and D, the framework states that an authority crossing them acts illegitimately, adopted or not. It calls this "a claim, not a power." The position is stated openly so a reader can accept the structural questions and still dispute it. See [Answering the Circularity Objection](Circularity-Answer.md).

### 4. No records of real institutions in v1

The project does not publish or accept records of real institutions until the rules and forms have been reviewed. Anyone may still make one with the forms or the AI bundle. It is theirs, and publishing it is their choice and their responsibility ([TERMS](../../TERMS.md), section 5). The cost is that v1 shows no real cases. That cost is accepted.

### 5. Anyone may make a record; no one is credentialed

There is no gatekeeper who decides who may describe an institution. The risk of a poor or bad-faith record is accepted over the risk of controlling who may describe power. It is limited by structure, not by credentials: the three-answer rule, a source for every number, roles not names, facts kept apart from allegations, no silent replacement, and the institution's right to file a response beside the record ([SCBP-09](../../01-Framework/SCBP-09-Record-Quality-Standards.md)).

### 6. The checker checks form, not truth

The checker confirms that a record is well-formed and that every number has a source tag. It does not decide whether a source says what the record claims. Deciding truth would make the project a judge of the institutions it describes. A person must check the evidence ([Validator Coverage Matrix](../Validator-Coverage-Matrix.md); SCBP-09, Section IX). The bad-faith worked example passes the checker cleanly to show this limit.

### 7. BPF's own status is a self-assessment

BPF's own record finds that most of the project's powers are influence only: it publishes a proposal anyone can ignore. One finding is left open (UNKNOWN): whether the AI bundle shapes how institutions are described widely enough to count as authority. That is the project's own reading, and the Core Requirements say no authority may settle its own scope by itself. The record names when the finding would change. Rule 7.1 settles the editions question: adoption is of the edition in effect on the adoption date, a later edition does not bind an adopter merely by taking effect, and each authorization, renewal, or re-entry is decided under the edition in effect on that date. So once anyone adopts, the power to amend the rules reaches adopters at their next renewal, and would become indirect authority needing its own independent authorization (3.A.2). Reviewers are asked to check this record first.

### 8. Altered copies are made visible, not prevented

Every published file gets a fingerprint in the hash registry; a file that differs is not an official version (6.5). The registry file is created by the repository workflow on first publication; until then, no fingerprints are recorded. No edition may weaken a protection except by a stated, reviewed change (6.6). The open licences still let anyone copy and change the text. The hash registry shows which version is official; it cannot stop an altered copy from circulating.

## Open questions a reviewer may help with

- Whether limb (b), "equivalent to directive control," can be applied consistently.
- Whether the 7.1 editions rule (a later edition applies to each power at its next authorization, renewal, or re-entry) makes the power to amend the rules indirect authority from the first adoption, and if so, which independent body should authorize it.
- Who should carry out the independent check required by 6.6, since no such body exists yet.
- Which services count as essential services under 2.D.11 (for example housing and banking).
