# SCBP-08 — Witness Reports and Stop Signals

*v1 · 2026-10-13 · Published for independent review · Not adopted by anyone*

*Guidance document. Subordinate to the Core Requirements v1. Guidance may explain how to meet the Core Requirements; it may never narrow, waive, or reinterpret them (6.1).*

---

## Purpose

A **civic trigger** is any way an ordinary person can put a concern about a power on the record and make something happen because of it.

The Core Requirements already say what must happen. This document puts those rules in one place and explains how they work together:

- reporting must be easy (2.C.2);
- a stop signal pauses, then is checked (3.C.3);
- no penalty for signalling or challenging (3.C.6);
- the people's path to early review or ending (3.E.5);
- the public stop-signal record (7.5);
- warning signs at renewal (7.7).

The tool for all of it is the **Witness Report form (version 1.0.0)**: one front door for every kind of report.

This document grants no power to anyone. It does not make the framework, or anyone who uses it, a judge of any institution.

### What applies to whom

- **An adopting authority** (one that has formally accepted the Core Requirements, 7.1) is bound by everything below. A stop signal really pauses its operation.
- **A non-adopting authority** is not bound. A report about it is a dated record that its author keeps, and may choose to share or publish; once published, anyone can read and count it. v1 has no place to file reports, and nothing records a report for you (Section III). The framework cannot pause it, protect a reporter from it, or make it answer.

Be honest with yourself about that second point. Against an institution that has not adopted the Core Requirements, the protection a report offers is **visibility, not shelter**. Choose your identity level with that in mind (Section III).

## Section I — The five report types

Every report uses the same form. The report type says what you want to happen.

| Report type | Use it when… | What it can lead to (with an adopting authority) | Core rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `incident` | Something happened that you want on the record | A dated record. Counted with other reports about the same power or place | 2.C.2 |
| `stop_signal` | A defined limit was crossed, or there is a credible pathway to serious harm, and you want the operation to pause | Pause, then an independent check | 3.C.3 |
| `challenge` | The institution is acting outside its limits: beyond its scope, after its term ended, retaliating, or blocking a stop path | Independent review of that question | Parts III–V |
| `change_request` | You want a limit, safeguard, or procedure changed, even before anyone is harmed | A recorded request the institution and its authorizing body must be able to see | 2.C.2 |
| `review_petition` | You are starting or joining a request by affected people to review, narrow, or end a power early | Early review before a different independent body, once the published threshold is met | 3.E.5 |

You do not have to pick the "right" type. If you are unsure, file an `incident`. A report is never rejected for being the wrong type.

## Section II — What happens to each type

*This section describes what happens with an institution that has adopted the Core Requirements, or with a future place to send reports. In v1 no institution has adopted them, and there is no such place: a report is a record its author keeps.*

### 1. Incident

The report is recorded with its date. Reports about the same power or the same place are linked to each other, but never merged: each person's account stays as they wrote it.

Repeated reports of the same condition are **confirmation, not noise**. Their number does not dilute the response (2.C.2).

### 2. Stop signal: pause, then check (3.C.3)

Anyone positioned to observe a stop condition may give a stop signal. You need no prescribed form, no proof, and no permission.

There are two kinds of stop signal. The form asks which one (`stop_signal_basis`).

| The signal reports… | What happens |
|---|---|
| A **defined condition**: a published threshold or stop condition was crossed | The operation concerned **pauses immediately** |
| A **credible pathway** to serious harm that no defined condition covers | The checking body confirms, **within hours**, that the stated facts, if true, describe a pathway to serious harm. Then the pause starts. You may describe the facts in your own words; the checking body writes them down |

Then an **independent checking body** decides **one question only: did the trigger fact occur?**

- **No:** the pause ends. A false alarm is not a breach. It costs the person who signalled nothing, and it gives the institution no new authority.
- **Yes:** the pause becomes a stop. The power returns only through re-entry.
- **The checking body misses its time limit:** the matter passes at once to its designated substitute (3.A.3), which has the same time limit. If neither acts, a pause becomes a stop, and a pathway signal takes effect as a pause.

The check may **not** weigh how useful, costly, or popular the power is. It may not excuse a confirmed trigger.

Some things are not stop signals by themselves:
- lawful provision of a service to people who choose it;
- economic cost to a party, when the power exists to stop harm by that party.

Two related rules:
- **A crossed line needs no signal.** When a defined threshold is crossed, the operation stops at once, whether or not anyone reports it (3.C.2). A signal only makes sure someone notices.
- **Stops are narrow.** A pause or stop applies to the operation concerned, and to any other power the finding shows to be involved, but not to the institution's unrelated powers, and never only to a single agent when the cause is the operation itself (3.C.5; Force and status: Target of consequences). If stopping would push people beneath a Floor, only the minimum essential function continues (3.C.5).

SCBP-03 (Operations Manual), Stage 4, gives the step-by-step procedure for checking bodies and operators.

### 3. Challenge

A challenge says the institution is outside its limits. Examples:
- acting beyond its authorized scope (3.B.1);
- operating after its term ended, or without a valid authorization (3.E.1, 5.5);
- interpreting its own scope (Interpretation; 1.5);
- penalizing someone for a stop signal or a report (3.C.6);
- a stop path that is blocked or made harder to use (2.C.2, 5.7);
- a threshold that depends on the institution's own findings (3.A.5);
- information suppressed, distorted, or hidden (2.D.8, 3.B.8);
- unequal treatment (2.D.10);
- a Floor at stake (Part II).

The question goes to a body independent of the institution. The institution does not decide challenges about itself. Where a challenge reports a defined stop condition, file it as a stop signal as well, so that the pause-then-check process starts.

### 4. Change request

A change request asks for a limit, safeguard, or procedure to change. It does not need a harm to have happened first. It is recorded and visible to the institution and its authorizing body.

A threshold may be **tightened** at any time. Loosening one requires re-entry (3.A.5).

### 5. Review petition: the people's path (3.E.5)

People subject to a power can require that it be brought for **early review, narrowing, or ending** before an independent authorizing body **other than the one that last authorized it**, without waiting for its term to end.

- **The threshold** (how many people must join) is set in advance and published. It must be attainable by ordinary affected people. It may not depend on the institution's permission or cooperation. These requirements do not set the number; it must be set in advance and published (3.E.5).
- **Who counts:** only people subject to the power. A party with a commercial interest in the outcome may give evidence but may not trigger review.
- **Repeat petitions:** a petition on substantially the same grounds within 12 months of a decision is declined unless it adds new facts.
- **Burden:** the institution must show, on the public record, that the power should continue.
- **Outcome:** early review may narrow, shorten, or end a power. It may never lengthen its term.
- **No pause by itself.** A petition does not pause the power. Stop signals do that.

To join an existing petition, give its ID in the form (section 4c).

A successful review petition also takes away a power's minor status (3.A.4).

## Section III — How to report

### The form

Use the **Witness Report form, version 1.0.0**. Most people should start with the plain [filing guide](../00-Reading/09-Citizen-Witness-Report-Filing-Guide.md) or the [AI witness interview](../00-Reading/10-AI-Witness-Interview-Bundle.md). It explains each field in plain words. What matters most:

- **Write what you know. Leave the rest as UNKNOWN.** You do not need to be a lawyer, an expert, or a formal party (2.C.2).
- **Public sources and your own observation only.** Nothing private, leaked, or speculative.
- **Roles, not names.** "The caseworker", not a person's name.
- **Keep facts and allegations apart.** The evidence table asks you to mark each claim as an adjudicated or admitted fact, something settled without admission, your own observation, or an allegation or estimate.
- **A registration is not required.** If no registration record exists, use `record_id: NONE`. That no record exists is itself worth knowing.
- **Location** helps reports about the same place be grouped. Give it only as precisely as is safe for you, or choose `withheld`.

### Choosing how identified you are

| Level | What you give | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Identified | Name and contact | Most weight, and keeps legal options open. Can expose you to retaliation |
| Pseudonymous | A stable handle | Recommended for most people. You can be followed up, and your reports linked over time |
| Anonymous | Nothing | Most protection. Less weight, and no follow-up |

An optional **proof-of-authorship token** (`verification_hash`) lets you prove later that a report was yours without exposing yourself now. The form explains how.

### Where a report goes

- **To an adopting authority:** to its stop-signal channels and its independent checking body, which the authority must keep open and accessible (2.C.2, 2.D.8). A report must never be routed through performance goals, leadership approval, or reputational review (2.D.8).
- **About any institution:** you keep your own report. You may send it to the institution, its authorizing body, an oversight body, a court, a journalist, or anyone else you choose.

**Version 1 of the framework provides the form and the checker, but no public intake service.** A private way to send reports, and a public site to read them, are being considered. No host has been chosen and nothing is live. Until one exists, do not rely on any service to receive, store, or publish your report. Reports are **not** filed as GitHub issues or pull requests.

You can check a report before sending it with the record checker, `validate_record.py`. It checks the form's fields and values. It does not judge whether a report is true.

## Section IV — The public record of reports

### What an adopting authority must keep (7.5)

Every adopting authority keeps a **public record of stop signals and harm reports** about its powers. For each it shows:
- the category;
- the count;
- the result of each independent check;
- the final disposition.

The record **never identifies the people who give signals or reports** (3.C.6, 7.5). The authority **must not control, edit, or delete entries.**

Records relevant to a filed report or stop signal are placed on hold by an independent custodian until the matter concludes (2.D.14). The authority cannot destroy them.

### How reports are kept

*This applies to an adopting authority's record and to any future place to send reports. In v1, the person who writes a report keeps it.*

- **Nothing is overwritten.** A correction to a report is added as a new version. The original stays.
- **Linked, not merged.** Several reports about one incident are linked. Each account is kept as written.
- **Counted.** Every report counts toward review under 3.E.5 and toward the renewal warning signs (7.7), including reports that do not pause anything.

### Warning signs from the record itself

The stop-signal record is also evidence about the institution.
- **Signals falling while activity grows** requires an independent review within 30 days. If it finds suppression, the operation pauses (3.B.8). The same fact is a renewal warning sign (7.7).
- **Patterns of Floor violations** by the same power, or by powers of the same institution, are a structural finding, not a series of separate incidents (3.D.5).

## Section V — The institution's response

An **adopting authority** is expected to respond publicly to a report about it within a **published response window**. A non-adopting institution owes no response; if a report is published and it stays silent, that silence is simply a fact. The Core Requirements require every correction, review, and pause to happen within defined, short time windows (5.6), but do not set one number for responses to reports.

- **Non-response is recorded.** It is a fact in the public record, not by itself proof of wrongdoing.
- **Disagreement is recorded beside the report.** The institution may dispute a report's accuracy. The report stays; the dispute sits beside it; readers and reviewers see both.
- **A response never replaces what it answers.** An institution described in a *registration record* answers with the **Authority Response form (version 1.0.0)**, filed beside that record. See SCBP-09 (Record Quality Standards).

For a stop signal, the response does not decide anything. Only the independent check decides whether the trigger fact occurred.

## Section VI — Protection for people who report (3.C.6)

- **No penalty** of any kind — procedural, financial, reputational, or other — may attach to giving a stop signal, activating a stop, using oversight, or challenging an authority.
- **Retaliation is itself a violation** and requires narrowing of the power concerned. Report it with the same form, as a `challenge`.
- **Knowingly false statements of fact** may be addressed only through ordinary law, never through the stop path or its record.
- **No silencing by procedure.** Enforcement must apply symmetrically. Administrative procedures may not be used to exhaust or intimidate challengers.
- **Expression and petition are protected** (2.D.9). No authority may require approval before a report is published.

For a non-adopting authority these are the framework's public standard, not a protection it can enforce. Retaliation by such an institution can be reported and recorded, but the framework cannot stop it.

## Section VII — Flooding and misuse

Reports can be misused. A coordinated campaign could try to bury a checking body, or to pause a legitimate function again and again. The Core Requirements answer this **without** filtering or burdening the people who report.

**What the rules do:**
1. **No gatekeeping at the door.** Reporting needs no prescribed format, prior approval, proof, payment, or expertise (2.C.2). Any filter strict enough to keep out bad-faith reports would also keep out frightened, tired, or inexperienced people.
2. **Repeats are confirmation, not noise.** The volume of reports about the same condition does not dilute the response (2.C.2).
3. **No re-pause on facts already checked.** A signal that only repeats facts already independently checked and found unfounded does not pause the operation again unless it adds new facts. It is still recorded and still counts toward review (2.C.2, 3.C.3).
4. **One question, short time limits, a substitute ready.** The check asks only whether the trigger fact occurred. If the checking body is flooded and misses its time limit, its designated substitute acts at once. Each authorizing body must make sure its checking bodies can meet their time limits (3.A.3, 3.C.3).
5. **Falling signals are reviewed, not paused.** A drop in signals leads to an independent review within 30 days, not an automatic pause. A flood that ends cannot set off a second pause just by ending (3.B.8).
6. **Commercial interests cannot trigger the people's path.** They may give evidence, but they do not count toward a review petition's threshold (3.E.5). A party's own economic cost is not a ground for a stop signal against a power that exists to stop harm by that party (3.C.3).
7. **The rules cannot be turned into weapons.** Protections that limit authority may not be used by an authority, or by a party with a commercial stake, to obstruct the protection of others (6.3).
8. **Misuse is never a reason to weaken a stop path.** The possibility of misuse may not be used to remove, narrow, or burden a stop path (3.C.6).

**What stays visible:** counts, categories, check results, and outcomes (7.5). A pattern of repeated unfounded signals is visible in those numbers, without naming anyone.
