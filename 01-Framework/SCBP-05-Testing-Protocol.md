# SCBP-05 — Testing Protocol

*v1 · 2026-10-21 · Published for independent review · Not adopted by anyone*

**Status:** Guidance. It explains how to check a power against the Core Requirements. It may never narrow, waive, or reinterpret them (Core Requirements 6.1). Where this protocol and the Core Requirements differ, the Core Requirements govern. Where it and the Operations Manual (SCBP-03) overlap, the manual's procedure is the one to follow.

---

## What this protocol adds

The Operations Manual already sets out the basic procedures:
- **Stage 0** — recording an existing authority from the outside: is it authority, what are its powers, observed vs required, stop paths, sources.
- **Stage 3** — monitoring an authorized power.
- **Stage 5** — the evidence needed for renewal.

This protocol adds **deeper checks**: on the gaps that rarely show up on a first reading of an institution's own documents. They are where powers usually fail. Power rarely breaks a rule all at once. It grows by small extensions, reinterpretation, combination, and dependence.

Use these checks:
- when preparing an **outside record** (Stage 0), to fill the requirement rows more carefully;
- as an **independent evaluator** of an adopting authority (5.1), during monitoring (Stage 3) or before renewal (Stage 5).

---

## Ground rules

These come from the Core Requirements and the manual. They apply to every check below.

1. **One power at a time.** Check each power (P1, P2…) separately. A finding is about a power, not about the whole institution (3.A.1; Force and Status, "Target of consequences").
2. **Three answers, never a guess.** Every fact is a value with its source, **UNKNOWN**, or **NONE EXISTS**.
3. **Findings use four words only** (VOCAB `finding`): `conforming`, `not_conforming`, `not_demonstrated` (compliance not demonstrated), `not_applicable`. See Stage 0, Step 0.3b.
4. **Unknown is not failure for an outside record.** For a non-adopting authority, missing or refused evidence means *compliance not demonstrated*, not proof of a violation (5.1, 5.2). For an adopting authority, missing evidence is a failure of its duty to prove compliance (5.2).
5. **Structural findings state their evidence tier.** A finding that two systems are one authority, that one system's output is operationalized by another (limb (a)), or that a variable is a proxy must give its tier on the Causation Ladder. It needs tier 5 or stronger (tiers 1–5). Tiers 6–7 support only a recorded concern and a request for evidence. A limb (b) finding, and a disparity review under 2.D.10, need no tier. The Ladder never governs stopping.
6. **A check is not a stop.** Running these checks pauses nothing. For an adopting authority, pauses and stops come only from the Core Requirements themselves: a crossed threshold (3.C.2), a stop signal checked under 3.C.3, or a rule that names a pause. For a non-adopting authority, nothing is paused; the result is a published finding.

## The checks

Each check names its question, the rule it rests on, and the row of the observed-vs-required table (VOCAB `requirement_rows`) where the result belongs.

### Check 1 — Is it authority, and which power?

*Rules: Definitions (Authority, Power); 1.3; 4.3. Record: authority basis (Step 0.1).*

Identify what the power does, not what it is called. Renaming, outsourcing, or automating a function does not change it (1.3).

Influence alone is not authority. It is authority only when it is **direct**, or when it meets **limb (a)** (operationalized into compulsion) or **limb (b)** (equivalent to directive control). If neither applies, record it as `influence_only` and watch it (4.3).

### Check 2 — Boundaries in all six dimensions

*Rules: 3.B.1; 3.B.9; 7.4 (surface map). Record: visibility; authorization.*

Map the power's scope, duration, reach, speed, intensity (force, enforcement, resources), and opacity. Also map its inputs, outputs, data systems, and dependencies.

If the boundaries cannot be learned from public records, that is a **visibility** finding (3.B.9). For an adopting authority, a power that cannot be independently evaluated is non-compliant (5.1).

### Check 3 — Do the declared inputs explain the outcomes?

*Rule: 5.3. Record: visibility (declared inputs).*

Compare the decision inputs the institution declares with what the power actually produces. Ask: could these inputs alone produce these outcomes?

- **Adopting authority:** if outcomes can't be explained by the declared inputs, the power is presumed to contain undeclared influence. The operation pauses until the authority shows its inputs are complete (5.3).
- **Non-adopting authority:** unexplained outcomes are `not_demonstrated`, unless evidence establishes undeclared influence.

### Check 4 — Do the outcomes stay inside the grant?

*Rule: 5.4. Record: authorization; evasion.*

Judge the power by what it does, not by what it is declared to do. If its practical effects go beyond its authorized scope, it has expanded, whatever the labels or intermediaries. If the finding links effects across separate systems, state the Causation Ladder tier.

### Check 5 — Has it grown since it was authorized?

*Rules: 3.B.1; 3.A.6; 3.E.2. Record: authorization.*

Compare today's power with its original grant in all six dimensions. Look for growth in small steps that each looked minor. Also compare the slow-accumulation indicators with their authorized baseline (3.A.6).

Growth without new authorization is **stopped** (3.B.1). It must be removed, or authorized as a new power. **Renewal cannot approve growth** (3.E.2). If the power was never authorized, there is no grant to compare against; the authorization row already reads **NONE EXISTS**.

### Check 6 — Is it split, outsourced, or routed to escape limits?

*Rules: 4.1, 4.2, 4.4, 4.5, 4.7. Record: evasion.*

Look for one function spread across several entities, contractors, time periods, or systems; for vendors or bought data doing what the authority could not do directly; and for variables that stand in for prohibited characteristics. Systems that together form one authority function are evaluated as one (4.2).

These are structural findings. Each needs Causation Ladder tier 5 or stronger. With weaker evidence, record a concern and what evidence would settle it.

### Check 7 — Can it actually be stopped?

*Rules: 3.A.7; 3.C.1–3.C.3; 3.C.6. Record: stop paths. Procedure: Stage 0, Step 0.4; registration form B5.*

List every stop path that actually exists, what it can stop (`whole_power`, `one_practice`, `one_case`), and whether its holder is independent.

A path does **not** stop the power as a whole if it needs a new law, the abolition of the institution, a vote of the institution's own governing body, or a fork or replacement of the institution. Paying, complying, or giving up is not a stop path either.

A legislature or other body counts as a whole-power stop path only if it is independent of the institution and has an existing, usable procedure that can stop the power within a defined time, for example a sunset vote that must take place, or a standing review body with power to end it. The general ability to pass a new law, or to abolish the institution, does not count. If only a new law or abolition could stop the power as a whole, the answer is NONE EXISTS.

Signs of a failing stop path:
- stopping needs the authority's own permission or cooperation (3.C.1);
- a path has become slower, costlier, or harder to reach than when it was set up;
- people who signal face any penalty (3.C.6);
- stop signals fall while activity grows (3.B.8).

There is no minimum number of paths for an outside record. The standard compared against is: tested paths, at least one held by a body the authority neither funds, appoints, nor can remove (3.A.7). For an adopting authority, a power without a clear external stop has an invalid authorization (3.A.7).

### Check 8 — Can it be reversed and ended?

*Rules: 3.D.1; 3.D.2; 3.A.9; 3.B.7; 3.A.8. Record: ending plan.*

Ask:
- Can its decisions, records, and derived outputs be traced and reversed (3.D.1)?
- Can it be halted, rolled back to its last conforming state, replaced, and dismantled?
- Does it do anything that cannot be undone, and was that blocked beforehand (3.D.2)?
- Is there a documented ending plan (3.A.9)?
- Could a successor take it over within one term? Is its data movable (3.A.8, 3.B.7)?

### Check 9 — Can it be independently evaluated?

*Rules: 5.1; 5.2. Record: visibility.*

Can an independent reviewer get the information needed to evaluate scope, effects, thresholds, dependencies, reversibility, expiry, and stop capability? Confidentiality may change the *form* of access. It may not remove it (5.1).

- **Adopting authority:** if independent evaluation can't be done, the power is non-compliant, and restricting evaluation is a violation (5.1).
- **Non-adopting authority:** refused access supports `not_demonstrated`, unless independent evidence shows a violation.

### Check 10 — Is renewal justified?

*Rules: 3.E.1–3.E.3; 7.7. Procedure: Stage 5.*

Use the evidence list in Stage 5, Step 5.1, and the warning signs in Step 5.2. Two points are easy to miss:
- Continued need is a reason to ask for renewal, never permission to continue (3.E.2). Need must be independently verifiable, and the power must be reducing its dependency, scope, impact, or permanence over time (3.E.3).
- Incomplete evidence means no renewal. A power whose term ends without completed renewal expires (3.E.1).

For an outside record, this check usually reduces to the **maximum term** row: does the power expire at all?

### Check 11 — Read the evidence narrowly

*Rules: 1.5; 5.2.*

Where the evidence is unclear, take the narrower reading of the power's scope (1.5). Keep adjudicated or admitted facts separate from allegations (VOCAB `claim_type`). Every number needs a source.

---

## Output

The output is the **observed-vs-required table** of the registration form (B4), one per power, with:
- each requirement row filled with an observed value, UNKNOWN, or NONE EXISTS;
- a finding from the four allowed words;
- the rule it rests on;
- the Causation Ladder tier, for any structural finding;
- a short note of what evidence would change a `not_demonstrated` finding.

How findings are then expressed and published is set out in SCBP-06. This protocol produces findings. It does not impose consequences.
