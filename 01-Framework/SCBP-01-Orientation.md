# Bounded Power — Orientation

*v1 · 2026-10-13 · Published for independent review · Not adopted by anyone*

*A way to keep power small enough to stop.*

---

This is for the people living inside systems they didn't build — anyone who has watched things get bigger, more tangled, and harder to deal with, and had the feeling that something is off even while everything technically works.

Systems don't become a problem overnight. They become a problem when they pass the point where they can still be changed. This framework is about not letting it get that far.

---

## The problem

Systems rarely break all at once. They just keep going. Something is added. Nothing is taken away. A temporary measure quietly becomes permanent. A definition stretches by an inch. None of it looks like a problem on the day it happens — the clinic adds one more form, the agency keeps one emergency power past the emergency, the platform changes one default.

No single step looks like the problem. The pattern is the problem.

This doesn't take bad people or hidden plans. It is simply how systems are built: once something is in place, it tends to stay in place. Continuation is the default. Stopping takes effort — and the longer a thing runs, the more comes to depend on it, and the harder stopping becomes. So most of the time, nothing stops. You were never asked to agree to any of it; by the time you notice the system, it has been running for years.

---

## The inversion

Almost every system rests on one assumption, so common no one examines it: **authority continues unless someone stops it.**

That puts the entire burden on you. Notice the problem. Push back. Somehow reverse what has already piled up — against a system that grows harder to move every year. It is a burden almost no one can carry, which is exactly why things just continue.

This framework turns that assumption around.

**Authority does not continue by default. It must justify its continuation. If it cannot, it ends.**

The shift is small. The effect is not. Nothing coasts on having always been there. Power stays in its lane instead of widening into the next one. Nothing becomes permanent unless someone makes the case, out loud, to keep it. The pressure moves off the people and onto the power.

This is not a political position. Left or right, what you want an authority to *do* is a separate fight. This is about something underneath that fight: how authority behaves once it exists, no matter who holds it.

---

## What safety actually means

Safety is not a feeling. It is a structural condition.

A system is safe only if it can still be controlled — stopped, examined, corrected, replaced — without bringing everything down around it. Miss any one of those and the system is unsafe, however well-meaning, however well-run, however long it has operated without incident. A system that cannot be stopped is unsafe by definition, even if it has never once caused harm.

And no system gets to hide behind its own complexity. Authority is the power to decide; structure is everything that carries the decision out — staff, contractors, software, paperwork. Spreading the work across a thousand hands does not spread the responsibility. **Work can be distributed. Responsibility cannot.** If a system causes harm, the authority that directs it answers for it — however many layers sit in between.

## What you can actually do

Most people have almost no leverage over the systems that run their lives. You can vote. You can file a complaint into a box the institution itself controls, handled at its own discretion, leaving no public trace. After that, the tools run thin.

This framework adds two tools.

**A registration record.** It sets out what an institution's powers are, one power at a time, and for each: who authorized it, when that permission expires, what would stop it, and how to start a stop. Every answer is either a value with its source, **UNKNOWN**, or **NONE EXISTS** — never a guess. Anyone can make one, without the institution's cooperation, without a lawyer, without anyone's permission. An AI assistant can help, using the registration bundle. The record is yours: you keep it, and publishing it is your choice and your responsibility. It changes only when you issue a new version that quotes what changed. If the institution disagrees, it files a response beside your record; it cannot replace it. (It is a record of how authority behaves, not of private individuals.)

**A witness report.** It is a written, dated report that something is wrong: an incident, a stop signal, a challenge, or a request for review. You don't need proof, a special format, or anyone's permission. If the institution has adopted the framework's rules, a stop signal can pause the operation while one simple question is checked: did the thing it reports actually happen? If it hasn't, the report still leaves a dated mark that a specific concern was raised about a specific institution on a specific day. Where witness reports will be filed and published is still being decided; v1 provides the form, not a filing service.

Neither tool is a promise that you win. Both are influence, not authority — they compel no one and decide nothing. Their strength is that they are written down, sourced, and dated, so a concern is much harder to ignore quietly.

## Why the rules come next

Everything above is the idea. On its own, an idea constrains nothing.

What makes it real is the **[Core Requirements (SCBP-04)](SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md)** — the rules, and the only binding part of the framework. Everything else, this page included, explains and applies; where anything here conflicts with the Core Requirements, the Core Requirements govern. One principle runs through all of it: where two readings compete, the one that gives authority less wins. The framework never expands power. It only ever constrains it.

That is where "authority must justify its continuation, or end" stops being a slogan and gains teeth. For an authority that adopts the Core Requirements, they bind its conduct. For one that does not, they are the standard a public record measures it against. No authority has adopted them yet: v1 is a draft for independent review. And in every case, what the framework offers is the record and the standard — not a promise that a power it cannot reach will stop on its own.
