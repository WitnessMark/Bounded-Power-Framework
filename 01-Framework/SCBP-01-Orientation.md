# SCBP-01 — Orientation

*v1 · 2026-11-05 · Published for independent review · Not adopted by anyone*

*A way to keep power small enough to stop.*

Systems seldom become a problem overnight. They become a problem when they pass the point at which they can still be changed. The framework is concerned with keeping them on the near side of that point.

## The problem

Systems seldom fail all at once. They continue. Something is added; nothing is removed. A temporary measure becomes permanent. A definition stretches. None of it looks like a problem on the day it happens: a clinic adds one more form, an agency keeps an emergency power after the emergency, a platform changes one default. No single step is the problem. The pattern is.

This requires neither bad people nor hidden plans. It is how systems tend to work: once something is in place, it tends to stay in place. Continuation is the default, and stopping takes effort. The longer a thing runs, the more comes to depend on it, and the harder it becomes to stop. Most of the time, nothing stops. The people affected were never asked to agree to any of it, and by the time they notice the system, it has been running for years.

## The inversion

Most systems rest on an assumption so common it is rarely examined: *authority continues unless someone stops it.*

That places the burden on the people affected. They must notice the problem, object, and reverse what has accumulated, against a system that becomes harder to move every year. Few can carry that burden, which is why things continue.

The framework reverses the assumption: **authority does not continue by default. It must justify its continuation, and what cannot be justified ends.**

The inversion applies to each power separately, not to institutions. A power that is still justified is renewed and continues without interruption. A power that cannot be justified stops, and the institution carries on without it; where stopping would push people beneath a Floor, the power's minimum essential function continues (3.C.5). [SCBP-02](SCBP-02-Foundational-Theory.md#what-the-inversion-does-not-mean) follows a worked case through renewal.

The change is small; its effect is not. Nothing continues merely because it has always been there. A power stays within its bounds instead of widening into the next area. Nothing becomes permanent unless someone makes the case, in public, for keeping it. The pressure moves from the people to the power.

This is not a political position. What an authority should do is a separate question, on which people disagree. The framework concerns something beneath that question: how authority behaves once it exists, whoever holds it.

## What safety means

Safety is not a feeling. It is a structural condition.

A system is safe only if it can still be stopped, examined, corrected or replaced without bringing everything down with it. A system that lacks any one of these is unsafe, however well-intentioned or well-run it is, and however long it has operated without incident. A system that cannot be stopped is unsafe by this definition, even if it has never caused harm.

No system is excused by its own complexity. Authority is the power to decide; structure is everything that carries the decision out: staff, contractors, software, paperwork. Spreading the work across a thousand hands does not spread the responsibility. *Work can be distributed. Responsibility cannot.* When a system causes harm, the authority that directs it answers for it, however many layers lie between.

## What the framework provides

Most people have little leverage over the systems that shape their lives. There is the vote, and there is the complaint, made to a channel the institution itself controls, handled at its discretion, often leaving no public trace. The framework adds two instruments.

**A registration record** sets out an institution's powers, one at a time, and for each: who authorized it, when that permission expires, what can stop it, and how a stop is started. Every answer is a value with its source, UNKNOWN, or NONE EXISTS, never a guess. Anyone may make one, without the institution's cooperation, a lawyer or anyone's permission, and an AI assistant can help. The record belongs to its author, who decides whether to publish it. It changes only through a new version that quotes what changed. An institution that disagrees files a response beside the record; it cannot replace it. A record concerns how authority behaves, not private individuals.

**A witness report** is a dated, written account that something is wrong: an incident, a stop signal, a challenge, or a request for review. It needs no proof, no special format and no permission. With an institution that has adopted the framework's rules, a stop signal pauses the operation while one question is checked: did the reported thing happen? With any other institution, the report is a dated record that a specific concern was raised about a specific institution on a specific day. v1 provides the form, not a place to file it.

Neither instrument guarantees an outcome. Both are influence, not authority: they compel no one and decide nothing. Their strength is that they are written, sourced and dated, which makes a concern harder to ignore quietly.

## Why the rules come next

An idea constrains nothing on its own. What gives this one force is the **[Core Requirements (SCBP-04)](SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md)**, the only binding part of the framework. Every other document, this one included, explains or applies them, and where anything conflicts with them, they govern. One principle runs through all of them: where two readings compete, the one that gives authority less prevails. The framework never expands power. It only constrains it.

For an authority that adopts the Core Requirements, they bind its conduct. For one that does not, they are the standard a public record measures it against. No authority has adopted them; v1 is a draft for independent review. In every case, what the framework offers is the record and the standard, not a promise that a power it cannot reach will stop on its own.
