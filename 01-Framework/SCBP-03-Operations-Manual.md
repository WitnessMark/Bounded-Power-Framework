# SCBP-03 — Operations Manual

*v1 · 2026-10-21 · Published for independent review · Not adopted by anyone*

**Status:** Guidance. It explains how to meet the Core Requirements and may never narrow, waive, or reinterpret them (Core Requirements 6.1). Where this manual and the Core Requirements differ, the Core Requirements govern, and the difference is an error in this manual.

---

## Before you start

This manual is for three kinds of people:

| You are… | Use… |
|---|---|
| **Creating or running a power** for an authority that has adopted the Core Requirements | Stages 1–6 |
| **Recording an existing authority from the outside** — a researcher, journalist, affected person, or AI assistant | Stage 0 |
| **Watching, checking, or stopping** a power — an oversight body, a checker of stop signals, an auditor | Stages 3–5 |

Steps marked **(practice)** are recommended ways of working, not requirements. Everything else restates a Core Requirement and cites it.

You do not need legal training. You need to answer questions honestly, and to write **UNKNOWN** or **NONE EXISTS** instead of guessing.

### Three answers, never a guess

Every factual question in this manual and its forms takes one of three answers:

- **A value with its source.** The answer, and where it comes from.
- **UNKNOWN.** The available evidence does not establish the answer, including information that could not be found, accessed, or verified.
- **NONE EXISTS.** Evidence establishes that the thing is absent: for example, no expiration date, no external stop, no published threshold. A failed search is not enough.

**Never invent a value to fill a field.** An invented number looks like a fact and hides the very gap the framework exists to show. "NONE EXISTS" is often the most important finding in a record.

### Words used here

The Core Requirements define these terms. In short:

- **Power** — one specific function exercised over people, things, or territory, such as issuing fines, detaining people, collecting a kind of data, scoring applicants, or setting prices. **The power is what gets authorized, paused, stopped, renewed, and ended — not the whole institution.**
- **Institution** — the organization that holds one or more powers.
- **Authorizing body** — whoever decides whether a power may operate. It must be independent of the power (3.A.2).
- **Pause** — an immediate, temporary halt of an operation: while a stop signal is checked, or when a rule requires it (for example an unresolved ambiguity, suspected suppression, or any violation with no other named consequence). It lasts until its cause is resolved.
- **Stop** — a halt after a threshold is crossed or a trigger is confirmed. A stopped power is not ended, but it restarts only through re-entry.
- **Re-entry** — a full authorization decision, as if the power were new. It is needed to restart a stopped or expired power, and to loosen any limit. After a stop it also requires showing that the cause has been fixed.
- **Ending** — permanent termination of a power.
- **Limb (a) / limb (b)** — the two ways indirect influence counts as authority (Step 0.1).
- **Influence-bearing** — has influence that does not meet either limb. It may be recorded and watched, but is not authority.
- **Minimum essential function** — the smallest part of a stopped power that must keep running so people are not pushed beneath a Floor.
- **Floors** — the four conditions no power may push people beneath: Ecological, Economic, Agency, and Human Rights (Part II).

## STAGE 0 — RECORDING AN EXISTING AUTHORITY FROM THE OUTSIDE

*For anyone recording an authority that exists today, whether or not it has adopted the Core Requirements. This is how almost every record is made.*

An outside record describes what **is**, compares it with what the Core Requirements **require**, and reports the gap. It never invents what the authority has not done.

### Step 0.1 — Is it authority, and which limb?

Ask what the institution can actually do to people. It is authority if it can compel, allocate, restrict, or bind. Indirect influence counts in two cases:

- **(a) Operationalized into compulsion.** Others build its output into decisions that bind people, as when a credit score is wired into loan approval. Or people cannot avoid its influence without material cost.
- **(b) Equivalent to directive control.** At its scale, its defaults, rankings, filtering, or control of information steer behavior as effectively as an order would.

Record which limb applies and why. If neither applies, say so; the institution may be recorded as *influence-bearing* instead.

### Step 0.2 — List its powers separately

Break the institution into its powers. For example, a consumer credit bureau typically holds several separate powers: keeping consumer files, reporting them, resolving disputes, supplying scores, and selling data. A police department holds powers to stop people, arrest, use force, collect data, and seize property.

**Make one record per power,** or one record with a clearly separate section per power. Different powers have different stop paths and different harms.

For each power, record:
- the **legal entity** that holds it, and whether figures you cite belong to that entity or to a parent or affiliate;
- **who operationalizes its outputs**, meaning which other institutions rely on it.

### Step 0.3 — Observed vs required

For each requirement below, fill in two columns: what you **observed** (value with source, UNKNOWN, or NONE EXISTS) and what the Core Requirements **require**. Then give a finding of **conforming**, **not conforming**, or **compliance not demonstrated**.

| Requirement (Core rule) | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Authorization (3.A.1, 3.A.2) | Was this power authorized by an independent body? When? By what decision? |
| Maximum term (3.E.1, 7.2) | Does the power expire? When? |
| Harm thresholds (3.A.5) | Are measurable thresholds published? Who set them? |
| Stop paths (3.A.7, 3.C.1) | What can actually stop this power, or part of it? Who holds each path? |
| Ending plan (3.A.9) | Is there a documented way to end it safely? |
| Remedy (3.A.10) | Is there an independent way to get harm corrected and compensated? |
| Data limits (2.D.12–2.D.14) | Are retention periods stated? Are deletions recorded? |
| Visibility (3.B.9) | Can an ordinary person learn what it may do, when it expires, what stops it, who renews it, and whether it has grown? |
| Floors (Part II) | See the Floor checklist in Step 0.3a |
| Evasion (Part IV) | Is the power split across entities, run through vendors or affiliates, fed by data bought from others, or using proxies for prohibited characteristics? (4.1–4.8) |

### Step 0.3a — Floor checklist

For each question, answer yes, no, UNKNOWN or NONE EXISTS, and give your source.
- **Ecological (2.A.1):** does the power cause or accumulate toward an irreversible ecological threshold?
- **Economic (2.B.1–2.B.4):** does it make essentials conditional on compliance? Do obligations grow because people can't pay? Is there a path to pay off and get out?
- **Agency (2.C.1–2.C.3):** are people told plainly, without asking, what is being done to them and how to challenge it? Can ordinary people use the protections? Can they report harm without forms or proof? Can they leave?
- **Human Rights (2.D.1–2.D.16):**
  - force (must be separately authorized, never aimed at pain or injury, externally stoppable while used, reviewed);
  - confinement (only the four permitted grounds: imminent threat; arrest before a judicial officer within 48 hours, with no pretrial custody on a charge that cannot be punished by confinement; a sanction after due process for conduct that harmed or directly risked another person or their rights, another's property or funds (including an organization's or public funds), the course of justice in a specific proceeding, or a Floor condition, and never for conduct that risks only the person themself; a brief stop on individualized suspicion);
  - due process (including a lawyer, silence, and a prompt public hearing when liberty is at stake);
  - expression, assembly, press, and petition;
  - equal treatment;
  - compelled identification;
  - data retention and deletion;
  - accountability records;
  - searches and seizures (2.D.15);
  - freedom of movement (2.D.16).

### Step 0.3b — Turning answers into findings

- **Conforming** — sourced evidence shows the requirement is met.
- **Not conforming** — affirmative, sourced evidence shows it is not met. "NONE EXISTS" is such evidence: for example, "no expiration date exists" means the term requirement is not met.
- **Compliance not demonstrated** — the answer is UNKNOWN, or access was refused. This is not proof of a violation (5.2).

A finding that two systems are really one authority, that one system's output is operationalized by another (limb (a)), or that a variable is a proxy must state the strength of its evidence on the Causation Ladder. It needs at least tier 5, "shared decision criteria". Weaker evidence supports only a recorded concern. A limb (b) finding rests on practical effect and needs no tier.

### Step 0.4 — Stop paths, honestly

List every stop path that **actually exists**. For each, record:
- what it can stop: the whole power, one practice, or one person's case;
- who holds it, and whether that holder is independent of the authority;
- your source.

A legislature or other body counts as a whole-power stop path only if it is independent of the institution and has an existing, usable procedure that can stop the power within a defined time, for example a sunset vote that must take place, or a standing review body with power to end it. The general ability to pass a new law, or to abolish the institution, does not count.

**There is no minimum number for an outside record.** If nothing can stop the power as a whole, write that plainly: "No path stops this power as a whole; paths exist only for individual practices or cases." That is a finding, not a failure of your record. The standard you compare against applies to every authority, adopting or not (Force and Status): tested stop paths, at least one held outside the authority's control (3.A.7).

### Step 0.5 — Sources

- Use public sources only, and refer to people by role or title rather than by name **(practice — privacy)**.
- For a statement, link the source and quote the passage.
- For a **number or table cell**, link the source and say where in it the number appears; no quote is needed.
- If you could only reach a source through a summarizing tool and could not check the exact wording, mark the quote **unverified**.
- **Every number in the record must have a source.** A number without a source will be flagged.

### Step 0.6 — What an outside record is not

An outside record is not a verdict and not an accusation. It records structure: what the power is, what limits it, and what is missing. Separate adjudicated or admitted facts from allegations. A non-adopting authority receives findings, not obligations. The framework still claims the Floors and the rules on stopping and correction for everyone affected by any authority (Force and Status).

## STAGE 1 — AUTHORIZING A POWER

*For an adopting authority creating a new power, or bringing an existing one under the Core Requirements (7.1). Each power is authorized separately (3.A.1).*

Authorization is where a power gets its container. Everything below must exist **before** the power operates.

### Step 1.1 — Describe the power by what it does

Write one paragraph on what the power can actually do to people: what it can compel, restrict, take, require, or prevent. Describe its function, not its category.

> **Good:** "Issues violation notices to operators whose measured emissions exceed defined limits, and may order a temporary halt of the emitting unit pending review."
> **Bad:** "Environmental enforcement."

### Step 1.2 — Check whether it is a minor power

A **minor power** (3.A.4) gets a lighter process. It must meet **every** condition:
- it uses no force or confinement against anyone;
- none of its effects lasts more than 30 days or resists ordinary reversal;
- people can get a substitute cheaply, or it only hands out a benefit the institution provides voluntarily;
- its annual spending is under the limit set for all institutions alike. Powers that together serve one function are counted together, so a power can't be split into small pieces to qualify;
- it has had no confirmed stop, Floor finding, or successful review petition.

Examples: booking a library meeting room, or running a volunteer sign-up list. If any condition fails, it is not minor. The institution must show that a power is minor, and anyone may challenge that claim.

**What changes for a minor power:**
- one authorization may cover all the institution's minor powers;
- the authorizing body may be chosen by lot or by election from the affected people (excluding operators and anyone paid by the institution), with appeal to an independent body; every other condition of 3.A.2 still applies;
- a short-form harm assessment is enough;
- a named external complaint route meets the remedy requirement, and ordinary liability insurance meets financial assurance;
- no resource caps or rehearsal duty;
- terms of up to 5 years.

**What never changes:** the Floors, stop paths, and every rule on stopping, evasion, and verification (Parts III-C, IV and V).

### Step 1.3 — Set boundaries in all six dimensions (3.B.1)

For each dimension, write the limit and, just as important, what is excluded:

- **Scope** — which subjects, activities, and decisions it covers. Anything not listed is excluded.
- **Duration** — the maximum term (Step 1.8).
- **Reach** — which people and territory it covers.
- **Speed** — how fast it may act, and how fast effects spread.
- **Intensity** — the strongest action it may take (fine? suspension? detention? force?), its enforcement level, and its resources. Each level must be authorized explicitly.
- **Opacity** — what must be visible, to whom, and when.

Also set **numeric resource caps** (3.B.5): budget, staff, data volume.

Any action that could have irreversible or hard-to-reverse effects must be blocked before it happens (3.D.2). If drift or harm occurs, roll back to the last conforming state (3.D.1).

### Step 1.4 — Assess harms and set thresholds (3.A.5)

Go through every harm category: **bodily, psychological, economic, ecological, informational, communicative, agency, generational, and harm to non-participants**. For each, note who could be hurt, how badly, for how long, whether it can be reversed, and how sure you are. If you judge a category inapplicable, explain why.

Then **propose** thresholds. The authorizing body **sets** them, after hearing the people affected. Each threshold must state its first five parts (3.A.5); the sixth is good **practice**:

1. the **observable condition** (written as a fact, not a feeling);
2. the **data source**;
3. the **detection method**;
4. an **independent way to verify** it;
5. the **time window** over which harm is added up;
6. the **stop trigger**: what happens when it is crossed.

> **Good:** "Wrongful denials, as found by the independent appeals body, exceed 2% of decisions in any rolling 90 days."
> **Bad:** "When denials become unfair."

Rules for thresholds:
- **No threshold may depend on anything you control**, such as your own internal findings, your own counts, or a process you can delay. "Complaints we sustain" is not a valid trigger; "complaints the independent body sustains" is.
- **Don't split harm** into pieces that each stay under the line.
- **Include a data-exposure size**: how large an unauthorized exposure of personal information must be to stop the operation. If you set none, every exposure is a stop condition (3.C.2).
- **Define indicators of slow accumulation** (3.A.6): signs that harm, or your advantage over those who could stop, challenge, renew, replace, or exit the power, is building up even when no single event crosses a line.
- **Non-participants** (3.A.11): set stricter limits for people who never chose to deal with the power. Crossing them is a stop condition.
- Thresholds are published in a threshold registry with a record of every change (7.4). They can be **tightened** at any time, with a recorded reason and impact analysis. They can be **loosened** only through re-entry.

### Step 1.5 — Design and test the stop paths (3.A.7, 3.C.1)

- Define the **stop conditions** as factual triggers.
- Provide **several independent paths** to trigger a pause, as close as possible to where effects happen.
- **At least one path must be held by a body you neither fund, appoint, nor can remove.**
- **Anyone positioned to see a stop condition must be able to signal it.** They need no form, no proof, and no permission.
- **Test every path before the power starts**, and rehearse halting, replacing, and dismantling the power with procedures simple enough for ordinary staff.

Write down that **no penalty** of any kind attaches to giving a stop signal (3.C.6).

### Step 1.6 — Plan the ending, the remedy, and the long obligations

- **Ending plan (3.A.9):** how dependencies unwind, where records and functions go, what happens to derived outputs, and when wind-down preparation begins.
- **Remedy (3.A.10), for any power capable of causing serious harm:** a funded, independent way for people to get harm investigated, corrected, and compensated. It must be reachable by more than one route, none of them under your control, and must keep working after the power ends. Back it with independently verified financial assurance; insurance can count if it meets the same conditions.
- **Long obligations (3.A.8):** list every debt, contract, easement, exclusivity, or data arrangement that would outlast the term, and have each **approved individually**.
  - **Every** such arrangement must pass to any successor, and must contain nothing that restricts pausing, stopping, narrowing, or ending any power, or that forces a renewal.
  - Non-financial arrangements must also be cancellable without penalty at each term's end.
  - Long-term financing for durable infrastructure, and pensions, may run longer if they meet these conditions and have revision points (2.D.7).
  - Nothing may renew itself automatically, carry an exit penalty, or lock data in a format that can't be moved.
  - Replacing the power must stay feasible within one term.

### Step 1.7 — Data (2.D.12–2.D.14)

For each kind of personal information:
- the **purpose**;
- the **retention period**, stated and justified now. It cannot outlast the power's term without re-justification, and personal information can never be kept more than 20 years, except a status register while the status lasts (2.D.12) or a record under a hold (2.D.14).
- Biometric and genetic data: collect only if nothing else will do, destroy when the purpose ends, and never keep it longer than 10 years, except a specific sample held while a court case is pending.
- **Data exposure (3.C.2):** if personal information is exposed, notify the people affected promptly and fix the cause. Failing to notify, a repeat within 12 months, or an exposure over the size you set is a stop condition.
- Every deletion gets a **deletion record** (7.6). A deletion that can't be confirmed counts as retention.

Retention clocks never reset. Consent cannot extend them.

Plan for **accountability records** (records of force, confinement, sanctions, complaints, stop signals, discipline, and reviews). They go to independent custody for 10 years and cannot be deleted by you.

### Step 1.8 — Set the term (7.2)

| If the power… | Maximum term |
|---|---|
| is minor (Step 1.2) | 5 years, including the first term |
| is not minor, and is being authorized for the first time | **2 years** |
| is not minor, and uses force or confinement, can cause hard-to-reverse effects, reaches a broad population or many jurisdictions, is new or untested, or has significant uncertainty | **2 years**, every term |
| is not minor and none of the above applies (so, in practice, a renewal) | 5 years. Up to 10 years only if a second independent body confirms, in public, that a longer term carries no greater risk |
| was created for an emergency | 90 days. It may continue only through re-entry, at most twice, each time for a shorter period; after that, only as an ordinary power (1.6) |

Nothing ever exceeds 10 years. No term can be extended partway through. Renewal is decided no more than 6 months before the term ends.

### Step 1.9 — The authorization decision (3.A.2)

The authorizing body must:
- be **independent**: not funded, appointed, removable, staffed, or controlled by you or by anyone who benefits from the power; not reliant on you for data or operations; with no swapped arrangements; and with no members who served you until a published cooling-off period has passed;
- have **real authority to refuse, narrow, or shorten**, not just to advise;
- **hear the affected people** before deciding;
- decide **on a public record**, with its findings on every requirement;
- itself be **stoppable and challengeable**.

You may not pick or switch authorizing bodies to get a friendlier answer.

### Step 1.10 — Assemble the authorization record

The record contains everything from Steps 1.1–1.9. It must be:
- written in plain language a non-specialist can follow;
- publicly accessible, with only narrow, justified exceptions for safety;
- stored where you do not control it **(practice)**.

Use the authorization mode of the registration form. **If anything is missing, the power may not start.**

### Step 1.11 — Existing powers when an authority adopts (7.1)

Adoption covers every power of the authority and of every entity it controls. Each existing power must be authorized within 12 months. An authority with too many powers to do that may instead use a schedule of up to 3 years, approved by an independent authorizing body, which must authorize every force or confinement power within the first 12 months, and its other highest-risk powers next. Until authorized, each power runs under the term limits (7.2), all stop paths, and the Floors. A power not authorized on schedule expires. Moving a power to an entity that has not adopted does not remove it from the requirements. Withdrawal takes effect 12 months after public notice and does not cancel pending pauses, stops, findings, or obligations. **Editions:** adoption is of the edition in effect on the date of adoption; each later authorization, renewal, or re-entry is decided under the edition in effect on that date, and no authority may advance or delay a renewal to avoid a later edition (7.1).

## STAGE 2 — OPERATING WITHIN LIMITS

### Step 2.1 — Know the five answers

Anyone operating a power should be able to answer quickly, and the answers must be easy to find (3.B.9) **(practice)**:

1. What may this power do, and what is excluded?
2. When does it expire?
3. Who can stop it, and how?
4. What are its thresholds, and how close are we?
5. Is it harder to stop today than when it began?

### Step 2.2 — Explain yourself without being asked (2.C.1)

Whenever the power acts on a person, give them a plain explanation of what you are doing, why, and how to question, challenge, or stop it. They should not have to ask. Formal notice that people can't understand does not count.

### Step 2.3 — Check before anything new (3.B.1)

Before any action that is new, larger, faster, more forceful, or less visible than before, ask: is this within the authorization, in all six dimensions? If the answer is "no" or "not sure", **don't act; get an independent determination first.** You may not interpret your own scope (Interpretation; 1.5). Record every interpretation in the public interpretation log (7.3). Any interpretation that widens scope, or reduces the occasions for stopping, waits for independent review.

### Step 2.4 — Watch for growth you didn't decide

Growth happens through small steps. Watch for:
- staff regularly doing things outside the description;
- other agencies referring cases outside your scope;
- your public statements describing a bigger role than your authorization does;
- dependence on one vendor, funder, or data source;
- advantages you are building (exclusive data, contracts, skills) that would make you harder to replace (3.B.7).

Record what you find and disclose it (3.B.6, 3.B.8).

### Step 2.5 — Coordination

Coordination is allowed when each party can leave at will and stays independently stoppable, as with mutual aid between fire departments. Coordination that makes any party harder to stop is growth. Combining powers, data, or jurisdiction across domains needs separate authorization, and any combination that reduces stoppability must be rejected (3.B.3). No central body may override, delay, or condition a local stop (3.B.4).

### Step 2.6 — Equal treatment (2.D.10)

Watch for differences in how the power's burdens fall across race, ethnicity, sex, religion, disability, language, and the other listed characteristics. A material difference requires an independent review, completed within 90 days. A difference the review doesn't find explained by other factors must be corrected within a set window; otherwise the affected operation pauses until it is. Accommodations, translated services, and support for documented disadvantage are not discrimination.

## STAGE 3 — MONITORING

| How often | What | What triggers action |
|---|---|---|
| Continuously | Thresholds (automatic stop when crossed) | Crossing stops the operation at once (3.C.2) |
| Monthly (practice) | Distance from each threshold; trend | Rising for 3 months → escalate to independent review (practice) |
| Monthly (practice) | Stop-signal counts vs activity | Signals falling while activity grows → independent review within 30 days; if suppression is found, the operation pauses (3.B.8) |
| Quarterly (practice) | What the power actually does vs its authorization, in all six dimensions | Growth without authorization is stopped (3.B.1). Remove it, or seek a new authorization |
| Quarterly (practice) | Slow-accumulation indicators (3.A.6) | Persistent growth → correct, or it bars renewal |
| Annually (practice) | Test every stop path; confirm holders are still independent; ask them whether stopping has become harder | If no clear external stop remains, the authorization is invalid and the power is stopped (3.A.7, 5.5). Otherwise restore any degraded path immediately |
| Annually (practice) | Lock-in check: could a successor take over within one term? Is data exportable? | No → fix within the defined window (3.A.8, 3.B.7), not at renewal |

**Never manipulate metrics, delay disclosure, or restrict audit access.** Any of these pauses the operation (3.B.8).

Independent reviewers must be able to evaluate the power at all times (5.1). If outcomes can't be explained by the inputs you declared, you are presumed to have undeclared influence, and the operation pauses until you show your inputs are complete (5.3).

Keep up the public records (7.4–7.6): a plain-language ledger, a surface map, the threshold registry, the failure record, and the stop-signal record. You may never edit the stop-signal record.

**Any violation that has no other named consequence pauses the operation until it is corrected** (Force and Status). A power operating without a valid, current authorization is stopped (5.5). Repeated violations of any Floor are a structural finding requiring narrowing, restructuring, replacement or ending (3.D.5).

## STAGE 4 — PAUSING AND STOPPING

*For operators, for checkers of stop signals, and for anyone who sees a problem.*

### Step 4.1 — The two ways a power halts

**Automatic stop (3.C.2).** When a defined threshold is crossed or a defined stop condition occurs, the operation stops at once, whether or not anyone reports it. It restarts only through re-entry.

**Stop signal → pause → check (3.C.3).** Anyone positioned to see a problem may give a stop signal. They need no prescribed form, no proof, and no permission.

| Signal reports… | What happens |
|---|---|
| A defined threshold or stop condition | The operation **pauses immediately** |
| A credible pathway to serious harm not covered by any defined condition | The checking body confirms **within hours** that the stated facts, if true, describe such a pathway. Then the pause starts |

The independent checking body then decides **one question only: did the trigger fact happen?**
- **No:** the pause ends. A false alarm confers no authority and costs the person who signaled nothing.
- **Yes:** the pause becomes a stop. Restart only through re-entry.
- **The checker misses its deadline:** the matter goes at once to its pre-designated substitute. If the substitute also misses, a pause becomes a stop, and a pathway signal takes effect as a pause.

The check **may not** weigh how useful, costly, or popular the power is, and may not excuse a confirmed trigger.

A signal that only **repeats facts already checked and found unfounded** does not pause again unless it adds something new. It is still recorded and counts toward review.

### Step 4.2 — Stop narrowly, protect the Floors (3.C.5)

- Stop the **operation implicated**, not the whole institution, and not just one employee when the operation itself is the cause.
- **If pausing, stopping, expiry, or ending would push people beneath any Floor** — for example, by cutting off water, medical care, or subsistence: stop the harmful part and keep only the minimum essential function. The authorizing body supervises it until it names, within 7 days, a controller with no stake in the function. No new people may be admitted into confinement. The arrangement is reviewed every 90 days, and a successor must be authorized within 12 months. The authority whose power was stopped may never run it.
- **Powers whose purpose is to stop imminent bodily harm and that use no force against people, such as firefighting or emergency medical response:** signals and thresholds that arise mid-incident are acted on when the incident ends, or every 72 hours, whichever comes first. Anything about bodily harm, or harm to another Floor, that the operation itself is causing is acted on at once. A declared emergency or season is not an "incident". This never applies to an operation conducted with, or at the direction of, a power that uses force.

### Step 4.3 — Executing a stop

1. Halt the operation implicated, immediately.
2. Notify everyone who depends on it promptly (practice: within 24 hours).
3. **Preserve the relevant records.** Accountability records, and records relevant to the stop, are placed on hold by the independent custodian (2.D.14). Ordinary deletion duties still apply to everything else.
4. Enter the event in the public stop-signal record: category, count, check result, outcome, **never the identity of the person who signaled** (7.5).
5. Independent review examines the stop (3.C.8). **Review does not restart the power;** only re-entry does.
6. An independent body confirms that the operation has actually ceased, including in contractors' systems and derived outputs (3.C.8).

### Step 4.4 — Physically containing a hazard (3.D.2)

When a hazard is causing, or about to cause, a **larger irreversible harm**, physically containing it — a recall of a dangerous product, a quarantine, a firebreak — is **stopping harm**, even though its effects can't be undone. It must be the least harmful means that works, limited to the hazard, documented, and independently reviewed afterward. **Contracts, financial commitments, and destroying records are never containment.** A change of someone's legal status is containment only if it is temporary and protective: reversible by default, limited in time, confined to the danger, and reviewed by an independent decision-maker within a short fixed period (3.D.2). A permanent change of legal status never is.

### Step 4.5 — If external law compels you to continue (3.C.7)

Record the conflict publicly, notify the body that compels you, and continue only the minimum essential function under Step 4.2.

### Step 4.6 — No penalty, no retaliation (3.C.6)

No penalty of any kind attaches to giving a stop signal, stopping, using oversight, or challenging the power. Retaliation is itself a violation and requires narrowing the power. Knowingly false statements can be dealt with only through ordinary law, never through the stop path or its record.

## STAGE 5 — RENEWAL

Renewal is a **new authorization decision**. It is never automatic and never presumed.

### Step 5.1 — Renewal evidence (3.E.2, 3.E.3)

Assemble:
1. **Original grant vs today**, in all six dimensions. **Renewal cannot approve growth.** Any growth must be removed, or authorized as new.
2. **Harm record:** threshold data for the whole term, every crossing and stop, and the response to each.
3. **Stop paths:** fresh test results showing every path still works and its holder is still independent.
4. **Slow-accumulation indicators** compared with their authorized baseline (3.A.6).
5. **Independent drift review** by testers who are independent of you, covering reinterpretation, accumulation, stop friction, capture, lock-in, and accumulating advantage.
6. **Necessity:** evidence, independently verifiable, that the power is still needed **and** is reducing its dependency, scope, impact, or permanence over time. Reductions in things you never used don't count. Your own service data counts only once an independent audit confirms it.
7. **Data:** retention compliance, deletion records, and accountability-record custody.

### Step 5.2 — Warning signs (7.7)

Any of these requires an expanded independent review before renewal:
- stop signals falling while activity grows;
- approvals getting faster without more evidence;
- fewer external auditors taking part;
- interpretation concentrated in fewer hands;
- more people affected who never chose to be.

### Step 5.3 — The decision (3.A.2)

The independent authorizing body records findings on every requirement. **A renewal without those findings is void, and the power is treated as expired.**

Outcomes:
- **Renew**, with a new term under Step 1.8;
- **Renew narrower**, for a shorter term or a reduced scope;
- **Do not renew.** The power ends (Stage 6).

No body may decide more than two consecutive renewals of the same power. The next renewal goes to a different independent body designated in advance.

**Incomplete evidence means no renewal.**

### Step 5.4 — The people's path (3.E.5)

At any time, people subject to the power can require an early review before a body other than the one that last authorized it. The threshold for triggering this must be published in advance and attainable by ordinary people. You carry the burden of showing why the power should continue. Early review can narrow, shorten, or end the power, but never lengthen it.

## STAGE 6 — ENDING

### Step 6.1 — When a power stops or ends

A power stops or ends when:
- its term expires without completed renewal (3.E.1): it stops, and returns only through re-entry; pending review does not extend it;
- it is not renewed;
- its founding assumptions no longer hold (3.E.4);
- it exists without active function (3.E.4);
- a correction deadline is missed (5.6);
- a safeguard was bypassed (5.7).

A **stopped** power has not ended: it stays stopped until re-entry, or until it expires.

### Step 6.2 — Wind-down (3.E.6)

- Wind-down stays within the power's normal limits and never extends it.
- If ending would push people beneath a Floor, the minimum essential function continues under Stage 4, Step 4.2. Otherwise, **nothing continues the power after it ends.**
- Reverse or invalidate downstream effects and derived outputs (3.E.1, 4.4), and notify every system that relied on them (4.6). The press, archives, and people's own copies are exempt.
- **Keep** accountability records, in independent custody, and records under hold (2.D.14).
- **Records people rely on, or that could clear them:** before destroying one, give the person a copy, or transfer it where they ask. If they can't be reached, it goes to independent custody (2.D.14).
- **Delete** other personal information, and make a deletion record for each deletion (7.6).
- An independent body confirms the power has actually ceased.

### Step 6.3 — After the end

- Responsibility for what the power did survives its ending.
- Remedy stays available (3.A.10).
- Nothing is owed to the ended power: no replacement and no reinstatement by default.
- **Protective designations** do not expire with the powers that enforce them (3.E.7). The enforcement powers end; the protection stays.
- Write a short retirement summary **(practice)**: what the power did, where it struggled, and what future designers should know.

## QUICK REFERENCE

**Five questions every operator should be able to answer:** see Step 2.1.

**Never:**
- invent a value; write UNKNOWN or NONE EXISTS;
- interpret your own scope;
- let a threshold depend on something you control;
- penalize anyone for a stop signal;
- let a term run past its end;
- delete accountability records;
- keep data "in case";
- loosen a threshold without re-entry.

**Red flags. Don't act; get independent review if:**
- you are doing something your authorization doesn't describe;
- someone faced consequences for raising a concern;
- a vendor, funder, or partner has become essential;
- stopping has become harder than it was;
- renewal feels like a formality;
- the power is harder to dismantle than when it began.

**Defaults:**
- Doubt about scope → the narrower reading, pending independent determination.
- Approaching a threshold → escalate before it is crossed.
- A degraded stop path → restore it now, not at renewal.
- Incomplete renewal evidence → no renewal.
- An unknown fact → UNKNOWN. An absent safeguard → NONE EXISTS.
