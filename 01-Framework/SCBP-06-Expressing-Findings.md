# SCBP-06 — Expressing Findings

*v1 · 2026-10-13 · Published for independent review · Not adopted by anyone*

**Status:** Guidance. It may never narrow, waive, or reinterpret the Core Requirements (6.1). Where it and the Core Requirements differ, the Core Requirements govern. Where it and the Operations Manual (SCBP-03) overlap, the manual's procedure is the one to follow.

---

## BPF enforces nothing

The Bounded Power Framework has no power over anyone. It cannot compel, pause, stop, fine, or end any institution or power. Nothing in this document gives BPF, its authors, or anyone who prepares a record such a power.

In this framework, **"enforcement" means how findings are expressed and published**: clearly, with sources, in a fixed vocabulary, and in a way that cannot be quietly changed. Any real consequence comes from others: courts, funders, the people affected, or the institution itself (Core Requirements, Force and Status).

What a finding means depends on whether the institution has adopted the Core Requirements:

| The institution… | A finding is… | Who acts on it |
|---|---|---|
| **has not adopted** them (almost every case) | a public statement: conforming, not conforming, or compliance not demonstrated. It is not an obligation. | No one is required to. Others may use it. |
| **has adopted** them (7.1) | a trigger for the consequences the Core Requirements name | The institution itself, through its own stop paths, authorizing bodies, and independent oversight functions (3.A.2, 3.A.3) |

Filing a registration record, or an authority response, is **not** adoption. Adoption is a formal act under 7.1, announced to readers with an `adoption_notice` response. Nothing has been adopted by anyone so far.

**Normative claim.** For the Floors (Part II), Part I, and the rules on harm and correction (Part III, sections C and D), BPF also states its position that an authority crossing them acts illegitimately, adopted or not. That is a claim, not a power.

## Part A — Expressing a finding

Every finding in a record follows the same pattern.

### A.1 One power, one row, one rule

A finding is about **one power** and **one requirement**. It names the rule it rests on, by its Core Requirements number. It never condemns a whole institution. Consequences fall on the power concerned and on any other power the finding shows to be involved, never automatically on the whole institution (Force and Status, "Target of consequences").

### A.2 Four words only

Use the finding vocabulary (VOCAB `finding`):

| Finding | Use when |
|---|---|
| `conforming` | Sourced evidence shows the requirement is met. |
| `not_conforming` | Affirmative, sourced evidence shows it is not met. **NONE EXISTS** for a required item is such evidence. |
| `not_demonstrated` | The answer is UNKNOWN, or access was refused. This is not proof of a violation (5.2). |
| `not_applicable` | The requirement does not apply to this power, with the reason stated. |

Do not add stronger words. For a non-adopting authority, don't write "illegal", "violation", "must stop", or "is paused". Write what the evidence shows: for example, "No path stops this power as a whole; paths exist only for individual cases" or "No expiry date exists".

### A.3 Show the evidence

- Every observed value carries a source [S#], or reads UNKNOWN or NONE EXISTS. Never invent a value.
- Keep adjudicated or admitted facts apart from allegations (VOCAB `claim_type`).
- A structural finding (one authority across several systems, limb (a) operationalization, a proxy) states its **Causation Ladder tier**. Tier 5 or stronger is needed. Tiers 6–7 support only a recorded concern and a request for evidence.
- Refer to people by role, never by name.

### A.4 What a finding is not

A record is not a verdict and not an accusation (Step 0.6). It records structure: what the power is, what limits it, and what is missing. A `not_demonstrated` finding says the evidence is not available. It does not say anything is wrong.

## Part B — Publishing and answering

### B.1 Who publishes

- BPF publishes only **worked examples** (all fictional) and **its own record**.
- Anyone who prepares a record, by hand or with the AI registration bundle, keeps it. Whether to publish it is **their choice and their responsibility**.
- BPF does not run a register of records about real institutions.

### B.2 No silent replacement

- A record changes only by a **new version from its original preparer**, which quotes word for word everything it changed or removed.
- Superseded versions stay readable. The same rule binds BPF's own documents (6.5).

### B.3 The institution's answer

An institution described in a record may file an **authority response** beside it: a `correction`, `added_information`, a `disagreement`, or an `adoption_notice`. A response never replaces the record and does not change any finding by itself. Readers see both.

### B.4 Witness reports and stop signals

Anyone may file a witness report, including a stop signal (Witness Report form). For a non-adopting institution, the report is a record its author keeps and may publish; it pauses nothing. For an adopting institution, a stop signal starts pause-then-check (3.C.3; Operations Manual, Stage 4). SCBP-08 (Witness Reports and Stop Signals) explains how reports, stop signals, and the people's path (3.E.5) work together.

v1 has no place to file reports. A private way to send reports, and a public site to read them, are being considered. No host has been chosen and nothing is live.

## Part C — Consequences for an adopting authority

This part applies only when an institution has adopted the Core Requirements. It summarizes what the Core Requirements already require; the procedures are in the Operations Manual (Stages 3–6). The institution carries these out itself, through its own stop paths and independent oversight functions. BPF does not.

### C.1 The only consequences

The Core Requirements name five consequences: **pause**, **stop**, **re-entry**, **narrowing**, and **ending** (Force and Status; Definitions). Two further duties support them: **rollback** to the last conforming state (3.D.1), and keeping only the **minimum essential function** where a halt would push people beneath a Floor (3.C.5).

There is no other consequence.

### C.2 From finding to consequence

| What is found | Consequence | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| A defined threshold is crossed, or a stop condition occurs | Automatic **stop**; restart only through re-entry | 3.C.2 |
| A stop signal reports a defined condition | Immediate **pause**; checked on one question (did the trigger fact occur?). Yes: **stop**. No: the pause ends. | 3.C.3 |
| A stop signal reports a credible pathway to serious harm | **Pause** once the checking body confirms, within hours, that the facts, if true, describe one; then checked as above | 3.C.3 |
| Material ambiguity about scope, found by an independent body or left undecided past its time limit | **Pause** until resolved; the narrower reading applies meanwhile | 1.5 |
| Undeclared influence: outcomes not explained by declared inputs | **Pause** until the inputs are shown complete | 5.3 |
| Manipulated metrics, delayed disclosure, or restricted audit access | **Pause** pending independent review | 3.B.8 |
| Stop signals falling while activity grows | Independent review within 30 days; if suppression is found, **pause** | 3.B.8 |
| Unexplained disparity across protected groups not corrected in its window | **Pause** of the affected operation until corrected | 2.D.10 |
| Growth beyond the grant in any of the six dimensions | **Stop** of the growth; remove it or authorize it as a new power | 3.B.1 |
| Practical effects beyond authorized scope | **Narrowing** to authorized scope, or **stop** | 5.4 |
| Operating without a valid, current authorization | **Stop**; restart only through re-entry | 5.5 |
| No clear external stop path | Authorization invalid; **stop** | 3.A.7, 5.5 |
| Term ends without completed renewal | Expiry; **stop**; restart only through re-entry | 3.E.1 |
| Risk of an irreversible effect | Blocked before it happens | 3.D.2 |
| Drift or harm | **Rollback** to the last conforming state; if impossible, isolate the effects | 3.D.1 |
| Power reached a dependency that resists removal | **Narrowing**, division, or transfer within a defined window | 3.B.7 |
| Resources over the cap | Automatic **narrowing** to the cap, or **ending** | 3.B.5 |
| Retaliation against anyone who signals, stops, challenges, or reports | A violation; **narrowing** of the power | 3.C.6 |
| Repeated Floor violations by one power or institution | Structural finding: independent review, then **narrowing**, restructuring, replacement, or **ending** | 3.D.5 |
| A safeguard bypassed, disabled, or ignored | **Ending** of the power, and of any other power used in the bypass | 5.7 |
| A correction not completed in its window | **Ending** of the power | 5.6 |
| Any other violation with no named consequence | **Pause** until corrected | Force and Status |

### C.3 No ladder, no discretion

- **No ladder.** Consequences do not climb a fixed ladder. Each trigger leads to its own consequence. A crossed threshold stops at once (3.C.2). A bypassed safeguard leads straight to ending (5.7). When a power exceeds its limits, the response moves directly to narrowing or ending, not through layers of review (3.D.4).
- **No discretion.** Within an adopting authority, a met trigger is acted on. Usefulness, cost, convenience, or popularity cannot override it (5.5, 3.C.3).
- **Short clocks.** Every correction, review, pause, and ending has a short, defined time window. A missed window leads to ending, not to a longer window (5.6).
- **A substitute when a checker fails.** If a checking or oversight body misses its time limit, its pre-designated substitute acts at once (3.A.3, 3.C.3).

### C.4 Narrow, and protect the Floors

- A pause or stop applies to the **operation implicated**. It does not reach unrelated powers of the same institution. It never falls only on one employee when the operation itself is the cause (3.C.5).
- If a halt would push people beneath a Floor, only the **minimum essential function** continues (3.C.5; Operations Manual, Step 4.2). It is controlled by someone with no stake in it, named within 7 days, and never by the authority whose power was stopped. It is reviewed every 90 days, and a successor must be authorized within 12 months. It may not admit new people into confinement. "Essential" may never be widened to keep the power running.
- Review after a stop examines the stop. **Review never restarts a power; only re-entry does** (3.C.8).

### C.5 Consequences never add power

A consequence may only pause, stop, narrow, roll back, or end. It never creates new authority, never becomes a reason for permanence, and never justifies an exception (3.D.3, 6.3).

### C.6 Public records

The adopting authority keeps the public records required by Part VII:
- the plain-language ledger, surface map, threshold registry, and failure record (7.4);
- the stop-signal record, giving category, count, check result, and outcome, never the identity of anyone who signaled, and never edited by the authority (7.5);
- the deletion record (7.6);
- the interpretation log (7.3).

Useful status words for these records **(practice)**: operating, paused, stopped, narrowed, minimum essential function only, expired, ended.
