# One-Page Map — The Anti-Evasion Rules

*A reading aid, not a rule. The rules govern as written in the [Core Requirements (SCBP-04)](SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md); this page changes nothing in them. It only shows that the rules people find repetitive are really **one principle, applied to seven ways of evading a limit**.*

---

## The one principle

> **The framework follows the function, wherever it goes and whatever it is called.**

An authority can try to slip a limit by changing the *form* of what it does — the name, the shape, the owner, the timing, the variable, the paperwork — while keeping the *function* the same. Each rule below closes one of those moves. They overlap on purpose: each shuts a different door on the same escape.

Rule 4.1 (General Closure and Shared Responsibility) states the principle directly: no requirement can be defeated by a change of name, structure, location, sequence, intermediary, technology, or anything else, if the effect is the same. The moves below are known examples. They are not a complete list.

---

## The seven moves, and the rules that close each

| The evasion move | "We didn't break the limit, we just…" | Rule(s) that close it |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Rename it** | …called it something else, reclassified it, automated it, or called a decision a "score" or "recommendation" | **1.3** (Function Over Form), **4.3** (Indirect Authority) |
| **2. Split it up** | …divided it across departments, contractors, or time periods | **4.2** (Fragmentation and Functional Continuity) |
| **3. Add it up slowly** | …grew one small step at a time, or lined up with others informally | **3.B.1** (Six Dimensions of Growth — growth by small steps), **3.B.3** (Coordination and Aggregation) |
| **4. Route it through someone else** | …had a vendor, partner, or foreign system do it | **4.5** (External Systems), **4.1** (shared responsibility of every system in the chain) |
| **5. Use a stand-in** | …used a proxy variable that does the same thing | **4.7** (Proxies) |
| **6. Push the effect downstream** | …the effect lives in a derived output, or in a system that received our data | **4.4** (Derived Outputs and Laundered Data), **3.D.1** (Reversibility and Rollback — downstream effects), **4.6** (Propagation Duty) |
| **7. Just look compliant** | …followed the words while keeping the power | **4.8** (Interpretation Abuse), **5.4** (Outcomes Govern) |

---

## Why this is the whole of it

Read top to bottom, the table is one sentence: *you cannot escape a limit by changing the name, the shape, the speed, the route, the variable, the place the effect lands, or the wording — because the framework tests what the authority actually does, not how it describes itself.*

Two things sit under all seven:

- **5.2 — Burden of Proof and Evidence Integrity.** An authority that has adopted the Core Requirements must show that it complied; missing or non-reproducible evidence counts as non-compliance. For an authority that has not adopted them, missing evidence is recorded as "compliance not demonstrated" — not, by itself, as proof of a violation.
- **The Causation Ladder** (in the Definitions). Before anyone can *find* that separate systems are really one authority (4.2, 4.5, 5.4), that a variable is really a proxy (4.7), or that one system's outputs are wired into another's decisions (4.3(a)), they must state the strength of their evidence and may not claim more than it supports. A finding of one authority or a prohibited proxy needs tier 5 or stronger. This is the brake that keeps the anti-evasion rules from becoming their own kind of overreach. The ladder governs findings about structure; it never governs stopping.

So the cluster isn't many ideas to remember. It's **one idea (function over form), seven doors, and one brake.**
