# [CITY] Workers' Cooperative — Registration

## PART A — The institution

A1. **What it is, in one paragraph.** A worker-owned business with one production site. About 40 worker-owners [EX] each hold one membership share and one vote; about 12 newer workers [EX] are candidates on a path to membership. Members elect a board, set wages and working conditions at a general assembly, and share any surplus through individual capital accounts. Membership ties three things together: a job, a vote, and a stake in the business's capital. Expulsion removes all three at once.

A2. **Legal basis.** The [STATE] cooperative corporation statute, the cooperative's bylaws, and a membership agreement each worker-owner signs on admission. National and state employment law applies to the cooperative as an employer: wage-and-hour, anti-discrimination, workplace-safety, and the national labor law protecting concerted activity.

A3. **Who controls it.** The general assembly of all worker-owners, one member one vote. A board of 5 [EX] members elected for staggered two-year terms [EX] runs operations between assemblies. A peer review committee of 3 members [EX], elected by the assembly, handles discipline.

A4. **Related entities.** [REGIONAL COOPERATIVE FEDERATION] is a voluntary federation of cooperatives. It supplies a trained outside mediator when both sides agree, but has no power to decide. All figures belong to the cooperative.

A5. **Adoption.** not_adopted. Findings are diagnostic only [S1].

A6. **Powers found.**
- P1 — Expulsion of a worker-owner (loss of job, vote and capital stake together).
- Not recorded: admission of candidates to membership (benefits_eligibility_or_allocation); setting wages and surplus allocations (rules_standards_or_prices); lesser discipline such as warnings and unpaid suspension (employment_or_discipline).

## Power P1 — Expulsion of a worker-owner

**B1. What it can do to people.** The peer review committee can recommend expulsion for serious or repeated breach of the bylaws or work rules. Expulsion takes effect on a two-thirds vote of members present at a general assembly [EX]. The expelled member loses at once:
- their job — members can only be dismissed through expulsion;
- their vote and any office; and
- control of their capital account. The bylaws repay the account in equal yearly instalments over 5 years [EX], without interest [EX], and let the board defer repayment if cash is short. For a member expelled "for cause", the current year's surplus allocation is forfeited [EX], and losses can be charged against the balance before repayment.

An average capital account is about 18,000 currency units [EX], roughly four months' pay [EX].

**B2. Who and where.** The roughly 40 worker-owners [EX]. Their households depend on the wage. The cooperative has expelled 3 members in its 15 years [EX]; about 2 members a year resign [EX].

**B3. Authority basis.** `direct`. The cooperative decides membership and employment itself.

Loss of property (the capital account) and of legal standing as a member is exactly what 2.D.5 covers: a deprivation of property needs a challenge before a body independent of the one taking it.

**B7. Data.** Personnel files, time records, discipline files, hearing minutes and capital account statements. Employment law requires keeping payroll and personnel records for 7 years [EX]. Discipline files and hearing minutes have no stated retention period. Hearing minutes are kept by the board secretary, an officer of the cooperative. As a discipline record about a member, they are a sanction listing under 2.D.12 and 2.D.6, not an accountability record, so 2.D.14 does not send them to an independent custodian or set a 10-year [S1] period; they need a stated retention period and deletion on request unless a hold applies.

**B8. Dependencies and evasion.** The cooperative uses an outside bookkeeper for capital accounts [EX]. No sign of splitting or outsourcing the power.

**B9. Harms seen.** Economic and agency: an expelled member loses wage and capital access at the same moment, and may wait years for repayment. Psychological: expulsion is decided by co-workers in a small workplace. These are structural risks; no case is adjudicated in this example.

**B10. Decision inputs and oversight.** Witness statements from co-workers, attendance and production records, and the committee's written findings. The grounds ("serious or repeated breach", "conduct harmful to the cooperative") are published but broad. An outside accountant audits finances yearly [EX]; nobody outside the cooperative reviews discipline.

## Teaching notes (non-binding)

- One act, three losses. Expulsion takes job, vote and capital together, so it touches property and legal standing under 2.D.5 as well as work.
- Democratic control is not independence. A general assembly vote is the cooperative acting on itself; it fails the independence condition for authorization and stop paths (3.A.2, 3.A.7). Rule 3.A.2 offers a route through a lot panel of affected people with appeal to an independent body. In a worker cooperative, excluding everyone paid by the institution excludes every member, so the panel must come from other affected people. None exists here.
- Outside bodies (courts, the labor board, discrimination agencies) reach only unlawful grounds in single cases. That is the usual pattern for private discipline, and it leaves the whole power with no external stop (3.C.1).
- Holding capital for 5 years [EX] after any departure is the 2.B.4 issue, and it also burdens voluntary exit under 2.C.3, which forbids a penalty for leaving.
- Contrast with a conventional employer: at-will dismissal ends the job but leaves retirement savings in the worker's name and takes no vote. Here one act also holds back capital and removes a governing vote. Exit is better in one way (peers decide, no non-compete) and worse in another (capital is held back) (2.C.3, 2.D.5).
- Hearing minutes and discipline files about a member are a sanction listing, not an accountability record. They follow 2.D.12 and 2.D.6, with a stated retention period and the member able to ask for earlier deletion. They are not held for 10 years [S1] in outside custody (2.D.14). Their retention is still UNKNOWN, so the data row stays compliance not demonstrated.
- Conforming rows matter too: rules are given to every candidate before they join (3.B.9), and there is no outsourcing or scoring (Part IV).
- Compare the gig-delivery platform example, where discipline is algorithmic and the worker holds no capital or vote (2.C.1, 4.7).
- Forfeiting a member's surplus allocation for cause permanently takes property because of a breach of rules published in advance. 2.D.15 allows that only after a finding of responsibility with due process, which here lacks an independent hearing (2.D.5).
