# Test Case: A Name Does Not Change the Power

*Worked example · Core Requirements v1 · Not a rule. It shows how rules 1.3, 1.6, 3.B.1 and 3.E.2 apply.*

> **WORKED EXAMPLE — not about any real operation, country, or body.** Names are placeholders.

## The rules being tested

- **1.3 Function over form.** What a power does decides what it is. Renaming, relabelling or reclassifying an activity does not change the authority being exercised.
- **3.B.1 Six dimensions of growth.** Scope, duration, reach, speed, intensity and opacity are each bounded by the authorization. An increase in any of them is growth, and growth without new authorization is stopped. A new function, population or territory is a new power.
- **1.6 Emergencies do not enlarge power.** A power created because of an emergency expires within 90 days, and may continue only through re-entry, at most twice, each time for a shorter period.
- **3.E.2 Renewal.** Renewal cannot approve growth. Growth needs a new authorization.

## The scenario

`[COUNTRY]` authorizes a power called a **"stabilization mission"** in `[REGION]`. Its authorization says:

- **Function:** protect a defined list of supply routes and escort aid convoys.
- **Force:** only in defence of the convoy and its staff.
- **Territory:** the listed routes.
- **Term:** 90 days, because it was created in response to an emergency (1.6).

Over the following weeks the mission:

1. holds positions away from the routes, and engages armed groups there for days at a time;
2. moves into two districts not on the list;
3. adds heavier weapons and air support;
4. keeps operating past day 90, on the basis that "the mission is still a stabilization mission".

## How the rules apply

| What changed | Dimension (3.B.1) | What the rules say |
|---|---|---|
| Sustained combat away from the routes | Scope and intensity | A new function. It is a new power that needs its own authorization (3.B.1), whatever the mission is called (1.3) |
| Two new districts | Reach | A new territory is a new power (3.B.1) |
| Heavier weapons, air support | Intensity | Growth. Stopped unless newly authorized (3.B.1) |
| Operating past day 90 | Duration | The emergency power has expired (1.6). It may continue only through re-entry, and the continuation must be shorter. A power that reaches its end without completed renewal stops (3.E.1) |

**The finding.** Measured by what it does, the mission is now sustained armed engagement in a wider territory. The label "stabilization" decides nothing (1.3). Each change is growth, and growth without a new authorization is stopped (3.B.1). Renewal cannot fix this: it cannot approve growth (3.E.2). The only route is a new authorization, decided by an independent body, for the power as it actually now operates (3.A.1, 3.A.2).

**What stops, and what does not.** The stop applies to the operations that grew, not to every power of the institution (3.C.5). If stopping would leave people beneath a Floor, for example by halting aid deliveries that people depend on, only the minimum essential function continues. It is supervised as 3.C.5 requires, and it may never be run by the authority whose power was stopped.

**If the mission's own command decides what "stabilization" covers.** That is the authority interpreting its own scope, which no authority may do. The question goes to an independent body and into the public interpretation log (Interpretation; 7.3). While it is open, the narrower reading applies, and a material ambiguity pauses the operation concerned (1.5).

**Adopted or not.** If `[COUNTRY]` has adopted the Core Requirements, these consequences bind it. If it has not, each is a public finding: the record says "not conforming". It compels nothing (Core Requirements, "Force and status").

## What this teaches

- Words in an authorization describe a power; they do not enlarge it. Judge a power by what it does.
- Growth can come in any of six dimensions. Small steps add up, and the total is still growth (3.B.1).
- An emergency clock runs whatever the power is called (1.6).
- The answer to growth is a new authorization or a stop. It is never a quiet reclassification, and never a renewal.
