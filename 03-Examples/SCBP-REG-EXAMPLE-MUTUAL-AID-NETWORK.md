# [NEIGHBORHOOD] Mutual-Aid Network — Registration (authorization record)

## PART A — The institution

A1. **What it is, in one paragraph.** A volunteer-run network in one neighborhood that gives groceries, small cash grants and help with rides and errands to neighbors who ask, with no eligibility screening. About 80 volunteers [EX] and a rotating group of 3 coordinators [EX] run it. Neighbors also swap time through an hour-for-hour exchange board, recorded in a simple ledger. Many people both give and receive help. The network has adopted the Core Requirements and this record sets out how its two powers are authorized before they operate under them.

A2. **Legal basis.** An unincorporated association under [STATE] law, with written founding rules adopted by its all-volunteer meeting. The cash fund is held by [FISCAL SPONSOR], a local nonprofit, under a written fiscal-sponsorship agreement.

A3. **Who controls it.** The all-volunteer meeting, held quarterly [EX], sets practice; coordinators rotate every six months [EX]. Under the founding rules, authorization of the network's powers belongs to bodies the network does not control (see each power and PART E).

A4. **Related entities.** [FISCAL SPONSOR] holds and pays out the cash fund and charges a fee of 5% of funds handled [EX]. [HOSTED SPREADSHEET SERVICE] stores the contact list and ledger. Figures belong to the network.

A5. **Adoption.** adopted. The all-volunteer meeting adopted the Core Requirements and published the adoption; both existing powers were brought under them within the 12 months that 7.1 allows [S1].

A6. **Powers found.**
- P1 — Allocation of groceries and small cash grants (minor power claimed).
- P2 — Contact list and time-exchange ledger (not minor; fully authorized).
- Not held: the network does not exclude anyone from help for longer than the 30 days that 3.A.4 allows a minor power [S1]. A longer exclusion, for example of a volunteer for safety reasons, would be a new power needing its own authorization (3.B.1), and the network has not sought one.

Small requests are filled first-come. Cash requests over 100 currency units [EX] are decided by the 3 coordinators [EX]. Cash grants are capped at 300 currency units per household in any 90 days [EX]. The network may pause a household's cash requests for at most 30 days [EX], for example after duplicate requests, with a written reason. It never asks about income, immigration status, religion, sobriety or views, and help never depends on volunteering.

**B2. Who and where.** About 250 households receive help in a typical quarter [EX], from a neighborhood of about 9,000 residents [EX]. Children in those households are affected without choosing. Annual cash flow is about 45,000 currency units [EX].

## Teaching notes (non-binding)

- A minor power must meet every condition of 3.A.4, and the institution must show it. P1 qualifies mainly because it only gives away what the network itself gathers, and no effect lasts beyond 30 days [S1].
- Design choices keep a power minor. Capping pauses at 30 days [S1] and declining to exclude anyone for longer keeps P1 inside 3.A.4; a longer exclusion would be a new power (3.B.1).
- The data power is not minor. An exposure cannot be undone, so P2 gets the full process: an independent board, a 2-year first term [S1], resource caps and a rehearsed shutdown (3.A.4, 3.A.5, 3.A.7, 7.2).
- Anti-slicing: P1 and P2 serve one function, so their spending is counted together against the minor limit (3.A.4, 4.2).
- Minor status lightens the process but keeps stop paths and stop tests; only rehearsal is waived (3.A.4, 3.A.7). Deleting one's own entry is leaving, so it is not listed as a stop path (Form B5).
- A fiscal sponsor paid a fee by the network is not independent, so its power to freeze the fund is not counted (Definitions: Independent).
- The time-bank ledger folds into P2: a negative balance that never grows or gets collected keeps the ledger clear of 2.B.2.
- Rule 3.A.2 lets a member-governed body such as this network be authorized, for non-minor powers too, by a panel chosen by lot from the people affected, excluding operators and paid staff, with appeal to an independent body. P1's panel already has that shape. P2 went to the independent board; a lot panel would also have met the independence condition, but it would still owe findings on every requirement and the full process for a non-minor power.
- An adopted record can still carry open items. P1's equal-treatment method and P2's vendor deletion are marked compliance not demonstrated, with a named deadline and the consequence (2.D.10, 7.6, 1.5, 5.6).
