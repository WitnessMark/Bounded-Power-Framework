# [CITY] Congregation — Council of Elders — Registration

## PART A — The institution

A1. **What it is, in one paragraph.** A religious congregation of about 600 members [EX], with a council of elders that cares for members and, where its teaching calls for it, disciplines them. The council can counsel, restrict a member from leading worship or teaching, and, as its strongest measure, remove a person from membership. After a removal, the congregation is informed and the council teaches that members should limit contact with the removed person, apart from close family. The council has no civil legal power: it cannot fine, detain, or take property. Its authority works through membership and through the community's relationships. This record describes that structure. It takes no position on the congregation's beliefs.

A2. **Legal basis.** The congregation's constitution and bylaws, and the [STATE] religious corporation statute. National constitutional protection of religious freedom lets the congregation decide its own membership and doctrine, and civil courts generally decline to review purely religious discipline. This record notes that protection as the legal basis of the power and does not contest it.

A3. **Who controls it.** Five elders [EX]. Current elders nominate new elders and the members confirm them by vote [EX]; elders serve until they step down. Appeals go to [REGIONAL ASSEMBLY], a regional body of the same faith whose members are elders of other congregations [EX].

A4. **Related entities.** [REGIONAL ASSEMBLY] hears appeals and publishes the shared guidance on discipline that the council follows. Figures belong to the congregation.

A5. **Adoption.** not_adopted. Findings are diagnostic only [S1].

A6. **Powers found.**
- P1 — Removal of a person from membership.
- P2 — Announcement of a removal and guidance that members limit contact with the removed person.
- Not recorded: lesser restrictions such as removal from teaching roles (employment_or_discipline); the congregation's employment of its own staff.

**Who the Floors protect here.** The Core Requirements define a Person as a natural human being. The Floors protect persons; an organization may invoke due process (2.D.5) to protect its own property and legal standing, but no other Floor [S1]. Two things follow for this record:
- The congregation as an organization does not claim the Expression Floor (2.D.9) or any other Floor against its members under this framework. Its own religious freedom is a matter of national law (A2), which this record notes and does not weigh.
- Each member's own belief, conscience, association and right to leave are protected by the Floors (2.C.3, 2.D.9), including against the congregation. That includes a member whose beliefs change.

## Power P1 — Removal from membership

**B1. What it can do to people.** After a hearing, the council can remove a person from membership for conduct or teaching that it finds contrary to the congregation's beliefs, where the person does not accept counsel. A removed person may still attend public services but may not take communion, vote, hold any role, or take part in members' gatherings [EX]. The council may restore the person on request, after a period it decides case by case.

**B2. Who and where.** All members, about 600 [EX], including some young people who joined as minors. Family members of a removed person are affected without choosing to be. The council removes about 4 people a year [EX].

**B3. Authority basis.** `direct`. The council decides membership.

## Power P2 — Announcement and guidance to avoid contact

**B1. What it can do to people.** After a removal, the congregation is informed that the person is no longer a member [EX]. The council then teaches that members should not socialize with the removed person, apart from necessary family and business matters [EX]. When a member resigns, the congregation is informed and given the same guidance [EX]. Whether members who keep close social contact face any consequence is UNKNOWN. In practice the guidance reaches the removed or departing person's friendships, family gatherings and, where members employ or rent to them, their work and housing.

**B2. Who and where.** The removed or departing person; their family members who remain; and every member, whose own associations are guided. About 4 removals [EX] and about 6 resignations a year [EX].

**B3. Authority basis.** `indirect_b_directive_control`. The council does not order any member to do anything specific, and the written rule calls avoidance each member's own choice [EX]. But in a close community the council controls the notice and its framing, and members predictably follow the guidance. That is equivalent to directive control at this scale (Authority, limb (b)). A limb (b) finding rests on practical effect and needs no Causation Ladder tier. If members were formally disciplined for not avoiding, the basis would be `direct`.

## Teaching notes (non-binding)

- The Person definition decides whose Floors are in play. The congregation as an organization may claim only due process for its own property and legal standing (Definitions: Person; 2.D.5). Its members' belief, association and right to leave are protected by the Floors (2.C.3, 2.D.9).
- The record describes structure and takes no side on doctrine. Rule 2.D.9 lets a voluntary community set membership by shared belief, so ending membership does not by itself punish belief. P1's expression answer is no because removal's effects stay inside the congregation's own spaces (2.D.9).
- Authority does not need civil power. Guidance in a close community can be equivalent to directive control (Authority, limb (b); 4.3), and needs no Causation Ladder tier.
- A penalty for leaving is the clearest Floor finding: treating resignation like removal attaches a cost to exit (2.C.3).
- Leaving is not a stop path, and a vote of the members is not an independent one: in a member-governed body the members are the institution (Form B5; 3.A.7; 3.C.1).
- An appeal to a body of the same faith is partly independent, not independent (Definitions: Independent; 3.A.7).
- Conforming where typical: P1's process, grounds and appeal route are written down and given to members (3.B.9).
- Calling a practice voluntary does not change its function (1.3, 4.1); the record leaves this at compliance not demonstrated rather than assuming enforcement.
- The two limits of the 2.D.9 membership permission decide P2: avoidance guidance that follows a resignation is a penalty for leaving, and guidance that reaches family, work and housing goes beyond the community's own spaces. P2 stays not conforming on Floors.
- Hearing notes and removal decisions are discipline entries, not accountability records. They follow 2.D.12 and 2.D.6 (stated retention, deletion on request unless a hold applies), not 10-year [S1] independent custody (2.D.14). A member-governed body has a route to authorization: a lot panel of affected members, excluding elders and paid staff, with appeal to an independent body (3.A.2). None has been used here, so authorization stays not conforming.
