# [RATINGS NONPROFIT] — Registration

## PART A — The institution

A1. **What it is.** A nonprofit that buys household and children's products at retail, tests them in a contract laboratory, and publishes letter grades from A to E on a free website. When a test finds a serious hazard, it issues a do-not-use advisory. It sells nothing to manufacturers and orders no one to do anything.

A2. **Legal basis.** Nonprofit incorporation under [STATE] law and federal tax exemption. No statute gives it any role. Its publications are ordinary speech, subject to general law, including defamation law.

A3. **Who controls it.** A board of 9 [EX] directors elected by the board itself; an executive director; a methodology committee that includes 2 [EX] outside scientists.

A4. **Related entities.** A contract laboratory runs the tests. [NATIONAL RETAILER], a large national retailer, incorporates the advisories into its supplier rule (P2). The retailer's own delisting decisions are the retailer's power and would be recorded in a separate registration.

A5. **Adoption.** not_adopted.

A6. **Powers found.**
- P1 Published product grades — influence only.
- P2 Do-not-use advisory as wired into a retailer's supplier rule — operationalized (limb (a)).
- Not recorded: donor and reader data (not a power over the people it describes).

## Power P1 — Published product grades

**B1. What it can do to people.** Nothing directly. It publishes a grade. Shoppers, retail buyers, and journalists may use it or ignore it.

**B2. Who and where.** About 1,500 [EX] products graded a year, from about 600 [EX] manufacturers; about 4 million [EX] website visits a year, nationally.

**B3. Authority basis.** `influence_only`. Neither limb of the Authority definition is met, on the evidence available:
- **Limb (a), operationalized.** No one has been shown to use a letter grade as a structured determinant of a decision that binds anyone. The evidence found sits low on the Causation Ladder: sales of products graded D or E fall by about 15% [EX] in the following quarter (tier 7, residual correlation); retail buyers say in trade interviews that they "look at" the grades (anecdotal; no routine, structured reliance is shown, so it sits with the tier 7 evidence). A finding needs tier 5 or stronger (tiers 1–5), so this supports only a recorded concern.
- **Material cost.** A manufacturer can keep selling a product graded E; shoppers can ignore the grade. The grade's reach and reputation do not by themselves make its cost material (Definitions: Material cost).
- **Limb (b), directive control.** The nonprofit controls no defaults, rankings, or access to information; its grades are one source among many.

Recorded for watching only (4.3). Ordinary reporting, criticism, and advice that people can disregard without material cost are not authority (Definitions: Authority).

## Power P2 — Do-not-use advisory as wired into a retailer's supplier rule

**B1. What it can do to people.** [NATIONAL RETAILER]'s supplier rule, written into every supplier contract, requires any product under a do-not-use advisory from [RATINGS NONPROFIT] to be pulled from sale within 14 [EX] days, with the supplier paying return costs. Through that rule, the advisory — not the retailer's own review — decides whether a product can stay on the shelves of a large national seller.

**B2. Who and where.** About 20 [EX] advisories a year. Manufacturers that sell through the retailer are affected without choosing to be; for small manufacturers, the retailer can be most of their sales. Their workers are affected downstream. Shoppers benefit from removal of hazardous products.

**B3. Authority basis.** `indirect_a_operationalized`. Another party relies on the advisory as a structured determinant of a decision that binds identifiable organizations. Evidence: the published supplier rule and the contract clause that incorporates it — tier 2 on the Causation Ladder (contractual dependency); tiers 1–5 support a finding. The influence is operationalized "by that actor's use" (Definitions: Operationalized into compulsion); the nonprofit did not ask for it. Both are in scope: the nonprofit for the effect of its advisory, the retailer for its own decision (Definitions: Authority).

## Teaching notes (non-binding)

- The same organization can hold an influence and a power. Grades that people can ignore are influence only (4.3); an advisory that a retailer writes into its contracts as a determinant is operationalized (Definitions: Authority, limb (a)) [S1].
- The boundary is set by someone else's use. The definition says a report becomes operationalized "by that actor's use" when another party formally makes it a determinant; the nonprofit did not have to ask for it.
- Evidence strength matters. Tiers 1–5 support a finding; tier 1 is the strongest. Sales drops (tier 7) and anecdotal remarks by buyers support only a recorded concern; a contract clause (tier 2) supports a finding (Causation Ladder, 4.3).
- Reach and reputation are not material cost. A widely read grade still leaves the manufacturer free to sell (Definitions: Material cost).
- Organizations claim only due process among the Floors (Definitions: Person; 2.D.5), but the owners and workers of small manufacturers are persons. Their position is assessed under the Economic and Agency Floors, which is why the P2 Floor row is compliance not demonstrated.
- Both actors are in scope: the nonprofit for the effect of its output, the retailer for its own delisting decision, which belongs in the retailer's own record (Definitions: Authority; 4.1).
- Influence-only rows are not_applicable, not "conforming": the framework makes no claim on ordinary speech, and 6.3 bars using it to suppress safety information.
