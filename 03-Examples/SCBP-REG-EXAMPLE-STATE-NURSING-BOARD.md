# [STATE] Board of Nursing — Registration

## PART A — The institution

A1. **What it is.** A state board that decides who may work as a nurse in [STATE], disciplines licensed nurses, suspends licences when another agency certifies an unpaid debt, and publishes a lookup of every licensee's disciplinary history. Whether a person may earn a living in the profession depends on its decisions.

A2. **Legal basis.** The [STATE] Nurse Practice Act and the board's rules made under it. The board is subject to the [STATE] sunset law, under which it ends on a set date unless the legislature continues it.

A3. **Who controls it.** A board of 11 [EX] members appointed by the Governor: 7 [EX] nurses, 2 [EX] public members, and 2 [EX] other health professionals. An executive director, hired by the board, runs staff and investigations.

A4. **Related entities.** A national exam vendor writes and scores the licensing exam; the pass score is set by a council of state boards, not by [STATE]. The [STATE] Office of Administrative Hearings supplies hearing judges in contested cases. A contractor runs the alternative-to-discipline program. Other state agencies certify debts that trigger suspension (P3). Figures below belong to the board unless stated; all are illustrative [EX].

A5. **Adoption.** not_adopted. Every finding below is diagnostic: a public finding, not an obligation (Force and Status).

A6. **Powers found.**
- P1 Licence grant and denial.
- P2 Professional discipline (reprimand, fine, probation, suspension, revocation).
- P3 Licence suspension on certification of unrelated debt.
- P4 Permanent public discipline record.
- Not recorded: continuing-education audits; approval of nursing schools.

Structural entrapment (2.D.6): yes. A nurse is bound to the classification for life, with no periodic justification and, except for reprimands, no way out.

**B7. Data.** Name, licence number, the action, and the order. A discipline entry against a nurse is a sanction listing, not an accountability record (2.D.14). It follows 2.D.12 and 2.D.6: a stated and justified retention period, deletion when its purpose ends, periodic justification, and the nurse's right to ask for earlier deletion unless a hold applies. While an action is in force, showing it on the licence lookup confirms the licence's current status, which the status-register rule of 2.D.12 allows. After the action is completed, permanent publication to the world has no such basis. The board's own case files, which record what it did, remain accountability records, which 2.D.14 places in independent custody for 10 years [S1].

**B8. Dependencies and evasion.** The national databases keep reports until the reporting board voids them. Commercial background-screening firms copy the public lookup. Removal by the board does not reach these copies.

**B9. Harms seen.** Economic and psychological: nurses with decades-old minor actions report being screened out by employers (allegation, public comment at the sunset review).

**B10. Decision inputs and oversight.** Posting is automatic for every public action. No one reviews whether permanent publication still serves its purpose.

## Teaching notes (non-binding)

- One board, four powers. Licensing, discipline, debt suspension, and the public record each have different stop paths and different gaps; each is assessed on its own (3.A.1, Definitions: Power).
- A sunset clause is a real, common partial answer to 3.E.1. It is not a power-specific term, and at 10 [EX] years without second-body confirmation it exceeds the limit in 7.2. It is also a whole-power stop path for the powers that end with the board: the legislature is independent of the board, and the sunset vote must take place on a set date. It has never ended the board, so it is not shown by test to work, and the P1 and P2 findings are compliance not demonstrated. The legislature's general power to amend or repeal particular powers between sunset dates does not count. P3 rests on statutes with no sunset, so only that general power could end it: NONE EXISTS and not conforming. For P4, whether publication ends at sunset or passes to the successor agency is UNKNOWN. Existing safeguards count for what they actually accomplish, even when they fall short of the full requirement (3.A.7, 3.C.1, 3.A.9).
- P3 shows leverage over livelihood: a licence used to collect a debt unrelated to the work (2.B.3) and made harder to pay by the suspension (2.B.2). Support owed to another person is not erased; the finding is about the method.
- P3 is also a chain: the certifying agency, the board, and the statute together make one function (4.1, 4.2). The board cannot excuse itself by saying it only acts on certification.
- Investigation costs that block renewal are escalation (2.B.2), even though the discipline itself has due process (2.D.5).
- Rule 2.D.14: a discipline entry against a nurse is a sanction listing, not an accountability record. It follows 2.D.12 and 2.D.6, and the nurse may ask for earlier deletion unless a hold applies. An action in force may be shown as part of the licence's current status (2.D.12 status registers); a completed one may not stay public for life. The board's records of its own conduct stay accountability records (10 [S1] years, independent custody).
- Fingerprinting for a licence is allowed by 2.D.11 at the point of a chosen licensed activity. Rule 2.D.11 limits it to that point, so continuous-notice enrollment, which keeps and searches the prints for the life of the licence, needs individualized cause. Its lifelong retention also fails the biometric limit of 2.D.13.
- Mixed findings are normal: visibility is conforming for P1, P3, and P4 because the rules and dates are public (3.B.9).
- A licence may carry an inspection condition limited to its regulatory purpose, but 2.D.15 says it never covers a person's own papers, communications or devices. An administrative subpoena for a nurse's personal records needs individualized cause and a judicial officer's approval, and drug testing agreed to under threat of formal discipline is not consent freely given.
