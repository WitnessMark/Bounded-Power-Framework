## PART A — The institution

A1. **What it is.** A special district that fights fires, rescues people, and sends paramedic ambulances to medical emergencies across most of [COUNTY]. It serves about 240,000 [EX] residents from 14 [EX] stations and answers about 32,000 [EX] calls a year, 70% [EX] of them medical. It bills people it carries by ambulance.

A2. **Legal basis.** The [STATE] fire protection district law and fire code; an advanced life support provider licence from the state EMS agency; a property-tax levy approved by voters. [EX]

A3. **Who controls it.** A board of 5 [EX] directors elected by district voters appoints the fire chief. A physician medical director under contract signs the medical protocols, which the [COUNTY] EMS Agency approves. [EX]

A4. **Related entities.** Neighboring districts send units under mutual-aid agreements that any party may leave. The sheriff orders evacuations. [COLLECTION AGENCY] collects unpaid ambulance bills. Figures belong to the district.

## Teaching notes (non-binding)

- Fire suppression (P1) and emergency medical response (P2) are protective operations under 3.C.5: their purpose is to stop imminent bodily harm and they use no force against persons. For an adopting district, a stop signal or crossed threshold during an active incident is recorded and acted on when the incident ends or after 72 hours [S1], whichever comes first, and every 72 hours [S1] after that.
- The deferral has two exceptions. A signal about bodily harm or another Floor harm caused by the operation itself is acted on at once: for example, foam running into a drinking-water well is ecological harm caused by the operation (2.A.1). And it never applies to an operation run with, or at the direction of, a power that uses force against persons, such as a joint evacuation with the sheriff or sedation requested by police [S2].
- A fire season, a wildfire emergency declaration, or a months-long campaign is not an incident [S3]. Each fire is. The 72-hour [S1] clock cannot be stretched by calling a season one event.
- Billing (P3) is not protective. Its purpose is revenue, not stopping bodily harm, so stop signals and thresholds on billing act at once under 3.C.2 and 3.C.3. A protective institution does not make every one of its powers protective (3.A.1).
- Pulling down a burning neighbor's garage to save a block is containment, which counts as stopping harm [S4] (3.D.2). It must still be the least harmful means, documented, and independently reviewed afterward. The district's immunity leaves owners no after-the-fact review, which fails 2.D.5.
- Emergency medical care without consent (2.D.2) fits paramedic work well: the capacity call is made by a clinician who is not seeking compliance, and a capable refusal is honored [S5]. Sedation of a person held by police is judged as force (2.D.1) if its purpose is control (4.1); whether that is so here is UNKNOWN, so the record leaves the Force row open.
- An expiring tax levy is not a maximum term. It ends funding, not the power (3.E.1). The provider licence in P2, which lapses unless an independent agency renews it, is.
- Ending the district's authority is not the same as ending fire protection. Dissolution by petition and vote can stop the district's power while a named successor keeps fire service; that continuity belongs to a safe ending plan (3.A.9, 3.C.5) and does not by itself disqualify dissolution as a whole-power stop. An existing dissolution procedure can count, but only if the body that decides it is independent of the district and it can end the power within a defined time. The general ability to abolish the district by a new law would not count. Here who decides and how long it takes are UNKNOWN, so P1's stop-paths finding is not demonstrated. A licence suspension by an independent state agency (P2) is a whole-power path in law.
- Ending a protective power must not cut people off from fire or ambulance service: the minimum essential function continues under 3.C.5, and mutual aid any party may leave is permitted coordination (3.B.3).
- The order to stay away from a fire scene is the narrow closure 2.D.16 permits: one specific place, for as long as the danger there lasts. A return visit to find the cause after the fire is out is a search under 2.D.15 and needs a judicial officer's approval or the owner's freely given consent; a refund intercept for an ambulance bill is a seizure that needs the same approval.
