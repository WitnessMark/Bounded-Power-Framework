# [COUNTRY] Central Bank — [RATE-SETTING COMMITTEE] — Registration

## PART A — The institution

A1. **What it is, in one paragraph.** The [COUNTRY] Central Bank is the national monetary authority. Its rate-setting committee sets the interest rate at which the bank lends to and pays commercial banks, and through that rate it moves borrowing costs, savings returns, and employment across the whole economy. In a financial crisis the bank can also open temporary lending facilities that put central-bank money directly into chosen markets and firms. Nobody joins or leaves this authority by choice: every person who uses the national currency is affected.

A2. **Legal basis.** The [COUNTRY] Central Bank Act, enacted by the national legislature in [EX] 1966 and amended in [EX] 2009 to make the committee's rate decision final. The act gives the committee the rate decision, requires the finance ministry to state an inflation target each year, and allows emergency facilities "in exceptional circumstances threatening financial stability" with finance-ministry approval [EX].

A3. **Who controls it.** A committee of 7 members [EX]: the Governor, a Deputy Governor, two senior bank officials, two external members appointed by the Finance Minister, and one external member chosen by the legislature's finance committee, the external members for 4-year terms [EX]. Members can be removed only for incapacity or serious misconduct [EX]. The bank funds itself from its own income and pays its profits to the national treasury [EX].

A4. **Related entities.** The Ministry of Finance approves emergency facilities and shares their losses. A special-purpose vehicle owned by the bank ([FACILITY SPV]) holds the assets bought under P2, and an outside asset manager administers it. All figures in this record belong to the bank or the SPV, as marked.

A5. **Adoption.** not_adopted. The bank has not adopted the Core Requirements; every finding below is diagnostic (Force and Status).

A6. **Powers found.**
- P1 — Setting the policy interest rate.
- P2 — Emergency market-stabilization lending facility (the assigned teaching point: the 1.6 emergency clock).
- Not recorded: bank supervision; the standing lending window for banks; currency issue; foreign-exchange operations on the ministry's behalf.

## Power P1 — Setting the policy interest rate

**B1. What it can do to people.** The committee fixes the rate paid on reserves and charged on overnight loans to banks. It compels nothing of households directly, but every variable-rate loan, deposit rate, and mortgage offer in the country is priced from it. The committee also issues forward guidance about future rates, which moves long-term borrowing costs at once.

**B2. Who and where.** The whole national population, about 40 million people [EX], plus foreign borrowers in the currency. Every resident is a non-participant: no one opts in, and there is no exit short of leaving the currency. About 30% of households hold variable-rate mortgages [EX] and feel a change within one billing cycle.

**B3. Authority basis.** `indirect_b_directive_control`. The rate binds only the bank's own counterparties, but at national scale its effect on credit and employment works as a directive. No Causation Ladder tier is needed for a finding about practical effect (Definitions: Causation Ladder).

Outsiders can evaluate the record from the minutes and forecasts; no verbatim record is published [EX].

## Power P2 — Emergency market-stabilization lending facility

**B1. What it can do to people.** When the board declares a financial-stability emergency and the Finance Minister approves, the bank opens a facility that lends to banks and, through the SPV, buys bonds and short-term notes from eligible firms. It decides who is eligible, what collateral it accepts, and at what price. The facility had a cap of 120 billion currency units [EX], and the ministry agreed to share losses up to 15 billion currency units [EX].

**B2. Who and where.** About 150 banks [EX] and 900 eligible bond issuers [EX] could use it. Non-participants: taxpayers who carry the loss-sharing, and smaller firms below the eligibility line — and the owners and workers behind them — who compete with firms that got cheaper funding.

**B3. Authority basis.** `direct`. The bank itself decides who receives credit and on what terms.

**B4. Observed vs required.** The emergency clock in 1.6 governs this power: a power created or activated because of an emergency expires within 90 [S1] days, may continue only through re-entry no more than twice, each continuation shorter than the one before, and after that only as an ordinary authorized power [S1].

Observed timeline [EX]: opened for new lending for 12 months; extended for 6 months; extended again for 6 months; 24 months of new lending in total; loans of up to 3 years outstanding until month 60.

## Teaching notes (non-binding)

- The emergency clock is mechanical. A 12-month first term [EX] fails 1.6 whatever the merits of the facility: the limit is 90 days [S1], and later periods must each be shorter and come through re-entry, not a board letter (1.6, 7.2).
- A standing facility that the bank can open without declaring an emergency is an ordinary power and needs an ordinary authorization and term (1.6, last sentence; 7.2). That is why the standing lending window is a separate power, not recorded here.
- The facility's run-off plan earns a conforming ending-plan finding (3.A.9), while loans running past the power's end are long obligations that must be listed and approved one by one (3.A.8).
- An SPV that buys what the bank may not buy directly is evaluated as the bank (4.1, 4.2, 4.5). Legal separation does not change the finding.
- High visibility is not stoppability. P1 conforms on 3.B.9 because the act states plainly that the power never expires; the failure is recorded under term, thresholds, and remedy (3.E.1, 3.A.5, 3.A.10).
- A stop path that exists in law but has never been tested gives "compliance not demonstrated", not "conforming" (3.A.7, 5.2). A suspension procedure already in the act counts as a whole-power path; a law that would have to be passed does not.
- Floors protect persons. Firms may claim only due process, but the owners and workers of small firms are persons, and their position is assessed (Definitions: Person).
- Indirect authority at national scale needs no Causation Ladder tier when the finding rests on the output's own practical effect (Definitions; 4.3).
- Every finding against a non-adopting central bank is diagnostic: it is a public record, not an order to stop (Force and Status).

**What registration adds when disclosure is already extensive.** This fictional central bank publishes its rate decisions, supporting analysis, and policy rationale. BPF registration may add relatively little to that existing information. Its additional contribution is to ask different questions: who can independently stop the power, when its authorization expires, what harms cannot be reversed, and whether renewal can occur without fresh justification.

**A test of BPF as well as the institution.** The example also raises a legitimate question about the framework: can a power that requires continuous institutional operation, such as monetary policy, be subjected to expiration and reauthorization without creating unacceptable instability? The record identifies the conflict but does not resolve it. A reader may regard that conflict as evidence of a missing safeguard in the institution, a design limitation in BPF, or both.
