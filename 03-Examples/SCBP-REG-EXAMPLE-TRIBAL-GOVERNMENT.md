# [NATION] Government — Registration

## How to read this example

The [NATION] is a sovereign government. The Core Requirements bind an authority only if it chooses to adopt them, through its own processes (7.1). The [NATION] has not adopted them. Every finding in this record is therefore **diagnostic**: a description measured against a public standard, with no power to compel, pause, or end anything (Force and Status). Decisions about the [NATION]'s powers belong to its citizens, its council, and its courts.

The standard is the same for every government. The three powers recorded here — deciding citizenship, excluding people from territory, and allocating public housing — are held by national, state, and city governments too, and a record of any of them would show similar gaps. Where a question here would also arise for a country's citizenship law, the record says so. Nothing in this record treats a tribal government differently from any other government.

## PART A — The institution

A1. **What it is.** The government of a self-governing nation. Among many functions, it decides who is a citizen (an enrolled member), may exclude people from its lands for serious conduct, and runs a housing program for citizens.

A2. **Legal basis.** The [NATION] Constitution, adopted and amended by vote of the nation's citizens, and ordinances of the [NATION] Council. National law recognizes the nation's authority over its own membership and territory. A federal civil-rights law for tribal governments guarantees due process and equal protection, and allows federal court review mainly through habeas corpus.

A3. **Who controls it.** An elected council of 9 [EX] members and an elected chair, under the constitution. The tribal court's judges are appointed by the council for fixed terms of 4 [EX] years.

A4. **Related entities.** The tribal court; the [NATION] Housing Authority, an agency of the nation; the [NATION] Police Department; a federal agency that funds the housing program under the national tribal-housing block-grant law. Figures belong to the nation unless stated.

A5. **Adoption.** not_adopted. All findings are diagnostic.

A6. **Powers found.**
- P1 Enrollment and disenrollment of citizens.
- P2 Exclusion from the nation's lands.
- P3 Housing allocation.
- Not recorded: all other powers of the nation, including its courts, police, taxation, and natural-resource powers.

## Teaching notes (non-binding)

- Sovereignty and adoption: the Core Requirements bind only an authority that adopts them (7.1). For the [NATION], as for any non-adopting government, findings are diagnostic and carry no consequence beyond the public record (Force and Status, 5.5) [S1].
- Same standard for every government. Citizenship, exclusion, and public housing are recorded here exactly as they would be for a country, state, or city; the gaps found (no term, no thresholds, no whole-power stop) are typical of governments generally (3.E.1, 3.A.5, 3.A.7).
- Real protections are shown where they exist: published rules, a court hearing before exclusion, an independent grievance officer, a capped rent with payment plans. P3 conforms on remedy, data limits, and visibility (3.A.10, 2.D.12, 3.B.9).
- Exit and expulsion differ. A citizen may leave (2.C.3); disenrollment is removal by the government and is judged under due process (2.D.5).
- A restraint that protects identified people, imposed by a tribunal, survives exit under 2.C.3, but must be reviewed on a schedule; permanent orders without review raise 2.D.6.
- An open question is recorded as a question, not a finding: whether 2.D.10 reaches the membership criteria of a political community (Interpretation, 7.3).
- A citizenship roll is a status register under 2.D.12. It may be kept while citizenship lasts if used only to confirm that status, as for any country's register. P1's data row still fails on DNA kept past the 10-year limit of 2.D.13 [S1], which the status-register rule does not reach.
- A self-governing nation has a route to authorization: a panel chosen by lot from the people affected, excluding officials and paid staff, with appeal to an independent body (3.A.2). No panel has been used for any of the three powers, so authorization stays not conforming.
- Changing the law is not a stop path. A citizens' referendum on the membership article resembles the people's path (3.E.5), but under the form it is recorded as a change of law, so the whole-power answer is NONE EXISTS.
- Exclusion from a territory is the 2.D.16 case: permitted as a proportionate sanction for an offense defined in advance, imposed with due process. The rule's bar on keeping a person from returning to their own country raises a question for excluded citizens that the record logs for independent interpretation (7.3) rather than deciding.
