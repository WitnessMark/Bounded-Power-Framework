# Bounded Power Framework (BPF) project — Registration

## PART A — The institution

A1. **What it is, in one paragraph.** The project writes and publishes the Bounded Power Framework under the name WitnessMark [S24]: the Core Requirements, an operations manual, forms, a vocabulary, a checker, two AI bundles (one for registrations, one for witness interviews), fictional worked examples, and background material that includes two historical datasets [S4] [S20] [S27]. It is run by a single volunteer maintainer; no one else is paid or works on it [S9]. It has no company or other legal entity and no funding [S9]. It does not fine, detain, license, or decide anything for anyone. For an institution that has not adopted the rules, the framework "does not compel, pause, stop, reverse, or end the authority" [S3]. In v1 the project does not take in records or reports from anyone [S5], and nothing is collecting reports or personal information [S9]. Whether its tools steer how other people describe real institutions closely enough to count as authority has not been determined (P3).

## Power P2 — Amending the Core Requirements

**B1. What it can do to people.** The maintainer can publish a new edition of the Core Requirements, changing what the rules require [S9]. Rule 6.6 governs: a new edition may not take effect "until an independent check confirms that every protection in the prior edition is present at equal or greater strength" [S8]. A protection may be weakened only as a deliberate change, stated separately with reasons, independently reviewed, and adopted through the same process as the edition [S8]. Rule 6.5 requires every superseded version to stay public [S1]. Today no one is bound by any edition [S4], so for adopters an amendment changes a proposal, not anyone's obligations.

The AI bundle is rebuilt automatically from the Core Requirements and the form whenever either changes on the main branch, and the rebuilt bundle is committed without further review [S17]. An amendment therefore changes, at once, what newly downloaded bundles instruct AI assistants to find about real institutions (P3). The 7.1 delay and the 6.6 gate do not apply to that effect.

**B2. Who and where.** Today: readers, and the people and institutions reached through bundle-made records (P3). If adopters existed, every adopting authority, and everyone subject to its powers, would be affected by an edition that bound them. Number of adopters: NONE EXISTS [S4].

**B3. Authority basis.** `influence_only` today, because no one has adopted the rules [S4] and nobody is bound by an amendment.

This changes as soon as anyone adopts. Under 7.1 (Editions), adoption is of the latest edition in effect at adoption, and each authorization, renewal, or re-entry is decided under the latest edition in effect at the time of that decision. An edition counts only once it has "taken effect under 6.6" [S1]. The maintainer's amendments would then be operationalized into compulsion through the adopter's own commitment: a later edition would become a structured determinant of what the adopter must do to the people subject to its powers. That is indirect authority under limb (a) and 4.3 [S1]. At that point P2 would need its own authorization under 3.A.2, by an authorizing body independent of the maintainer, which heard affected people and decided on a public record [S1].

What exists for that case today:
- An authorizing body for P2 independent of the maintainer: NONE EXISTS [S22].
- A designated independent body to carry out the 6.6 check: NONE EXISTS [S22]. A check the maintainer commissions does not qualify under the definition of Independent ("Not funded, appointed, removable, staffed, or controlled by the authority concerned") [S1].
- The rule on editions: an amendment reaches an adopter's power at that power's next authorization, renewal, or re-entry decision, not the moment it is published [S1]. It is still indirect authority then. Adopters can leave under 7.1's withdrawal, which takes 12 months [S1].
