# What This Is and Is Not

*What the Bounded Power Framework is for, how it was built, what it does not attempt, and the limits of any record made with it.*

## What it is

**A way to describe authority by what it does.** Not by its name, its mission or how it prefers to be classified, but by its effect: what it can compel, restrict, allocate or impose, and on whom.

The framework asks one question: **what structural conditions let authority cause harm, and what limits would reduce that harm?** It starts from no ideology, party or form of government. Power exists in many forms, and every form can fail. The concern is not who holds power. It is how power behaves.

**A fixed set of questions, asked power by power.** A record starts with an institution and lists each of its powers separately: fining, detaining, collecting data, scoring, deciding benefits. For each power it asks the same questions: who authorized it, when it expires, what can stop it, what harm it can cause, and whether it pushes anyone beneath a Floor. Because the questions do not change, one record can be compared with the next. If many institutions used the same requirements, people moving between them would not face a new system each time; that is an expected benefit of wide use, not a proven one.

**A set of rules to compare against.** The [Core Requirements](../../01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md): 101 rules, including four **Floors** beneath which no authority may push a person.

**Applicable to any body that holds power over others:** governments and agencies, companies, platforms, professional bodies, unions, schools, health systems, nonprofits, member associations. It is tied to no country, ideology or institution.

**A draft for review.** v1 is published so that others can test it. No institution or government has adopted it. More than thirty fictional [worked examples](../../03-Examples/) show the questions applied.

## How it was built

The framework began with two sources: [BPF Reasoning](BPF-Reasoning.md) and a collection of historical institutional failures. The history showed recurring patterns: authority expanding beyond its purpose, oversight failing to interrupt harm, institutions becoming difficult to challenge or replace, harmful practices continuing after their justification had gone. The reasoning treated those patterns as a structural problem. Its question was not whether the people exercising power were good or bad, but what conditions allow power to grow, persist, cause harm, or become impossible to stop.

Each layer rests on the one before it, so any rule can be traced to its reason:

1. **Historical evidence.** The [supporting datasets](../../05-Reference/04-Supporting-Data/) and [Historical Context](Historical-Context.md) document selected failures. They are research inputs, not proof that every institution behaves the same way.
2. **Founding reasoning.** [BPF Reasoning](BPF-Reasoning.md) argues that the scope, duration, reach, dependencies and capacity for harm of power must be limited.
3. **Core Requirements.** [SCBP-04](../../01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md) turns that reasoning into explicit constraints: powers identified, justified, bounded, independently stoppable, open to challenge, and subject to expiry and review.
4. **Method.** The [Operations Manual](../../01-Framework/SCBP-03-Operations-Manual.md), the [forms and checker](../../02-Forms/) and the [Record Quality Standards](../../01-Framework/SCBP-09-Record-Quality-Standards.md) turn the requirements into questions asked of one institution, one power at a time.
5. **Testing and illustration.** The remaining framework documents cover safeguards, testing, reporting and review. The worked examples illustrate the method; being fictional, they establish nothing about real institutions.

The history informs the rules but does not prove each one. The reasoning explains the rules but does not replace testing them. The framework's value depends on whether its requirements can be examined, applied, challenged, and shown to reduce preventable harm.

## What it is not

- **Not a rating, a score or a ranking.** There is no badge and no league table. A record shows structure; it does not grade.
- **Not a certification or an endorsement.** A record says only that a power was described against the framework's questions, never that it is safe, lawful or approved.
- **Not a legal judgment or legal advice.** It is not a court filing, a finding of guilt, or a ruling on liability. Where conduct is contested or alleged, a record says so.
- **Not a replacement for existing rights.** The Floors are a minimum. Where a constitution, a law or a treaty protects more, that protection applies (2.1). See [Your Rights and the Core Requirements](../1-Something-Happened/Bill-of-Rights-Crosswalk.md).
- **Not a political platform.** It takes no side on who should hold power or which policies are right. It asks only how any authority must behave to remain stoppable and correctable. At the Floors it is not neutral, and says so; see [Standing Positions](../../05-Reference/02-Assessments/Standing-Positions.md).
- **Not a government, a regulator or a court.** It grants no authority and removes none, and it creates no legal obligation.
- **Not a registry of real institutions.** v1 holds the worked examples and BPF's own record only.
- **Not a replacement system of government.**

## How to read a record

A record should be *accurate to a defender and a critic alike.* Its value comes from that restraint: the structure carries the weight, not the adjectives.

- **It shows structure, not verdicts.** A record can show that a power has no independent way to be stopped, has no expiry date, or keeps data indefinitely. The conclusion is the reader's.
- **Three answers, never a guess.** Every fact is a sourced value, **UNKNOWN** (the evidence does not establish the answer), or **NONE EXISTS** (evidence establishes that it is absent). An honest gap is itself a finding, and NONE EXISTS is often the most important one.
- **An outside record reports what exists.** It compares what exists with what the Core Requirements require, and never fills in what only the institution can set. Stop paths, thresholds and expiry dates set in advance are what an institution would declare if it adopted the framework; until then, their absence is the finding. See [Why do most records say nothing can stop a power?](FAQ.md#why-do-most-records-say-nothing-can-stop-a-power)
- **Findings have three outcomes:** conforming, not conforming, or compliance not demonstrated, where the evidence is insufficient either way.

## Records of real institutions

Anyone may record a real institution with the forms or the AI bundle, using public sources and first-hand observation, without the institution's cooperation. This project does not publish such records, for three reasons:

- **The rules and forms are under review.** They should be tested before they describe real institutions in this project's name.
- **The project cannot verify every record.** The checker confirms that a record is well formed, not that it is true ([Validator Coverage Matrix](../../02-Forms/Validator-Coverage-Matrix.md)). Publishing records here would lend them weight they had not earned.
- **A record belongs to its author.** Publishing it is the author's choice and responsibility ([Terms](../../TERMS.md)).

The framework is designed for a later stage: a public register of records, with aggregate reports across them, intended to open only after review and under published operating rules ([the public register, planned](../../04-Registry/README.md#the-public-register-planned)). v1 operates no register.

A record changes only by a new version from its author, quoting what changed; nothing is silently replaced. The institution can answer with an **authority response**, published beside the record. The response never replaces the record, and readers see both.

## Limits

- **No record is complete.** Records improve through new versions.
- **Structure is not morality.** That a power is hard to stop is a structural fact, not a moral charge. A well-meaning authority can be poorly bounded; an unpopular one can be well bounded.
- **One lens, applied consistently.** The framework does not claim to be the only way to judge an institution. Its strength is asking the same questions every time, so that institutions can be compared fairly.

## What it claims, and what it leaves to others

The claim is smaller than it may appear: asking the same structural questions of every authority, and keeping the answers in a durable, correctable record, makes facts about power visible that were not visible before.

- **An outside record** can make an institution's powers, limits, harms and missing safeguards visible. It does not compel the institution to change.
- **Adoption** means an institution accepts the Core Requirements as obligations on its powers. Adoption does not guarantee compliance, but it creates requirements beyond visibility.
- **For an institution that has not adopted them,** which today is every institution, each rule is a public finding, not an obligation. The consequences of a record, whether challenge, correction, public pressure or no response, depend on others. The framework claims no particular outcome.
- **Silence is an outcome.** An answer from the institution is on the record; so is the absence of one.
- **The claim fails** if records are inaccurate to a fair reader, if the questions are applied unevenly, if records can be quietly changed, or if they do not last. Those are the standards the framework holds itself to.

The project applies the same questions to itself. [BPF's own record](../../04-Registry/SCBP-REG-0001-BPF.md) finds that most of what the project does is influence only, and that it cannot yet show whether its AI bundle shapes how institutions are described widely enough to count as authority. It is a self-assessment, not a self-certification.

## Guiding principle

**Prevent harm where possible. Repair harm where necessary.**
