# FAQ

## Is this a political system?

It takes no side on who should hold power, or on most questions of policy. It sets limits on how any authority behaves, whoever holds it. At the Floors it does take clear positions, such as no execution, and states them openly: see [Standing Positions](../../05-Reference/02-Assessments/Standing-Positions.md), section 3.

## Who is it for?

Anyone who designs, studies or lives under institutional power: people affected by an institution, journalists, researchers, reviewers, and institutions themselves.

## What does SCBP stand for?

**Structural Constitution of Bounded Power.** The nine framework documents are numbered SCBP-01 to SCBP-09, and SCBP-04, the Core Requirements, is its binding text. Record identifiers carry the same prefix: `SCBP-REG` for a registration record, `SCBP-WR` for a witness report, `SCBP-RSP` for an authority response.

## Does every institution have to shut down and justify itself?

No. The requirement to justify continuation applies to each power separately, such as a power to suspend students or to shut off water, not to the institution as a whole. A power is authorized for a set term and, before the term ends, an independent body reviews it. A power that still meets the requirements is renewed and continues without interruption. A power that does not is refused renewal and stops, while the institution's other work carries on; where stopping a power would push people beneath a Floor, its minimum essential function continues (3.C.5). [SCBP-02](../../01-Framework/SCBP-02-Foundational-Theory.md#what-the-inversion-does-not-mean) follows a worked case through renewal.

## Has anyone adopted it?

No. v1 is a draft published for independent review. For an institution that has not adopted the Core Requirements, each rule describes a public finding, not an obligation.

## Does it need governments to adopt it?

No. It can be read, tested, criticized and applied in part or in full. Anyone may compare an institution with the Core Requirements without the institution's cooperation.

## Why do most records say nothing can stop a power?

Because the framework is built for two different moments, and v1 lives in the first.

- **Today: observed records.** Anyone may record an existing institution from the outside. The record reports what exists: which stop paths, thresholds, expiry dates and remedies are there, and which are missing. For most institutions today, many are missing. A record that finds NONE EXISTS for a way to stop a power as a whole is not a weak record. That is the finding.
- **Later: authorization records.** An institution that adopts the Core Requirements would set all of this out itself, *before* a power operates: its limits, the thresholds that stop it, the independent bodies that can stop it and how each stop path is tested, when it expires, and how it would end safely. That is Part E of the [registration form](../../02-Forms/01-Registration.md) and Stage 1 of the [Operations Manual](../../01-Framework/SCBP-03-Operations-Manual.md). No institution has done this, and it may take a long time.

Both kinds of record use the same form on purpose. Today's records measure the distance between how a power is built and how the Core Requirements say it should be built. An authorization record would close that distance in writing, where anyone can check it.

The same holds for witness reports. Anyone can make one today, and it is a dated record its author keeps. Two report types, a **stop signal** and a **review petition**, make something happen only with an institution that has adopted the Core Requirements. With any other institution, they are records too.

## Why are there no records of real institutions?

v1 contains **worked examples** of fictional institutions and BPF's own record, and nothing else, for three reasons:

- **The examples teach better.** They show how the framework applies to a *kind* of institution, without the arguments that surround one particular case.
- **Records of real institutions need care** that a draft framework cannot yet promise: checking sources, handling disputes, publishing responses.
- **A record belongs to its author.** Anyone who records a real institution with the forms or the AI bundle keeps that record, and publishing it is that person's choice and responsibility.

The framework is designed to hold records as a public register in a later version, and to publish aggregate reports across them; the forms already carry what such a register needs. It is intended to open only after review, under published operating rules. See [the public register (planned)](../../04-Registry/README.md#the-public-register-planned).

## Can an institution change a record about it?

No. Only the record's author can change it, and only by issuing a **new version** that quotes, word for word, everything it changed and why. Earlier versions stay available.

An institution that considers a record wrong files an **authority response**: a correction, added information, a disagreement, a notice of change, or a notice of formal adoption. The response is published beside the record and never replaces it, and readers see both. This is the *no-silent-replacement* rule. The worked examples include three authority responses and one record revised to a second version.

## Does this replace rights under existing law?

No. The Floors are a **minimum** (2.1). Where any constitution, law or treaty gives more protection against an authority, that protection applies as well, and no right under other law permits anyone to push a person beneath a Floor.

The framework is not legal advice. [Your Rights and the Core Requirements](../1-Something-Happened/Bill-of-Rights-Crosswalk.md) compares it with the US Bill of Rights; [What You Can Do](../1-Something-Happened/What-You-Can-Do.md) sets out practical options.

## Why is it written this way?

The Core Requirements separate binding rules from plain-language explanations, the "Human Understanding" passages, which never narrow or override the rules. The separation keeps interpretation from quietly enlarging or shrinking what the rules require.

## What makes it different?

It assumes failure and designs for containment. Most systems assume correct behavior and repair mistakes afterward. This framework asks, before a power starts: how is it stopped, when does it expire, and how is a mistake undone?

## Can the framework be used without reading all of it?

Yes. The [AI Registration Bundle](../2-Examine-an-Institution/AI-Registration-Bundle.md) is a single file holding everything an AI assistant needs to draft a record. It is attached to an AI conversation, the institution is named, and a draft comes back. The bundle is written for the AI, not for people. The draft is read carefully and its sources checked, since an AI can be wrong. The [Inquiry Guide](../2-Examine-an-Institution/Inquiry-Guide.md) explains each step.

## Does the bundle file a record anywhere?

No. The draft stays with the person who made it. Nothing is sent to this project, and v1 has no place to submit records. Whoever publishes a record is its publisher ([Terms](../../TERMS.md)).

## Is it complete?

No system is final. This is a structured attempt to state clear limits on power, published so that others can find what is wrong with it.

## How can the framework be commented on?

Questions, criticism and proposed revisions are welcome in [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/WitnessMark/Bounded-Power-Framework/discussions). Discussions is not a place for witness reports, identifying information, private documents or sensitive evidence; the project accepts no witness reports.

The project's public contact is the GitHub account WitnessMark and the address WitnessMarkGitHub@proton.me, for comments on the framework itself, not for reports about real institutions or people.
