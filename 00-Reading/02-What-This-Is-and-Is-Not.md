# What This Is and Is Not

*Read this before you dive in. It sets out what the Bounded Power Framework is for, what it deliberately does not try to do, and the honest limits of any record made with it.*

---

## What it is

**A way to describe authority by what it actually does.** Not by its name, its mission statement, or how it prefers to be classified — by its effect. What can it compel, restrict, allocate, or impose, and on whom?

**A fixed set of questions, asked power by power.** A record starts with an institution and lists each of its powers separately: fining, detaining, collecting data, scoring, deciding benefits. For each power it asks the same questions: who authorized it, when it expires, what can stop it, what harm it can cause, and whether it pushes anyone beneath a Floor. Because the questions are the same for everyone, one record can be compared with the next.

**A set of rules to compare against.** The [Core Requirements](../01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md): 101 rules, including four Floors no authority may push a person beneath.

**Worked examples.** More than 30 fictional institutions, recorded with the forms, show how the questions apply in practice. They are in [03-Examples](../03-Examples/).

**A draft for review.** v1 is published so others can test it. No institution or government has adopted it.

---

## What it is not

- **Not a rating, score, or ranking.** There is no "good authority" badge and no league table. A record shows structure; it doesn't grade.
- **Not a certification or endorsement.** A record says only that a power was described against the framework's questions — never that it is safe, lawful, or approved.
- **Not a legal judgment or legal advice.** It is not a court filing, a finding of guilt, or a ruling on liability. Where conduct is contested or only alleged, an honest record says so.
- **Not a replacement for your rights.** The Floors are a minimum. Where your own constitution, law, or treaty protects you more, that protection still applies (rule 2.1). See the [rights guide](08-Bill-of-Rights-Crosswalk.md).
- **Not a political platform.** It takes no side on who should hold power or which policies are right. It asks only how any authority must behave to stay stoppable and correctable. At the Floors it is not neutral, and says so openly.
- **Not a registry of real institutions.** v1 holds worked examples and BPF's own record only.
- **Not a finished blueprint.** It does not propose a replacement system of government.

---

## How to read a record

A record should be **accurate to a defender and a critic alike.** The framework's value comes from that restraint: the structure does the work, not the adjectives.

- **It shows structure, not verdicts.** A record can show that a power has no independent way to be stopped, or has no expiry date, or keeps data forever. What you conclude is up to you.
- **Three answers, never a guess.** Every fact is a sourced value, **UNKNOWN** (looked, couldn't find it), or **NONE EXISTS** (found that it is absent). An honest gap is itself a finding. NONE EXISTS is often the most important one.
- **An outside record reports what exists; it does not fill in what the institution never set.** A record made from the outside reports what exists and compares it with what the Core Requirements require. It never fills in what only the institution can do.
- **Findings have three outcomes.** A power is conforming, not conforming, or compliance not demonstrated (not enough evidence either way).

## Records of real institutions

Anyone can use the forms or the AI bundle to make a record of a real institution, using public sources and their own observation, without the institution's cooperation.

- **That record belongs to the person who made it.** It is not filed with this project. Publishing it is their choice and their responsibility.
- **It changes only by a new version** from the person who made it, quoting what changed. Nothing is silently replaced.
- **The institution can answer** with an authority response, published beside the record. The response never replaces the record; readers see both.

See [TERMS](../TERMS.md).

## Limits worth stating plainly

- **Completeness is impossible, and that's expected.** No record captures everything. Records improve through new versions.
- **Structure is not morality.** That a power is hard to stop is a structural fact, not a moral charge. A kind authority can be poorly bounded; a disliked one can be well bounded.
- **One lens, applied consistently.** The framework doesn't claim to be the only way to judge an institution. Its strength is asking the same questions every time, so many institutions can be compared fairly.

---

## What it claims — and what it leaves to others

The framework's claim is smaller than it may look: asking the same structural questions of *every* authority, and keeping the answers in a durable, correctable record, makes facts about power visible that were not visible before.

- **What it claims.** Only visibility: a consistent, durable, correctable record of each power's scope, harms, stop paths, and reversibility.
- **What it leaves to others.** It does not claim that visibility changes behavior or stops anything. Whatever a record leads to — challenge, correction, pressure, or nothing — is other people's work. Visibility makes those things possible; it does not promise them.
- **Silence is an outcome too.** If an institution answers a record, that is on the record. If it does not answer, that is also a fact anyone can weigh.
- **Where the claim can fail.** It fails if records are inaccurate to a fair reader, if the questions are applied unevenly, if records can be quietly changed, or if they do not last. Those are the standards the framework holds itself to.

The rules in the Core Requirements say how authority *should* behave. For an institution that has not adopted them — which today is every institution — each rule is a public finding, not an obligation.
