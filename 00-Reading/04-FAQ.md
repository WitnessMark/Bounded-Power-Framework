# FAQ

## Is this a political system?

It takes no side on who should hold power or on most policy. It sets limits on how any authority behaves, whoever holds it. At the Floors it does take clear positions (for example, no execution), and it says so: see [Standing Positions](../05-Reference/04-Assessments/Standing-Positions.md), section 3.

---

## Who is this for?

Anyone who designs, studies, or lives under institutional power: people affected by an institution, journalists, researchers, reviewers, and institutions themselves.

---

## Has anyone adopted it?

No. v1 is a draft published for independent review. For an institution that has not adopted the Core Requirements, each rule describes a public finding, not an obligation.

---

## Does it need governments to adopt it?

No. It can be read, tested, criticized, and applied in part or in full. Anyone can compare an institution with the Core Requirements without the institution's cooperation.

---

## Why are there no real records?

v1 contains only **worked examples** of fictional institutions, plus BPF's own record. There are no records of real institutions, for three reasons:

- **The examples teach better.** They show how the framework applies to a *kind* of institution, without arguments about one particular case.
- **Records of real institutions need care** that a draft framework can't yet promise: checking sources, handling disputes, and publishing responses.
- **Your record is yours.** If you make a record of a real institution with the forms or the AI bundle, you keep it. Publishing it is your choice and your responsibility.

---

## Can an institution change a record about it?

No. Only the person who made a record can change it, and only by issuing a **new version** that quotes, word for word, everything it changed and why. Earlier versions stay available.

An institution that thinks a record is wrong files an **authority response**: a correction, added information, a disagreement, a notice of change, or a notice of formal adoption. The response is published beside the record and never replaces it. Readers see both. This is the **no-silent-replacement** rule.

The worked examples include three authority responses and one record updated to a second version.

---

## Does this replace my rights under law?

No. Rule 2.1 of the Core Requirements says the Floors are a **minimum**. Where any constitution, law, or treaty gives you more protection against an authority, that protection also applies. And no right under other law lets anyone push you beneath a Floor.

The framework is also not legal advice. For a US comparison, see [Your rights and the Core Requirements](08-Bill-of-Rights-Crosswalk.md). For practical options, see [What You Can Do](07-What-You-Can-Do.md).

---

## Why is it written this way?

The Core Requirements separate:

- binding rules; and
- plain-language explanations ("Human Understanding" passages), which never narrow or override the rules.

This keeps interpretation from quietly enlarging or shrinking what the rules require.

---

## What makes this different?

It assumes failure and designs for containment. Most systems assume correct behavior and try to fix mistakes after the fact. This framework asks, before a power starts: how is it stopped, when does it expire, and how is a mistake undone?

## Can I use the framework without reading everything?

Yes. The [AI Registration Bundle](AI-Registration-Bundle.md) is a single file with everything an AI assistant needs to draft a record. Attach it to an AI chat, name the institution, and you get a draft back. You don't need to read the bundle; it is written for the AI.

Read the draft carefully, and check its sources yourself. An AI can be wrong.

---

## Does running the bundle file my record anywhere?

No. The draft stays with you. Nothing is sent to this project, and v1 has no place to submit records. If you publish it, you are its publisher. See [TERMS](../TERMS.md).

---

## Is this complete?

No system is final. This is a structured attempt to state clear limits on power, published so others can find what is wrong with it.

---

## How can I comment on the framework?

Questions, criticism, and suggestions about BPF are welcome in [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/WitnessMark/Bounded-Power-Framework/discussions).

Do not post personal witness reports, identifying information, private documents, or sensitive evidence. The project does not accept witness-report submissions.

The maintainer's public contact is the GitHub account WitnessMark and the project email address WitnessMarkGitHub@proton.me. Both are for comments on the framework itself, not for reports about real institutions or people.
