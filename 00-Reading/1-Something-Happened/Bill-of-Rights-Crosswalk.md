# Your Rights and the Core Requirements

*Reading guide · Non-binding · A comparison of the US Bill of Rights with the [Core Requirements](../../01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md) v1. Rule numbers refer to the Core Requirements.*

## In brief

- **The framework takes no right away.** The Floors are a minimum (2.1). Where a constitution, a law or a treaty protects more, the stronger protection applies.
- **Most familiar US rights appear in the framework,** often in a stronger form.
- **A few are left out on purpose,** because they belong to one country's legal system or are matters of open disagreement. Rule 2.1 keeps them wherever the law already grants them.
- **One difference matters most.** The Bill of Rights limits *government*. The Floors limit *any* authority: a government, a company, a platform, a homeowners' association, a religious body, a data broker.

The comparison is selective. It covers the familiar points of the US Constitution, not every protection in the framework; the complete set is in the [Core Requirements](../../01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md).

## Amendment by amendment

| US amendment | What it protects | Where it is in the framework | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | Religion, speech, press, assembly, petition | 2.D.9 (expression, belief, assembly, press, petition); 2.D.8 (information needed to report harm) | Binds private authorities as well as government. No approval before publication. |
| 2nd | Keeping and bearing arms | Not addressed | Left out on purpose. Kept wherever the law grants it (2.1). |
| 3rd | No soldiers quartered in homes | Partly: 2.D.15 (the home), 2.B.3 | Rarely at issue today. |
| 4th | No unreasonable search or seizure; warrants | 2.D.15 (searches and seizures); 2.D.11 (identification and tracking) | Adds that refusing consent is never cause, and that property is never kept permanently without a finding of responsibility. |
| 5th | Grand jury; no double jeopardy; no self-incrimination; due process; just compensation for taken property | 2.D.5 (due process, silence, no second trial after a final verdict); 2.D.15 (property) | Grand jury and just compensation are not stated; 2.1 keeps them. |
| 6th | Speedy public trial; jury; notice of the charge; confronting witnesses; a lawyer | 2.D.5 (a public and prompt hearing, a lawyer, free if needed); 2.D.3(b) (a specific charge, a judge within 48 hours) | A jury is not required; 2.1 keeps it. |
| 7th | Jury in civil cases | Not addressed | Kept wherever the law grants it (2.1). |
| 8th | No excessive bail or fines; no cruel and unusual punishment | 2.D.1 (no torture, no cruel or degrading treatment, no execution); 2.D.3(b) (pretrial custody only on individual findings); 2.B.2 (fines do not grow because a person cannot pay) | Goes further: execution is barred as a punishment. |
| 9th | Rights not listed are still kept | 2.1 (the Floors are a minimum) | The same principle. |
| 10th | Powers not delegated to the federal government stay with the states or the people | Not a rights rule | The framework's design points the same way: narrow grants (3.A.1), and nothing enlarges limits (1.4). |
| 13th | No slavery; no forced labor except as punishment for a crime | 2.D.3 (confinement never used for coercive labor); 2.D.1 | Goes further: no exception for punishment. |
| 14th | Due process and equal protection by the states | 2.D.5; 2.D.10 (equal treatment) | Applies to every authority, not only states. |
| 15th, 19th, 24th, 26th | Voting rights | Not addressed | The framework limits power; it does not organize elections. Kept wherever the law grants them (2.1). |

## Rights the framework adds

These have no direct counterpart in the Bill of Rights:

- **Data:** limits on keeping personal data, with a 20-year ceiling (2.D.12) and 10 years for biometrics (2.D.13).
- **Exit:** the right to leave a program, a service or a membership without penalty (2.C.3).
- **Necessities:** food, housing and income are never used as leverage (2.B.1, 2.B.3).
- **Plain explanation:** people are told plainly what is being done to them and how to challenge it (2.C.1).
- **Future generations:** protections that reach them (2.D.7), and an ecological floor (2.A.1).
- **Movement:** freedom of movement, including leaving and returning to one's own country (2.D.16). US courts recognize a right to travel, but the Bill of Rights does not state it.

## What this guide is not

It is not legal advice. It compares ideas and does not predict how any court would rule. The binding text is the [Core Requirements](../../01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md). Practical options are in [What You Can Do](What-You-Can-Do.md).
