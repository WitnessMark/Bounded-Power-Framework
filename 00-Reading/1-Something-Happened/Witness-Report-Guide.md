# Witness Report Guide

A **witness report** is a dated, signed statement of something an institution did, failed to do, or threatened to do, with the witness's own words kept beside it. It is first a record for its author: it keeps what they remember, and the details that identify what happened and who was involved, before they fade. It is written in a set shape so that it can be read, compared with other reports, and checked. It needs no proof, no special wording, no lawyer, and no one's permission (2.C.2). It does not need a registration record of the institution.

**If anyone is in immediate danger, emergency services come first.** A report is a record, not a rescue.

There are three ways to make one: a web form, an AI interviewer, or by hand. All three produce the same document. The rest of this guide explains each part, for any of them.

## With the web form

The [witness report web form](https://witnessmark.org/witness-report/) asks the questions one step at a time. It works entirely inside the browser: its security settings block every connection, so nothing typed into it is sent anywhere, and it works offline once open. It asks when and where, which organization, then what happened in the witness's own words, then only the questions that fit the kind of situation. From the answers it puts together a statement, using fixed sentence patterns so that nothing is added; the statement can be edited before it is confirmed. It calculates the verification code and file fingerprints itself. At the end it saves the full report, and, if wanted, a **copy for sharing** in which the details of private people are replaced. **Save or send** opens the phone's own menu, to save a copy to Files or email it to oneself; a copy can also be copied, downloaded or printed.

Opened from inside another app, such as Instagram, the page often cannot save. The app's menu (often ⋯ or ⋮) has an option to open the page in the phone's own browser.

## With an AI interviewer

The interview takes about twenty minutes. The AI asks what happened and lets the witness tell it in their own way. It keeps those words exactly, asks only about what is missing, and then writes a statement from everything the witness said. The witness reads it, corrects it and confirms it.

1. **Download the interviewer file:** [AI-Witness-Interview-Bundle.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WitnessMark/Bounded-Power-Framework/main/00-Reading/1-Something-Happened/AI-Witness-Interview-Bundle.md)
   - **iPhone or iPad:** press and hold the link, then tap **Download Linked File**. It is saved in the **Files** app, under **Downloads**.
   - **Android:** press and hold the link, then tap **Download link**.
   - **Computer:** right-click the link, then choose **Save link as**.
2. **Open an AI app** (such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini) and start a new chat.
3. **Attach the file** with the **+** or paperclip button.
4. **Type "Interview me."** Any question can be skipped, and the interview can stop at any point.
   **To talk instead of type,** switch the chat to the app's voice mode after typing "Interview me." and tell the story out loud. Switch back to the written chat at the end; the report is there. Some voice modes cannot read the attached file or make a new one, which is why the file is attached and the report saved in the written chat.
5. **Save the report at the end.** The AI offers it as a file. If the app cannot make files, it gives the text and explains how to keep it.

**Photos, video and recordings stay out of the chat.** The AI company may keep them, and they can identify people. The report lists what exists and where it is kept; the files themselves stay with their owner.

The file can be [read here](AI-Witness-Interview-Bundle.md) before it is used.

## By hand

Copy the template from the [Witness Report form](../../02-Forms/02-Witness-Report.md) into a new text file and work from top to bottom. The technical fields come last, under "Technical details"; their names stay as written. The line under each field asks its question in plain words.

A finished example, about a fictional water shutoff, shows the result: [SCBP-WR-EXAMPLE-WATER-SHUTOFF](../../03-Examples/Witness-Reports/SCBP-WR-EXAMPLE-WATER-SHUTOFF-2026-08-12.md).

## Four rules

1. **Made in good faith.**
2. **First-hand observation and public sources only.** A report holds what the witness saw, heard or went through, and what public records show. What someone else told them is labelled as such.
3. **What is proven stays apart from what was seen or believed.** A conviction, a settlement or a final order is stated as fact, with its source. Everything else is the witness's observation or an allegation, and is labelled as such.
4. **Nothing leaked or private.** No material the witness had no right to hold.

## Names, and a copy for sharing

A report is a record to help its author remember and to identify what happened, and a record that leaves out names and details loses what makes it useful later. So a report keeps them as the witness gives them: names, badge and ID numbers, unit and car numbers, licence plates, descriptions of people, places, phone numbers, and case and reference numbers. Each is given with how sure the witness is.

**A report that names people can expose its author to retaliation or a legal claim if it is shared or published.** So a second copy, a **copy for sharing**, can be made if the witness wants one. It is never made automatically:

- the witness's own details (About me) are left out;
- the names and details of **private people** are replaced with a description in square brackets, such as "[a bystander]" or "[the man in handcuffs]": the witness, other witnesses, the person it was done to, private addresses, licence plates of private vehicles, private phone numbers, account numbers;
- **people acting in an official role keep their names, badge numbers and unit or car numbers**, because what an officer or official does on duty is public conduct;
- the place is given only as precisely as the witness chooses.

The copy for sharing is read once more, as a stranger would read it, before it is sent: no tool catches everything. Pasting the full report into a new AI chat to strip it gives that company everything in it.

## The parts of a report

The report comes in two halves. The first is for people: the statement, the evidence, the witness's own words, and who the witness is. Below a line comes the part for record-keeping: optional questions, what the witness is asking for, what happened next, and the technical fields.

### The statement

The statement reads like a formal witness statement. It opens with when, where, who the witness is, and what they saw or went through, in one sentence; then it goes through what happened in time order. It is written in the first person and in everyday words, one fact to a sentence. It holds only what the witness said: their words for things, and their uncertainty ("about 1:30", "what looked like a flashlight"). What is not known is left out of the statement; the technical fields record it as UNKNOWN.

When someone else writes the statement from the witness's account (a helper, the AI interviewer, or the web form), the witness reads it, corrects it and confirms it, and the report says who wrote it. It ends with four ticked rules, an optional line "This statement is true to the best of my knowledge and belief.", and the witness's signature and date.

### What every statement covers

Each of these is asked only if the witness's own account did not already cover it:

- **when**: the day, roughly what time, and whether it is still going on;
- **where**: as exactly as the witness can say, and the town, state and country;
- **which organization or office**;
- **how the witness knows**: it happened to them, they saw or heard it, they learned of it first-hand afterwards, someone told them, or they are reporting for someone else;
- **what happened, in order**;
- **who did it**: a name, a badge or ID number, a job title, a unit or car number, what they looked like;
- **who it was done to**: what they looked like, or what the witness knows about them;
- **who else saw or heard it**, and whether the witness knows how to reach them;
- **what happened after**: how it ended, and whether anyone was hurt, taken away, or left without something;
- **what it did to people**, and roughly how many;
- **evidence**: video, photos, papers, messages, and where each is kept;
- **whether it was reported anywhere**, and any case or reference number;
- **how sure** the witness is about each name, number and description;
- **anything that could have shaped the memory**: whether the witness talked with other witnesses, or saw video or news about it, before writing.

### What else, depending on the kind of situation

- **A scene** (something the witness saw happen): where the witness was and how far away; whether they could see clearly and hear what was said; the light, and anything in the way; how long they watched and when they left; how many people; vehicles; words said, as close to exact as they can; and what the witness did.
- **A process** (something done to the witness through decisions, letters or calls): each contact in order, with its date, how it happened, with whom, and what was said or decided; reference numbers; the papers and messages they have; the decision and any reason given; whether they were told how to challenge it, and by when; deadlines. For a call: the number, the date and time, and any reference number. For a website: the page's address and the date it was seen; pages change, so a saved copy or the Wayback Machine (web.archive.org) helps.
- **A condition** (something ongoing the witness has noticed): when it was first noticed; each time since, and what was seen, smelled, measured or experienced; any tests and their results; who else is affected; whether anyone was warned; and whether it is getting better or worse.

A situation can be more than one kind: an arrest followed by a complaint that went nowhere is a scene and a process.

### Evidence

Evidence is listed, not attached: "video, on my phone", "letter dated…", "case number…", each with where it is kept. Each key claim that comes from somewhere other than the witness's own eyes and ears is given a source and a type: an adjudicated or admitted fact, a settlement without admission, the institution's own published statement (what it says it does, not proof that it did it), the witness's own observation, or an allegation.

**Photos, video and recordings stay out of the report.** Files carry hidden data such as time and place. Originals are kept safe, with backups, and given only to someone who needs them, such as a lawyer or an oversight body.

An optional **file fingerprint** (a SHA-256 hash) can be recorded in place of a file. A matching fingerprint later shows that the file has not changed. It does not show when the file existed. If the institution holds the same file, such as a letter it sent, it can match the fingerprint and learn who wrote the report; an anonymous report leaves it out.

### In the witness's own words

The witness's account, exactly as they told it or wrote it: not corrected, not edited. It is the check on the statement. A reader can compare the two and see that the statement adds nothing.

### About the witness

- **Identified** (name and contact) carries the most weight, and can expose the author to retaliation.
- **Pseudonymous** (a stable made-up name) allows follow-up and lets reports be linked over time.
- **Anonymous** gives the most protection, carries less weight, and allows no follow-up.

An optional **verification phrase** lets a pseudonymous or anonymous author step forward later. The phrase is at least five random words, with nothing personal in it. A SHA-256 generator turns it into a code, and only the code goes in the report, in `verification_hash`. The phrase is typed exactly, with no space or Enter at the end, and the tool is noted, so the code matches later. If the phrase is revealed, anyone can check it against the code. This links a person to the report; it does not prove who first wrote it.

### Record-keeping

Below the line:

- **Four optional questions,** answered only where they fit: what feels wrong about it; what the institution should have done; whether this happens to others (where a [registration record](../2-Examine-an-Institution/Inquiry-Guide.md) of the institution exists, the part it bears on); and whether the harm could get worse or last, including harm to land, water, air or living things. That last one is a warning, not a finding.
- **What the witness is asking for.** The form covers five kinds of report, described in the [Witness Report form](../../02-Forms/02-Witness-Report.md). Two of them, a **stop signal** and a **review petition**, have an effect only on an institution that has adopted the Core Requirements, and none has. For any other institution every kind is a dated record, so `incident` is usually the right choice.
- **What happened next,** filled in later by whoever reviews the report. It stays blank.
- **Technical details,** for computers: the same facts in fixed words, so that reports can one day be grouped and counted by place, time, kind of situation, what was done, and the harm.

### Name and check

The file is named `SCBP-WR-{SHORT-NAME}-{date}.md`, for example `SCBP-WR-DETROITPD-2026-11-11.md`, using the date of the incident, or the date of writing if that is unknown. A copy for sharing adds `-SHARE`.

The checker in `02-Forms/` can test the file: `python3 validate_record.py FILE` (it needs Python 3 and `pip install pyyaml`). Every ERROR is fixed. Dates and figures in the witness's own sections are not flagged, since an observation is its own source. The checker is optional. An AI-made report can instead be checked by asking the AI to run its "Before you hand it over" checks.

## Keeping the report

*The report belongs to its author.* It is a file on the author's device, and no one else stores it. A report made with an AI is saved before the chat is closed. A dated copy, such as an email to oneself, shows when it was written.

This project has no place to send reports. A private way to send them, and a public site to read them, are being considered; nothing is live and no date is set. Reports do not go to the project's repository or its Discussions.

## Sharing it safely

Whether to share a report, with whom, and when, is the author's decision. Before sharing:

1. **Decide which copy.** A lawyer, a legal aid service or an oversight body may need the full report. Anyone else can be sent the copy for sharing.
2. **Read it as a stranger would.** A street, a shift or a rare job title can identify the author; a wider area, or leaving the detail out, prevents that.
3. **Share the text only**, without the original photos.
4. **Use a separate email address** for a pseudonymous report.
5. **Consider who receives it.** A lawyer, a legal aid service or an oversight body (an inspector general, an ombudsman, a licensing board) can act on it. A journalist may publish it; how the author is described is agreed first. The institution itself is sent a report only after retaliation has been considered, unless it has adopted the Core Requirements.
6. **A report published by its author** is that author's public statement. It holds to what was seen and what public records show.

[What You Can Do](What-You-Can-Do.md) sets out the usual next steps. It is information, not legal advice.

## Retaliation

Under the Core Requirements, no penalty may attach to reporting or to challenging an authority, and retaliation is itself a violation that can be reported on this same form (3.C.6). That protection binds only institutions that have adopted the Core Requirements, and none has. Against any other institution, a report brings *visibility, not shelter.* Legal protections, such as whistleblower and anti-retaliation laws, come from the law where the author lives, not from this framework. A pseudonym, anonymity, the copy for sharing and the verification phrase are the means this guide offers; the identity level is chosen with these limits in mind.

How a stop signal works with an institution that has adopted the Core Requirements is set out in [SCBP-08](../../01-Framework/SCBP-08-Witness-Reports-and-Stop-Signals.md).

## Purpose

The purpose is not to accuse. It is to put a specific, dated, honest account on paper, where it can be read, checked and counted. If the institution stays silent, that silence is on the record too.
