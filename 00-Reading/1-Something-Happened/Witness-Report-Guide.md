# Witness Report Guide

A **witness report** is a dated, written account of something an institution did, failed to do, or threatened to do. It is written in a set shape so that it can be read, compared with other reports, and checked. It needs no proof, no special wording, no lawyer, and no one's permission (2.C.2). It does not need a registration record of the institution, and it does not need the author's name.

**If anyone is in immediate danger, emergency services come first.** A report is a record, not a rescue.

There are three ways to make one: a web form, an AI interviewer, or by hand. All three produce the same document. The rest of this guide explains each part, for any of them.

## With the web form

The [witness report web form](https://witnessmark.org/witness-report/) asks the questions one step at a time and assembles the report as it goes. It works entirely inside the browser: its security settings block every connection, so nothing typed into it is sent anywhere, and it works offline once open. It asks first when and where, then who, then what happened, and starts the account with that first line already written. It calculates the verification code and file fingerprints itself. At the end it offers two copies: a **private copy** with everything as written, whose file name ends in `PRIVATE`, and a **copy to share**, which shows the place only as precisely as chosen and lets names in the account be changed to roles. The page cannot find every name typed into the other answers, so the copy to share is read once more before it is sent. **Save or send** opens the phone's own menu, to save a copy to Files or email it to oneself; a copy can also be copied, downloaded or printed.

Opened from inside another app, such as Instagram, the page often cannot save. The app's menu (often ⋯ or ⋮) has an option to open the page in the phone's own browser.

## With an AI interviewer

The interview takes about twenty minutes. The AI asks what happened and lets the witness tell it in their own way. It takes from the story everything the report needs, reads that back, and asks only about what is missing. The witness's own words are kept as given.

1. **Download the interviewer file:** [AI-Witness-Interview-Bundle.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WitnessMark/Bounded-Power-Framework/main/00-Reading/1-Something-Happened/AI-Witness-Interview-Bundle.md)
   - **iPhone or iPad:** press and hold the link, then tap **Download Linked File**. It is saved in the **Files** app, under **Downloads**.
   - **Android:** press and hold the link, then tap **Download link**.
   - **Computer:** right-click the link, then choose **Save link as**.
2. **Open an AI app** (such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini) and start a new chat.
3. **Attach the file** with the **+** or paperclip button.
4. **Type "Interview me."** Any question can be skipped, and the interview can stop at any point.
   **To talk instead of type,** switch the chat to the app's voice mode after typing "Interview me." and tell the story out loud. Switch back to the written chat at the end; the report is there. Some voice modes cannot read the attached file or make a new one, which is why the file is attached and the report saved in the written chat.
5. **Save the report at the end.** The AI offers it as a file. If the app cannot make files, it gives the text and explains how to keep it.

**Photos, video and recordings stay out of the chat.** The AI company may keep them, and they can identify people. The report lists what exists and where it is kept; the files themselves stay with their owner.

The file can be [read here](AI-Witness-Interview-Bundle.md) before it is used.

## By hand

Copy the template from the [Witness Report form](../../02-Forms/02-Witness-Report.md) into a new text file and work from top to bottom. The technical fields come last, under "Technical details"; their names stay as written. The line under each field asks its question in plain words.

A finished example, about a fictional water shutoff, shows the result: [SCBP-WR-EXAMPLE-WATER-SHUTOFF](../../03-Examples/Witness-Reports/SCBP-WR-EXAMPLE-WATER-SHUTOFF-2026-08-12.md).

## Three rules

1. **Public sources and first-hand observation only.** A report holds what the witness saw or heard, and what public records show. Private, leaked or guessed material is left out.
2. **Roles, not names, in any copy that is shared.** "The officer", "the caseworker", "the manager". The report concerns the institution. A person's name turns a record into a personal dispute, and can expose the author to legal risk. An office that only one person holds, such as the Sheriff or the Mayor, identifies that person, so conduct is attributed to the office or the institution: "the Sheriff's Office", "the city". The author's own copy can keep names, so nothing is forgotten (see below).
3. **What is proven stays apart from what was seen or believed.** A conviction, a settlement or a final order is stated as fact, with its source. Everything else is the witness's observation or an allegation, and is labelled as such.

## Two copies: one to keep, one to share

A report is first a record for its author, and a record that leaves out names and places tends to lose what makes it useful later. So a report can be kept in two copies:

- **The full copy** keeps names, the exact place, unit numbers, phone numbers and web addresses as the witness gave them. Each person is named by role first, with the name after it in square brackets. It starts with the line "Full copy. Private.", and its file name ends in `-PRIVATE`. It is for the author, and for anyone the author chooses to trust with it, such as a lawyer.
- **The copy to share** is the same report with names and other details that point to a private person replaced by roles, and the place given only as precisely as is safe. In the witness's own words, each replacement is shown in square brackets, such as "[the manager]".

The AI interviewer makes the full copy and then offers the copy to share, listing each thing it will replace. The web form saves both. Either way, the copy to share is read once more before it is sent: no tool catches everything. Pasting the full copy into a new AI chat to strip it gives that company everything in it.

## The parts of a report

### Where

The place comes first, because it is usually easier to recall than the order of events, and because reports about the same place can be grouped. The full copy keeps it as exactly as the witness gives it. The copy to share gives it only as precisely as is safe: an exact address that could identify the witness, such as a home or a small workplace, is better given as the town or region, and the location can also be `withheld`. Map coordinates, if any, come from a map app and are never estimated.

### What happened

In plain words, with people named only by role:

- **the account**, in the witness's own words;
- **when**: dates and times, in order, as far as they are known;
- **how**: how it was done or said, such as in person, by letter, by phone, by email, online or by a notice on the door. Whether there is a written record of something tends to depend on this. For a call: the number, the date and time, and any reference number given. For a website: the page's address and the date it was seen; pages change, so a saved copy or the Wayback Machine (web.archive.org) helps;
- **the impact**: what it did to people;
- **who is affected**: roughly how many, and whether any of them never chose to deal with this institution;
- **who else saw it**: anyone else who saw or heard what happened, by role, such as "a neighbour in the same building". A name the witness knows can go in the full copy; it comes out of the copy to share. Their contact details are not asked for;
- **earlier warnings and attempts to fix it**: whether anyone raised the problem before or after, what followed, and where any letter, complaint, inspection or report can be found.

Four further questions are optional and are answered only where they fit:

- **a warning of wider harm**: whether the harm could spread, accumulate, last, or become irreversible, including harm to land, water, air or living things, and what was observed that suggests it. This is a warning, not a finding, and needs no measurement;
- **a duty to protect**: something the institution was supposed to do to protect people and did not do, did late, or did only for some;
- **what seems wrong**: the concern in the witness's own words, with no rule or law needed;
- **what this shows about the power**: whether the incident says something about how the power works for everyone, such as a protection or exemption that did not work, a way to challenge the decision that was missing or unexplained, or a practice written down nowhere. Where a [registration record](../2-Examine-an-Institution/Inquiry-Guide.md) of the institution exists, the report can name the part it bears on.

Anything not known is written as **UNKNOWN**. A report with honest gaps is more useful than one with none.

The last section of the form, **Resolution**, is completed later by whoever reviews the report. It stays blank.

### Evidence

Evidence is listed, not attached: "letter dated…", "case number…", "photos on my phone", "a receipt", each with where it is kept. Each key claim is given a type: an adjudicated or admitted fact, a settlement without admission, the institution's own published statement (what it says it does, not proof that it did it), the witness's own observation, or an allegation.

**Photos, video and recordings stay out of the report.** Faces and voices identify people, and files carry hidden data such as time and place. Originals are kept safe, with backups, and given only to someone who needs them, such as a lawyer or an oversight body. Only media that is already public is linked.

An optional **file fingerprint** (a SHA-256 hash) can be recorded in place of a file. A matching fingerprint later shows that the file has not changed. It does not show when the file existed. If the institution holds the same file, such as a letter it sent, it can match the fingerprint and learn who wrote the report; an anonymous report leaves it out.

### Kind of report

The form covers five kinds of report, described in the [Witness Report form](../../02-Forms/02-Witness-Report.md). Two of them, a **stop signal** and a **review petition**, have an effect only on an institution that has adopted the Core Requirements, and none has. For any other institution every kind is a dated record, so `incident` is usually the right choice.

### How identified

- **Identified** (name and contact) carries the most weight, and can expose the author to retaliation.
- **Pseudonymous** (a stable made-up name) allows follow-up and lets reports be linked over time. It suits most people.
- **Anonymous** gives the most protection, carries less weight, and allows no follow-up.

An optional **verification phrase** lets a pseudonymous or anonymous author step forward later. The phrase is at least five random words, with nothing personal in it. A SHA-256 generator turns it into a code, and only the code goes in the report, in `verification_hash`. The phrase is typed exactly, with no space or Enter at the end, and the tool is noted, so the code matches later. If the phrase is revealed, anyone can check it against the code. This links a person to the report; it does not prove who first wrote it.

### Name and check

The file is named `SCBP-WR-{SHORT-NAME}-{date}.md`, for example `SCBP-WR-COUNTYJAIL-2026-10-01.md`, using the date of the incident, or the date of writing if that is unknown.

The checker in `02-Forms/` can test the file: `python3 validate_record.py FILE` (it needs Python 3 and `pip install pyyaml`). Every ERROR is fixed. Dates and figures in the witness's own account are not flagged, since an observation is its own source. The checker is optional. An AI-made report can instead be checked by asking the AI to run its "Before you hand it over" checks.

## Keeping the report

*The report belongs to its author.* It is a file on the author's device, and no one else stores it. A report made with an AI is saved before the chat is closed. A dated copy, such as an email to oneself, shows when it was written.

This project has no place to send reports. A private way to send them, and a public site to read them, are being considered; nothing is live and no date is set. Reports do not go to the project's repository or its Discussions.

## Sharing it safely

Whether to share a report, with whom, and when, is the author's decision. Before sharing:

1. **Send the copy to share, not the full copy,** unless the person receiving it needs the details and the author trusts them with it.
2. **Read it as a stranger would.** A street, a shift or a rare job title can identify the author; a wider area, or leaving the detail out, prevents that.
3. **Check for names**, the author's included.
4. **Share the text only**, without the original photos.
5. **Use a separate email address** for a pseudonymous report.
6. **Consider who receives it.** A lawyer, a legal aid service or an oversight body (an inspector general, an ombudsman, a licensing board) can act on it. A journalist may publish it; how the author is described is agreed first. The institution itself is sent a report only after retaliation has been considered, unless it has adopted the Core Requirements.
7. **A report published by its author** is that author's public statement. It holds to what was seen and what public records show.

[What You Can Do](What-You-Can-Do.md) sets out the usual next steps. It is information, not legal advice.

## Retaliation

Under the Core Requirements, no penalty may attach to reporting or to challenging an authority, and retaliation is itself a violation that can be reported on this same form (3.C.6). That protection binds only institutions that have adopted the Core Requirements, and none has. Against any other institution, a report brings *visibility, not shelter.* Legal protections, such as whistleblower and anti-retaliation laws, come from the law where the author lives, not from this framework. A pseudonym, anonymity and the verification phrase are the means this guide offers; the identity level is chosen with these limits in mind.

How a stop signal works with an institution that has adopted the Core Requirements is set out in [SCBP-08](../../01-Framework/SCBP-08-Witness-Reports-and-Stop-Signals.md).

## Purpose

The purpose is not to accuse. It is to put a specific, dated, honest account on paper, where it can be read and counted. If the institution stays silent, that silence is on the record too.
