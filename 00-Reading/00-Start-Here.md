# Start Here

*The Bounded Power Framework (BPF) keeps power small enough to stop.*

Institutions make decisions that bind people's lives. Left unattended, their powers tend to widen, outlast their purpose, and become difficult to question or reverse. This is treated here as an ordinary tendency, not a moral failure. It is answered with structure, not with vigilance.

There are three ways in. None requires reading the whole framework first.

1. [Something happened to me](#1-something-happened-to-me): putting on record what an institution did.
2. [Examine an institution](#2-examine-an-institution): recording how its powers are built and what can stop them.
3. [Understand or evaluate the framework](#3-understand-or-evaluate-the-framework): the reasoning and the rules.

## The framework in brief

The framework rests on one inversion: *authority does not continue by default.* It must justify its continuation, and what cannot be justified ends. The inversion applies to each power separately, not to institutions. A power that still meets the requirements is renewed and continues without interruption. Only a power that cannot be justified ends, and the institution itself carries on.

BPF has two parts. The **Core Requirements** state, in 101 numbered rules, what bounded, stoppable and correctable authority looks like. The forms and guides apply those rules in two ways: a **registration record** shows how an institution's powers are built, and a **witness report** puts on record what one of those powers did.

BPF v1 is published for independent review. No institution or government has adopted it. The repository holds fictional worked examples and BPF's own record. It holds no records of real institutions.

## 1. Something happened to me

An agency, a company or another institution acted, failed to act, or threatened to act, and a person was affected.

**If anyone is in immediate danger, emergency services come first.** A record is not a rescue.

The first step is a **witness report**: a dated account, in the person's own words, of what happened, what was seen, and what is not known. Written early, while memory is clear, it supports most of what can follow: a complaint, a records request, a lawyer, an oversight body, or a later decision to let the matter rest. It needs no proof, no special format, and no one's permission.

There are three ways to make one:

- **[Witness report web form](https://witnessmark.github.io/Bounded-Power-Framework/witness-report/).** A page that asks the questions one step at a time and assembles the report inside the browser. Nothing on it is sent anywhere.
- **[AI Witness Interview](1-Something-Happened/AI-Witness-Interview-Bundle.md).** An AI assistant asks one question at a time and assembles the report. It takes about twenty minutes.
- **[Witness Report Guide](1-Something-Happened/Witness-Report-Guide.md).** The same report, written by hand, with each part explained.

*The report belongs to the person who makes it.* Nothing is sent to this project or anywhere else. Whether to share it, and with whom, is that person's decision.

[What You Can Do](1-Something-Happened/What-You-Can-Do.md) sets out the usual next steps in the United States: complaints, public-records requests, oversight bodies, courts, and their deadlines. [Your Rights and the Core Requirements](1-Something-Happened/Bill-of-Rights-Crosswalk.md) compares the framework with the Bill of Rights.

A report does not oblige an institution to answer or to stop. That obligation exists only where an institution has adopted the Core Requirements, and none has. Against any other institution, a report provides a record and visibility, not protection.

## 2. Examine an institution

An institution is examined one power at a time: the power to detain, to fine, to deny a benefit, to collect information, to shut off a service. For each power, a **registration record** establishes who authorized it, what it can do and to whom, when it expires, what harm it can cause, what independent body can stop it, and whether it reaches the framework's minimum protections, the **Floors**.

The [worked examples](../03-Examples/) are finished records of 32 fictional institutions, from police departments and water utilities to data brokers and libraries. A record of a familiar kind of institution is the quickest way to see how the form works.

A record is made in three steps:

1. The [Inquiry Guide](2-Examine-an-Institution/Inquiry-Guide.md) explains what a record asks, where the answers are found, and how to check them.
2. The [AI Registration Bundle](2-Examine-an-Institution/AI-Registration-Bundle.md) lets an AI assistant draft a record from public sources. The [registration form](../02-Forms/01-Registration.md) can also be completed by hand.
3. The person who will rely on the record checks its sources.

Every answer in a record is one of three kinds: a value with its source; **UNKNOWN**, where the evidence does not establish the answer; or **NONE EXISTS**, where evidence shows the thing is absent. A missing stop path or expiry date is recorded as missing, never supplied. That absence is often the most important finding.

A record made from outside describes what can be established. It creates no safeguard and imposes no obligation. It belongs to its author, who decides whether to publish it and answers for what is published ([Terms](../TERMS.md)).

## 3. Understand or evaluate the framework

The framework is published to be tested and criticized. A suggested order:

1. [What This Is and Is Not](3-Understand-the-Framework/What-This-Is-and-Is-Not.md): purpose, uses and limits.
2. [BPF Reasoning](3-Understand-the-Framework/BPF-Reasoning.md): the founding text, from which the rules were drawn.
3. [Core Requirements](../01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md): the 101 binding rules.
4. [Operations Manual](../01-Framework/SCBP-03-Operations-Manual.md): how the rules are applied.

[Historical Context](3-Understand-the-Framework/Historical-Context.md) sets out the institutional failures the rules respond to. The [FAQ](3-Understand-the-Framework/FAQ.md) answers common questions, and the [Glossary](../Glossary.md) defines the framework's terms. The remaining framework documents are in [01-Framework](../01-Framework/).

Two uses of the framework are kept apart. **Examination:** anyone may examine any institution against the Core Requirements, without its cooperation; the result is a record and findings, not enforcement. **Adoption** is different: an institution that adopts the Core Requirements accepts them as obligations on its own powers, including independent review, expiry, stopping and correction. Only the Core Requirements bind. Every other document explains or applies them.

Questions, objections and proposed revisions are welcome in [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/WitnessMark/Bounded-Power-Framework/discussions). *Discussions is not a place for witness reports, identifying details or private documents.*

## What BPF is not

BPF is not a government, a regulator, a court, an enforcement body or a certifier. It gives no legal advice and replaces no existing right. Its records make powers, limits and missing safeguards visible. Its rules describe what authority must do once it adopts them. The aim is to keep authority bounded, stoppable and correctable before harm becomes irreversible.
