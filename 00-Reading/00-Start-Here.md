# Start Here

*The Bounded Power Framework (BPF) is a way to keep power small enough to stop.*

Institutions make decisions that affect people's lives. Over time, their powers can expand, become permanent, or grow difficult to question or reverse.

BPF begins with a different principle: **authority does not continue by default. It must justify its continuation. If it cannot, it ends.**

The framework sets out standards for keeping authority bounded, stoppable, and correctable. It also provides tools anyone can use to examine an institution's powers or document something that happened.

**BPF v1 is a draft published for independent review.** No institution or government has adopted it. The repository contains fictional worked examples and BPF's own record, but no records of real institutions.

## 1. Something happened to me

An agency, company, or other institution did something that affected you, or is still affecting you.

**If anyone is in immediate danger, contact emergency services first.**

### Make a witness report

A witness report is a dated account of what happened, what you observed, and what you know or do not know.

You can begin in either of two ways:

- **Write it yourself.** The [Witness Report Filing Guide](09-Citizen-Witness-Report-Filing-Guide.md) explains how to make an accurate record and handle sensitive information safely.
- **Use an AI interviewer.** The [AI Witness Interview Bundle](10-AI-Witness-Interview-Bundle.md) guides an AI assistant to ask one question at a time and help prepare a report.

You do not need proof, special formatting, or permission to make a report.

**The report belongs to you.** BPF v1 does not provide a service for filing or publishing witness reports. Nothing is submitted to the project unless you independently choose to share information elsewhere.

### Find practical next steps

[What You Can Do](07-What-You-Can-Do.md) is a US-focused guide to complaints, public-records requests, oversight bodies, courts, and deadlines.

To understand how the framework relates to existing constitutional protections, see [Your Rights and the Core Requirements](08-Bill-of-Rights-Crosswalk.md).

A report does not, by itself, force an institution to respond or stop. The framework distinguishes between institutions that have adopted its obligations and those that have not.

## 2. Examine an institution

You want to understand what powers an institution exercises, where those powers come from, and what limits them.

BPF examines powers individually. For example, an institution might have the power to detain someone, deny a benefit, collect information, impose a fine, or make a decision affecting another person's opportunities.

For each power, a registration record asks questions such as:

- Who authorized it?
- What can it do, and to whom?
- When does its authorization expire?
- What harms can it cause?
- What independent mechanism can stop it?
- Does it threaten any of the framework's minimum protections, called the **Floors**?

### See how a record works

Begin with the [worked examples](../03-Examples/). They cover fictional institutions including police departments, schools, water utilities, data brokers, and libraries.

Choose an institution familiar to you. Read how its powers are described, how each is compared with the Core Requirements, and the teaching notes explaining the example.

**All examples are fictional.** Their names, locations, and figures are illustrative.

### Make your own registration record

1. Read the [Citizen Inquiry Guide](05-Citizen-Inquiry-Guide.md).
2. Download the [AI Registration Bundle](AI-Registration-Bundle.md). The [AI Registration Guide](06-AI-Registration-Guide.md) explains how to use it.
3. Identify the institution and the powers you want to examine.
4. Gather sources, or use an AI assistant to help prepare a draft.
5. Check the sources and the resulting record yourself.

The framework requires honest treatment of missing information:

- **Sourced value:** a documented answer.
- **UNKNOWN:** the answer could not be established.
- **NONE EXISTS:** evidence establishes that the item is absent.

Do not invent an expiration date, independent reviewer, or stop mechanism simply because the framework requires one.

You can also complete the [registration form](../02-Forms/01-Registration.md) manually. The other forms and the checker are in [02-Forms](../02-Forms/).

**Your record remains yours.** It is not automatically filed with BPF. Publication is your choice and responsibility. See the [Terms](../TERMS.md).

A record made without an institution's cooperation describes and evaluates what can be established. It does not create missing safeguards or impose obligations on an institution that has not adopted the framework.

## 3. Understand or evaluate the framework

You want to know why BPF exists, how its rules work, whether they are justified, or what adopting them would mean.

### Understand the idea

A suggested reading path:

1. [What This Is and Is Not](02-What-This-Is-and-Is-Not.md) — the framework's purpose, uses, and limits.
2. [SCBP-01 Orientation](../01-Framework/SCBP-01-Orientation.md) — why authority must remain stoppable and correctable.
3. [BPF Reasoning](01-BPF-Reasoning.md) — the reasoning behind its approach.
4. [SCBP-04 Core Requirements](../01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md) — the binding rules.
5. [SCBP-03 Operations Manual](../01-Framework/SCBP-03-Operations-Manual.md) — how the framework is applied.

The other framework documents are available in [01-Framework](../01-Framework/).

### Understand the difference between examination and adoption

**Anyone can use BPF to examine an institution**, including one that has never heard of the framework. That examination produces a record and findings; it does not give BPF enforcement authority over the institution.

**Adoption is different.** An institution adopting BPF accepts the applicable Core Requirements as obligations governing its powers, including requirements concerning independent review, renewal, stopping, and correction.

The [Core Requirements](../01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md) are the only binding part of the framework. Explanatory documents, examples, and guides do not override them.

No institution has adopted BPF v1.

### Evaluate or criticize BPF

The framework is published for independent testing and criticism.

Useful starting points include:

- [Historical Context](03-Historical-Context.md)
- [Core Requirements](../01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md)
- [Worked examples and teaching notes](../03-Examples/)

Questions and critical feedback are welcome in [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/WitnessMark/Bounded-Power-Framework/discussions).

**Do not post personal witness reports, identifying information, private documents, or sensitive evidence in Discussions.** The repository does not accept witness-report submissions.

## What BPF can and cannot do

BPF is not a government, regulator, court, enforcement body, or certification authority. It does not provide legal advice or replace existing rights.

Its records can make authority's powers, limits, and missing safeguards visible. Its Core Requirements describe what authority must do if it adopts the framework.

**The purpose is not documentation for its own sake. It is to help keep authority bounded, accountable, stoppable, and correctable before harm becomes irreversible.**
