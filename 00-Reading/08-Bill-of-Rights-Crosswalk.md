# Where Your Rights Are: the US Bill of Rights and the Core Requirements

*Reading guide · 2026-10-21 · Non-binding · For the [Core Requirements](../01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md) v1. Rule numbers below refer to the Core Requirements.*

## The short version

- **The framework never takes a right away.** Rule 2.1 says the Floors are a minimum. If a constitution, law, or treaty protects you more, the stronger protection applies.
- **Most familiar US rights also appear in the framework,** often in stronger form.
- **A few are left out on purpose.** They belong to one country's legal system, or people disagree about them. Rule 2.1 keeps them wherever your own law gives them.
- **One difference matters most.** The US Bill of Rights limits *government*. The Floors limit *any* authority: a government, a company, a platform, a landlord's association, a religious body, a data broker.

**This is a selective comparison, not an exhaustive list of BPF's rights or protections.** It highlights familiar points of comparison with the US Constitution. For the complete requirements, including protections not discussed here, consult the [Core Requirements](../01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md).

## Amendment by amendment

| US amendment | What it protects | Where it is in the framework | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | Religion, speech, press, assembly, petition | 2.D.9 (expression, belief, assembly, press, petition); 2.D.8 (information people need to report harm) | Also binds private authorities, not only government. No approval before publication. |
| 2nd | Keeping and bearing arms | Not addressed | Left out on purpose. Kept wherever your law grants it (2.1). |
| 3rd | No soldiers housed in your home | Partly: 2.D.15 (home), 2.B.3 | Rarely an issue today. |
| 4th | No unreasonable search or seizure; warrants | 2.D.15 (searches and seizures); 2.D.11 (identification and tracking) | Adds that refusing consent is never cause, and that property can't be kept permanently without a finding of responsibility. |
| 5th | Grand jury; no double jeopardy; no self-incrimination; due process; just compensation for taken property | 2.D.5 (due process, silence, no second trial after a final verdict); 2.D.15 (property) | Grand jury and just compensation aren't stated; 2.1 keeps them. |
| 6th | Speedy public trial; jury; know the charge; confront witnesses; a lawyer | 2.D.5 (public and prompt hearing, a lawyer, free if needed); 2.D.3(b) (specific charge, judge within 48 hours) | The jury isn't required; 2.1 keeps it. |
| 7th | Jury in civil cases | Not addressed | Kept wherever your law grants it (2.1). |
| 8th | No excessive bail or fines; no cruel and unusual punishment | 2.D.1 (no torture, no cruel or degrading treatment, no execution); 2.D.3(b) (pretrial custody only on individual findings); 2.B.2 (fines can't grow because you can't pay) | Goes further: bans execution as punishment. |
| 9th | Rights not listed are still kept | 2.1 (the Floors are a minimum) | Same idea. |
| 10th | Powers not given to the federal government stay with states or the people | Not a rights rule | The framework's whole design (narrow grants, 3.A.1; nothing enlarges limits, 1.4) points the same way. |
| 13th | No slavery; no forced labor except as punishment for a crime | 2.D.3 (confinement never used for coercive labor); 2.D.1 | Goes further: no exception for punishment. |
| 14th | Due process and equal protection by states | 2.D.5; 2.D.10 (equal treatment) | Applies to every authority, not only states. |
| 15th, 19th, 24th, 26th | Voting rights | Not addressed | The framework limits power; it doesn't set up elections. Kept wherever your law grants them (2.1). |

## Rights the framework adds

These have no direct match in the US Bill of Rights:

- **Data:** limits on keeping personal data, with a 20-year ceiling (2.D.12), and 10 years for biometrics (2.D.13).
- **Exit:** the right to leave a program, service, or membership without penalty (2.C.3).
- **Necessities:** no using food, housing, or income as leverage (2.B.1, 2.B.3).
- **Plain explanations:** you're told plainly what is being done to you and how to challenge it (2.C.1).
- **Future generations:** protections that reach them (2.D.7), and an ecological floor (2.A.1).
- **Movement:** freedom of movement, including leaving and returning to your own country (2.D.16). US courts recognize a right to travel, but the Bill of Rights doesn't state it.

This guide is not legal advice. It compares ideas and doesn't say how any court would rule. The binding text is the [Core Requirements](../01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md). For practical options, see [What You Can Do](07-What-You-Can-Do.md).
