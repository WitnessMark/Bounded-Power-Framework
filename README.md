# Bounded Power Framework

A framework for describing authority by what it actually does, and for checking it against a short set of structural limits: power must stay small enough to stop, harm must stay interruptible, and mistakes must stay correctable.
<br clear="all">

> **Status: v1, published for independent review.** Nothing here has been adopted by any institution or government. The rules are a proposal, published so that others can test and criticize them.

**Did an agency or company do something to you?** This project can help you write down what happened in a form others can read and compare. It does not file a complaint for you. Start with [Something happened to you](00-Reading/00-Start-Here.md#something-happened-to-you).

---

## Why this project exists

Many of the largest harms people suffer do not come from one malicious person. They come from institutions and systems operating without enough structural limits.

Again and again, authorities have gathered powers beyond their original purpose, kept them after the reason for them was gone, spread into nearby areas, or kept causing harm long after the warning signs were visible.

The project is an attempt to understand those failures. Its central question is:

**What structural conditions allow authority to cause harm, and what limits would reduce that harm?**

The concern is not who holds power. The concern is how power behaves.

---

## What is in v1

- **The Core Requirements** — 101 numbered rules in seven parts, including four **Floors** (minimum protections, such as bodily safety and due process) that no authority may push a person beneath. They are the framework's binding text. See [SCBP-04 Core Requirements](01-Framework/SCBP-04-Core-Requirements.md).
- **Forms** — a registration form that records an institution and each of its powers, a witness report form, and an authority response form, plus a shared vocabulary, a checker, and an AI bundle. See [02-Forms](02-Forms/).
- **Worked examples** — 32 fictional institutions, recorded with the forms. See [03-Examples](03-Examples/).
- **BPF's own record** — the framework recorded against its own rules. See [04-Registry](04-Registry/).

### The worked examples are the main way to see the framework applied

The examples show what a record looks like for a kind of institution: a city police department, a county jail, a water utility, a data broker, a school district, an owners' association, a worker cooperative, a town library, and many more. Each one lists the institution's powers, what can stop each power, how each power compares with the Core Requirements, and short teaching notes.

All examples are **fictional**. Names, places, and figures are illustrative. Some show an institution answering a record with an authority response, and one shows a record updated to a second version.

### v1 contains no records of real institutions

v1 holds only the worked examples and BPF's own record.

### If you use the AI bundle, the record is yours

You can give the AI bundle to an AI assistant, name an institution, and get back a draft record. That record is **yours**. It is not sent anywhere and not filed with this project. If you publish it, publishing is your choice and your responsibility. See [TERMS](TERMS.md).

## What the framework rests on

It rests on two sources:

- [BPF Reasoning](00-Reading/01-BPF-Reasoning.md), the founding text on keeping power small, stopping harm, and fixing mistakes;
- [historical patterns of institutional failure](00-Reading/03-Historical-Context.md).

The recurring patterns are set out as rules in the **Core Requirements**. The forms are a practical way to apply those rules to any institution.

## Who makes this

The framework's own record ([04-Registry](04-Registry/)) describes the project's powers and gaps against its own rules.

## Guiding principle

**Prevent harm where possible. Repair harm where necessary.**

Everything else in the framework serves that aim.

## Comments and review

**GitHub Issues are temporarily closed** while a review process is set up. The review-comment form is ready for when they reopen: reviewers will use it to point out errors, gaps, and hard questions about the framework. Comments are about the framework itself; this repository does not accept reports or records about real institutions or people.

The maintainer's public contact is the GitHub account **WitnessMark** and the project email address **WitnessMarkGitHub@proton.me**. Please don't send personal information about yourself or others, or reports about real people or institutions; this repository doesn't publish them.
